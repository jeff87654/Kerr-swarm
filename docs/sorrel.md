# Sorrel: a gazetteer of one drum

*Working-session gazetteer, Nov 2026. One nation drum, Sorrel on Herb Row in Merrow (ashgrove.md), mapped from the quay to the axle, to show how much a single drum of 200 million holds. Its interactive form is the Sorrel Atlas (`atlas/sorrel/`, on the site at sorrel-atlas/), a cutaway in which every point opens a place; this doc records what the atlas adds to the setting. Builds on raft-habitat.md (the drum, its bands, the Hollow, the sunline), drum-transport.md (the Lattice, line decks, station rings, the trim water), habitat-design.md (the jacket and the moat), raft-transport.md (the quay, the Glide, cyclers), food-system.md and cuisine.md (the food plant, blights), element-cycles.md (the dead come home; the frost), economy.md and people.md (places, the pool, counted drums), daily-life.md (prices and light), eating-out.md (Sorrel's kitchens), open-drums.md (customs Sorrel shares and doesn't), ashgrove.md and wander-year.md. The Rings and Hours, the Round, the Summer, Sorrel's Count and its pool reform, its assembly, and every named place below are proposals. The neighbourhoods the atlas generates behind every other door are sketches, not canon. Exploration page: [The Sorrel Atlas](https://claude.ai/artifact/Ngtk4xzrxyRhpozuWFUHAB).*

## Summary
- **One drum is a country.** Sorrel's 376 decks hold ~19,900 km² of floor, about the area of Wales, in ~69,400 neighbourhoods of ~3,000 people each. Visiting one a day would take 190 years, about a life and a half.
- **An address is deck, Ring and Hour.** "Seventy-seven, Three, Bee" is deck 77, Ring Three, the Hour of the Bee: the Daces' flat off Pear Street in Ashgrove.
- **Nine Rings run along the axis,** each named for the station ring in the Keel beneath it. Ring One is the power end, Ring Nine the heat end. Each station serves one level of glide lanes, so each Ring has its own traffic and temper: produce and the Gate at Three, swaps with Rue at Four, departures at Five, freight at Seven, refuge at Eight.
- **Twelve Hours go round it,** named for animals nobody in Sorrel has seen. Each is a wedge from the Keel to the Hollow, ~16.7 M people, with its own trades, words, dish and club, and a rivalry with the Hour hanging overhead across the Hollow.
- **The Round** is the Hours' yearly race right round deck 201, run twice: spinward (the Heavy Round, every stride a tenth heavier) and antispinward (the Light Round).
- **Gone up, gone down.** "She's gone up" is Sorrel's polite way of saying someone has retired to a High-deck flat, allotted by age, which most people reach at about a hundred (ashgrove.md). "He's gone down" means someone has died: the dead ride down to a Down Hall in the Keel and back into the cycles. Riding up and down by cab is just travel.
- **213 landmarks,** from the quay to the axle: 44 outside the hull and in the Keel, 14 on line decks, 102 on the residential decks, 32 in the Hollow and on its end walls, and 21 at the axis and beyond. They include the food plant that makes the air, the Gate, a lost property office for 6–9 M cabs, the Register of Places, the tide tanks, a heliox organ, the Larder, the lake's Bench Island, the terrace flats and the Windows.
- **History:** the Register runs back ~23,000 years; the Summer (~1,100 years ago), Sorrel's only season; the Count (~640 years ago), after which it became a pool drum; the refuge (~212 years ago); the Eleven Days (74 years ago).

## Addresses
- **Deck, Ring, Hour.** Sorrelers say the three in that order, the deck as a number and the rest as words: "two hundred and one, Five, Stag". The Lattice numbers its ~5,200 lift cores, but nobody says those aloud.
- **Counting starts at one, One, Hare:** a worn bronze plate on Station Ring One's platform wall marks where both the Rings and the Hours begin.
- **Up is in, down is out.** "Up" is toward the axis and lighter weight; "down" is toward the rim. "Spinward" and "antispinward" ("anti") are the two ways round; "front" and "back" the two ways along.

- **Rivalries run across the Hollow.** The Hour opposite yours hangs 3 km overhead, upside down and perfectly sharp. Stag hangs its Round banner upside down so Hare can read it; Owl checks that Badger's lamps are out; Bee says Moth's kite lamps confuse the blossom.

## The Hollow's plan
- **The lake:** Fox to Stag, Rings Four to Six, ~3 km² and 2 m deep, ~6 Mt of the drum's inside water. Its jetty and the elders' bench are on **Bench Island**, a pavilion that comes up through the lake bed in Otter. The elders have the lake 06:00–09:00, through lamp-dawn; it closes at dusk.
- **The orchards:** Heron to Bee, Rings Three to Seven, apples mostly, lit free by the sunline. Sections are chilled in **winter tents** for six weeks in turn, the only winter anyone can walk into, and pollinated by hand with brushes. A Hollow apple costs the same 4.4 kWh as a lamp-grown one; the saving on light goes to the commons.
- **The lawns:** Swift to Moth, Rings Two to Eight, with the kite field. **The grounds:** Wolf to Hare, Rings Three to Seven: the half-g track, twelve spinball pitches, the bandstand.
- **The Dim Ends:** the floor within ~700 m of each end wall, where the sunline's light falls away to ~38% at the wall's foot: woods, fern gardens and the Lamp Walk, Sorrel's evening places.
- **The end walls:** thirty tiers of terraces from the floor toward the axis, named by weight: the Third, the Quarter, the Tenth, the Twentieth. The flats in their risers, the only windows in Sorrel, go to its very oldest. Sorrel marries on the front wall's Third at dusk; the back wall's Third has the Long Reel; the top gardens are where a child can jump the height of a house.

## Customs
- **The Round:** twelve clubs, one club room per Hour on the Round Arcade, the Heavy Round in the morning and the Light after lunch; records are kept three ways, for axis, spin and anti.
- **Going up:** the Up Hall at the foot of the High decks, where each Ring's elders are given their flats; about one in twenty asks for another year. Couples go up together.
- **Gone down:** nine Down Halls in the Keel, one per Ring, each with twelve felt-hung rooms, one per Hour. The dead ride down in a cab with the stretcher level; the Register's clerk reads their share and place back into the pool; a steel bell is struck; the family may keep a pinch of salts in glass (element-cycles.md: a name, a voice, a pinch of ash in glass).
- **Names and notes:** children are named for the dead whose shares they carry (ashgrove.md); the Register gives the dead's family a card, which comes back within the year with the child's name, and the yearly notes travel by hand to the Note Room.
- **The Window:** twelve galleries behind the radiators, one per Hour, each taking its own Hour's eight-year-olds, about 75 to a round and five rounds a day. In Owl's, the adult tells the child what they saw there at eight.
- **Weddings** on the front wall's Third at dusk. The oldest guest cuts the apple crosswise, and the couple each say one thing they remember from the Window.
- **The Length:** Sorrel's Booking-night flight, from the front gate of the Spindle at 22:00 to the back wall, behind the oldest flyer's lamp. (The Long Flight is an open-drum custom; Sorrel keeps its own.)
- **The tide:** when commuters glide out each morning, moat water is pumped up to ~120 tanks at mid-drum to keep the spin trim, from about 07:00, fullest about 10:00, and back out through turbines in the Keel after 21:00 (drum-transport.md). Couples sit on the Tide Stair to hear it go.
- **Rides on purpose:** the Heavy Loop (line deck 113, spinward at 27.5 m/s, 1.2 g) and the Feather (line deck 363, antispinward at 21 m/s, 0.3 g), which elders ride for their knees.
- **Night service:** the last glide train at 01:00 and the first at 05:00, freight between.

## Government
- **The assembly:** 600 members, fifty from each Hour, elected every six years. Members sit by years served and the oldest opens each sitting. It sets the commons, the light share, the Hollow rota's rules and the price of gin.
- **The Small Court:** a magistrate and two neighbours drawn by lot, for disputes too big for a block.
- **Fixed goods by age** (ashgrove.md): High-deck flats and extra Hollow hours. Hollow turns can be swapped or given, never sold, like birth rights.
- **Guests:** Sorrel joins guest-right but keeps no Margin, so guests sleep in inns the drum keeps near the station rings.

## History
- **The Register of Places** runs back ~23,000 years without a gap.
- **The Summer, ~1,100 years ago.** A fault in the back cap's rotary joint cut the drum's heat flow by a third for forty days. Sorrel dimmed its lamps and kitchens to match and the rooms still ran three or four degrees warm: the only season it has ever had. Summer Day is kept with cold food, and a length of the cracked seal hangs in the back axle hall.
- **The Count, ~640 years ago.** Sorrel was a holdings drum; a dairy-yeast blight caught its thin households, and it counted for seventeen years. When the Count came down, the assembly made Sorrel a pool drum with one birth right per share, giftable but not sellable (people.md's reformers' drum). The old Count board hangs in the Register hall, and Dairy Line Four has never been used since.
- **The refuge, ~212 years ago.** Sorrel held back a week of births, ~30,000 shares, to take in people fleeing a counted drum three rows across. Many settled in Ring Eight, and their descendants still bring a cake to the refuge desk on the date.
- **The Eleven Days, 74 years ago** (ashgrove.md). The Eleven Days Room on deck 365 is kept by people who were there as children.

## Landmarks
Every landmark in the atlas, outside in, with where it is (deck, Ring and Hour). The atlas has a fuller entry for each: what it's like, a practical detail, and often someone you might meet.

### Outside the hull
- **The Moat** (Ring Two). Half a metre of Sorrel's own water held outside the jacket: the drum's X-ray shield and its reservoir, which nobody has seen and everyone has drunk.
- **The Quay, Ring Seven** (Ring Seven). A band of track round Sorrel's waist, outside the jacket and the moat, where cradles let glide trains go toward Lovage and Tansy and catch them coming home.
- **The Minutes, Ring Four** (Ring Four). Sixty short dark tunnels through the hydrogen jacket, five to each Hour, where glide trains go out to the quay and come home.
- **The Jacket** (Ring Nine). Fifty metres of liquid hydrogen at 20 K wrapped round the whole drum: Sorrel's shield against cosmic rays, and the fuel it would burn in a Dark.
- **The Guard Vacuum** (Ring Eight). The narrow gap of nothing between Sorrel's hull and its jacket, where cryopanels freeze every atom the hull leaks and the Gleaners bring it home.

## What the atlas generates
- **Every neighbourhood.** The residential decks (16–375, without the line decks) are cut into 58 layers of four to eight decks, each into 18 blocks along the axis and ~45–88 round it: 69,426 blocks of ~2,000–4,000 people (about 3,000 on average), ~210 m on a side. A block's address seeds its card, so the same door always opens on the same place. Ashgrove is decks 73–78; Larchmere, Netherby and the Saltings are its neighbours above and below.
- **A card** draws a name (121 first parts, 62 endings, 216 whole names), an identity line (56), its middle, a bright court, dim wood or other room (76), its hall (56), two trades (120), a sound (67) and a smell (58), a custom (132), a quarrel (84), a neighbour (170 roles and lines, 402 given and 349 family names), a history (83) and, on about one card in seven, something found only there (40), with lines from its Hour and Ring. Pools are split by band, so Low-deck cards have children and High-deck cards have elders.
- **The Keel, line decks, Hollow floor and terraces** have their own pools (37, 26, 40 and 21 units), and the air of the Hollow, the jacket, the moat, the quay, the mid-raft and the layers have fixed cards.
- **None of the generated places is canon.** The names are worn from every Earth language and repeat across the drum, as place names do.

## Open questions
- **Lane levels** are shared by every drum in a row, so Sorrel's Ring Five is the tube level for its whole row; wander-year.md's Marit leaves from Chervil's Ring Two and rides the quay to the tube level.
- **A gentler catch** for the frail (ashgrove.md): still open.
- **Night flying** has wardens with red lamps at the Spindle's 75 m edge, since a flyer who drifts off the axis falls for minutes and lands at ~85 m/s. Should the netting run the whole length?
- **Back-crew dose:** the atlas keeps the crews, the shielded track and the Windows in Sorrel's own X-ray shadow, with water hoods on sleds further out.
- **Demography:** at ~120-year lives the over-85s are ~29% of the drum, but the High decks hold ~15%, so most people go up nearer a hundred.
- **How the Hours began:** a founders' custom, but nobody in the atlas says why animals.

## Method
- **Decks:** r = 3,000 − 4(n − 1) m, g = r ÷ 3,000. Strata split each run of decks between line decks into groups of four to eight; blocks are 3,750 ÷ 18 = 208 m along the axis and ~210 m round (N = round(2πr ÷ 210) per layer). People per block = floor area ÷ ~87 m² (100 m² a person less the Keel's 5 and the line decks' 8).
- **Light:** the sunline as a diffuse line from z = 450 to 3,300 m (raft-habitat.md's Method), light on a surface facing the axis ∝ ρ²∫dz′ ÷ (ρ² + (z′ − z)²)², scaled to 0.33 kW/m² at mid-floor. It gives ~38% at the foot of an end wall, ~0.15 kW/m² on a terrace 750 m from the axis, and ~28 W/m² on the top terraces.
- **Cab trips:** drum-transport.md's model, door to door: a walk to the core at 1.4√g m/s, a 15 s wait, radial runs at 20 m/s and 1.5 m/s² (plus a second for jerk) to the quickest line deck, a loop leg at that deck's posted limit for the quicker direction, a cross-line at 30 m/s, 20 s per junction turn and 5 s per change. Random pairs average ~6.4 minutes (the doc's 6.2) and the quickest platform is ~1.9 minutes from deck 76 and ~3.1 from deck 375 (the doc's 1.9 and 2.9).
- **Everything else** (prices, weights, Coriolis drift, jump heights) follows the docs it cites; a dropped cup lands (2h)^1.5 ÷ (3√r) antispinward.
