# The third chair: seven Draw Days in a life born outside

*Narrative sketch from a working session, 10 Nov 2026. One life lived outside a household's allowance, in Tansy, a drawn-holdings drum one glide from Sorrel on Herb Row, told on seven Draw Days between the ages of eight and a hundred and twelve. Builds on families.md (born to a place, leave, the draw, the placeless-born, placing, the line outside), people.md (places and shares, the placeless, houses), economy.md (the dividend, the price of an hour, rent, the keep, saving without interest), open-drums.md (Chervil, the block's leave, guest-right), eating-out.md (Tansy, Strain & Vintage), ashgrove.md (Herb Row, the Eleven Days) and raft-habitat.md (the drum). Numbers come from those docs and from `models/placeless_life.py`. Jeff's steer of 10 Nov 2026: the sketch is set in Tansy, with Chervil and Lovage for contrast; the dividends of unheld shares go to the commons in most drums, and a few lend them to the old placeless; houses hold about one place in fifty, with a dissolution in a drum's history; a marriage across the line is placed through the same draw as thirds; and standing is settled by birth and travels by certificate. Everything with a name is invented, and where the docs leave a choice open the sketch picks one; both are listed under What the sketch assumes. Speech and names are given in translation.*

## At a glance
- **Where:** Tansy, a steady drum of 200 million shares on Herb Row of Merrow (eating-out.md), and in it Hartsease, a block of about 3,000 on decks 52–57, at 0.93 g.
- **Who:** Nell Varga, the third child of Dov and Marguerite Varga, born outside their allowance. Her brother Tomas is seven years older and her sister Liesl four, and both were born to a place. Jonah Abara holds a place on deck 148. Odalys Ferro and Bas Lindqvist were born outside too.
- **What she is:** placeless-born (families.md). Tansy says *born outside*, and calls such a child a third chair. She is registered, proofread, fed and schooled like her brother and sister. She can't inherit a place, be named heir to one or bring children into one unless she is placed, and Tansy's line for a share passes over her.
- **The days:** Draw Day at eight, eighteen, twenty-nine, thirty-one, sixty-four, eighty-one and a hundred and twelve. The last is the present.
- **How common she is:** about one birth in 33 in Tansy is outside the allowance, ~50,000 a year, and one in ten of those children is ever placed. Nell is one of the nine.
- **New here:** how Tansy draws; the keep in kWh; Tansy's houses and their boxes; the Emptying; marrying across the line and marrying late; the certificate; waiting shares.

## Tansy
- **A drawn-holdings drum** (families.md § Kinds of drum). Places pass whole to one heir. A household has two children by right and a third by leave. The heirless name an heir for their place, and the rest of the drum's room is drawn by lot.
- **Draw Day** falls a week after Booking Day, when the year's room has been counted. About 45,800 permits are drawn at the nine station rings, some 5,090 at each, and every one is read out with the name of a household that ended without an heir. At five seconds a name it takes about seven hours. A couple with two children and none outside has about one chance in 440 each year, and one in eighteen over the twenty-five years it can enter.
- **The keepers of the draw** are seven people from other drums of the Row, sworn for seven years and lodged at the drum's cost. Tansy's own offices of trust are full of people with no heirs, and those people have nieces and nephews.
- **The keep** for a child born outside is half the child's commons, 0.55 kW, 13.2 kWh a day, until the child is eighteen or placed. It is posted on the board at every station ring.
- **The houses.** About 46,000 bodies that don't die (crews, colleges, guilds, orders) hold some 1.86 million of Tansy's 90 million places, about one in fifty, with rooms for their members, a hall in common and no nursery. A house elects its members. With that many places out of the count of households, Tansy sits about half a percent over par, not the 2.7% of a drum whose placeless-born live in other people's back rooms.
- **The Emptying.** About nine hundred years ago Tansy's assembly dissolved its houses and gave their places to households. It made room for some 3.7 million children, forty-two years of leave at a stroke, and it put four million people into lodgings. Tansy ran 2.7% over par for three centuries, then gave a tenth of its heirless places back to houses each year for four more. The motion to do it again comes up about once a generation.
- **Interest** is forbidden, as in Sorrel. Banked kays are re-lent each booking year and come back as they went.
- **Unheld shares.** About six million of Tansy's shares are unheld on any day, each waiting in a family for its next child, as many as there are children waiting for a share. Their dividends fall to the commons.

## One: eight
*Nell is eight. Tomas is fifteen and Liesl twelve.*

Tansy's schools take every class of eight-year-olds to hear the draw, the way Sorrel's take theirs to the Window. Nell's class stands at the rail of Station Ring 5 from the second hour, above the Quaysides, where the platform has been cleared and a keeper from Yarrow reads from a lectern in a flat, careful voice.

"Permit three thousand and six. In the name of the household of Ines and Corwin Maddox, deck two hundred and one, which ended. Drawn for the household of Sunniva and Joss Pell, deck sixty-four."

Somewhere down the platform people shout, and a couple is lifted off their feet. A third by leave is congratulated like a wedding, and the Pells will very likely name the child Ines or Corwin. Nell's class has been told to count how many of the day's winners they know. By the fourth hour her deskmate Pia has one, because the keeper reads out Pia's own parents, and Pia is sick with joy over the rail.

There are thirty-one children in the class. Nell and a boy called Bas Lindqvist are the two whose parents aren't in the draw and never will be, and they've both noticed.

At dinner she asks why the Vargas never go.

"We had our third," says her mother. Marguerite is forty-seven and tired, and she says it kindly. "You."

Liesl explains it later, in the room they share, with the family's page of the Register on her slate. Great-aunt Sidonie had no children and a place on deck 90, and she had promised to name Marguerite's third as its heir. A naming is that child's leave, as good as a permit. Sidonie died at a hundred and nineteen with the naming unsigned, when Marguerite was four months gone. The place went back to the drum's pool and its room went to the draw. Nobody had done anything wrong, and Nell was born outside.

The page has three lines for the children. Tomas: born to a place. Liesl: born to a place. Nell: a dash. Under them is the keep, 13.2 kays a day, and the note that the household is out of the draw.

The keep is why the Vargas are five people on four wallets with the keep taken off the top: 14.6 kays a head a day, where Pia's family has 21.6. It is why Tomas has no worlds of his own to speak of, why the newest thing in the flat is a kettle, and why Nell, who lives in the one drum on Herb Row famous for its strawberries, has them on her birthday. Hartsease would never say the Vargas were poor. Nobody in Tansy goes hungry or dark. It would say they were *carrying a chair*.

## Two: eighteen
*Nell is eighteen.*

On the Draw Day after her birthday the keep comes off the Vargas' page. Dov has kept the running total in the back of the household book for eighteen years, and he shows it to nobody: 86,800 kays, eleven years of one wallet.

Tomas's dividend went into his own name when he turned eighteen, and Liesl's when she did: 21.6 kays a day each, for life, for nothing. On the morning Nell comes of age nothing arrives. A share comes down a family at a death, to the longest-waiting child born to a place, and in Tansy a child born outside stands behind every one of those. There will always be a baby.

She has the commons, which is more than half of what a person in Tansy lives on: the Mill table, the air, the lit decks, the clinic, the lifts, the Glide. For the rest she has taken a post as a vault hand at the strain library, which is the longest on the Row and the thing Tansy is proudest of after its vat lines. It pays what any full week of work pays, about 17 kays a day. Liesl, who is studying and doesn't work, has 21.6. Tomas, who works, has 38.6.

Her father tries to give her five kays a day out of his own wallet. A dividend can be given. She takes it for a year, to buy a bed and a coat, and then asks him to stop.

"You don't owe anybody anything," Dov says. He means the keep. She knows what he means, and she knows what her brother's worlds cost.

Bas Lindqvist comes by that evening in a jacket with the back crews' crimson at the collar. He has been elected. The crews take fifteen hundred a year on the long articles, twenty-five years in the closed sleds behind the radiators and a placing at the end of it, and about forty-five thousand of each year's third chairs have no other door. Hardly anybody dies on the crews, so nothing thins the ranks but the election. Bas is one in thirty, and he's nineteen, and he will be born to a place at forty-four.

## Three: twenty-nine
*Nell is twenty-nine. Jonah Abara is thirty-seven.*

She met Jonah when she was twenty-six, over a contaminated flask in the receiving room. He is a steward on the vat lines and holds his great-grandmother's place on deck 148: an heir, unmarried at thirty-four, which in Tansy makes a man interesting. They married when she was twenty-seven. A marriage across the line is a marriage. It brings no children into the place until the one from outside is placed, and in Tansy the way to that is the same draw as everybody's, at the same odds.

This is their third Draw Day at the rail. The placings by marriage are read at the end, after the thirds and after the adoptions, twenty-two of them at Ring 5 this year. It is well into the eighth hour when the keeper from Yarrow reaches them, and Abara is early in any list.

It isn't read.

The adoptions were read before it, three hundred and sixty-seven at this ring, and one of them was Odalys Ferro, who trained Nell in the vaults. Odalys was born outside too. For eleven years she has spent her evenings with an old heirless couple on deck 233, reading to the wife and doing the husband's accounts, and this spring they named her heir to their place instead of a great-nephew's child unborn. She is thirty-four, and from today she is born to a place. Nell holds her while she cries. About one heirless household in fourteen does what the old couple did, and a good many of Tansy's third chairs are being good to somebody old.

Jonah does the sum on the cab home, because he is a steward and sums are how he is kind. One chance in 440 a year. If they enter every year until he is sixty-seven it comes to seven in a hundred, and the clinic has banked what they'd need, so a win at sixty would still be two children. Ninety-three in a hundred it is no children at all.

"I can do thirty years of anything with you," he says. "I don't think I can do that."

She doesn't argue. A man who marries her and waits gives up two children he could have placed, for a seven-in-a-hundred chance of getting them back. Her own parents gave up an eighth of one, and it cost her brother his worlds. For three million generations the people who took Jonah's bet left nobody, and she thinks that most of what he calls his feelings is that arithmetic.

They part before the next Booking Day. He marries a woman called Ilse the year after, and Nell sees the birth read on the deck 148 board when she is thirty-three, and another when she is thirty-six.

## Four: thirty-one
*Nell is thirty-one.*

She spent most of a year away first. Chervil is across the rows, an open drum where blocks give leave out loud and place most of the children their own households had without it, and for a third chair from Tansy that sounds like the one place on the Row with a wider door.

It isn't hers. Standing is settled where a person is born. Tansy wrote her a certificate, which says that Tansy answers for her commons wherever she lodges and for any child she has there, and Chervil took her in on it gladly. She worked the overflow at a hall on Ring 4, at six kays an hour when there were hours, since Chervil's halls hire their own first. On her last evening the block's clerk walked her to the station ring.

"We place our own," the clerk said. "It costs somebody a third every time, and the Ledger shows who. If we placed everyone who came across the quay with a certificate, you'd all come, and then there'd be nothing to place anyone into. We could love you and still have no room."

Chervil's own old placeless-born draw what it calls a waiting wallet: the dividend of a share that is between its death and its child, lent until the child is born. Nell's certificate didn't run to that either.

So she is at the rail of Ring 5 again, and this Draw Day she is waiting for the end of it, when the houses read their elections. The motion to empty the houses was on the Booking Day ballot last week, as it is about once a generation. Forty-two years of thirds at once: every couple on the platform has thought about it. It lost by eight points.

Library House holds forty places on decks 30 and 31, above the vaults, and ninety keepers. It says it is the one house in Tansy the Emptying never emptied, since nobody would vote to turn out the people who kept the famine away. It elects two this year, and Nell is the second.

What she gets is a room of 24 m², a hall where a person cooks, eighty-nine people who will notice if she doesn't come down to dinner, and the box. Every keeper pays fifteen parts in a hundred of their pay into the box, about two and a half kays a day, from election until eighty-five, and the box pays a keeper who has stopped work about four kays a day for life. There is no nursery. Two keepers left the year before to have a child outside, in rented rooms on deck 70, and the keep takes 13.2 of the 34 kays the two of them earn. The House sends them a pot on Thursdays.

Corisande, who has the room next to hers, was born in Lovage, where leave is sold. "There it's the ones who couldn't pay who are born outside," she says, "and anyone can tell which third you are by your deck. I came over on a certificate at twenty. Here I'm a keeper with an accent."

Bas has twelve years in, and thirteen to go.

## Five: sixty-four
*Nell is sixty-four. Jonah is seventy-two.*

In between: she was thirty-eight in the Eleven Days, and spent them in the vault corridor with a hand lamp, logging the cold by hand. She was forty-seven when a blight took Tansy's *Fusarium* line in nine days. The vaults hold about two hundred thousand strains, and the one she brought up, a *Neurospora* that the keepers before her had carried for four hundred years without once needing it, fed two hundred million people for a season. The steaks came out nutty and a little orange. Tansy's blight years are vintages (eating-out.md), and the library put the keeper's name on the line, as it does.

Bas was placed at forty-four, married at forty-six and had two children by fifty-one. Odalys has grandchildren.

Jonah finds her at Strain & Vintage on Draw Day, at the long counter on the Ring 5 Quaysides, because it is the one day she is sure to be off shift and he knows where a keeper would eat. He and Ilse parted two years ago, when their younger child had her place, as a good many couples do with ninety years still ahead of them. Both children are householders. His line is set, and nothing he does now can cost it anything.

He orders the flight and pushes the middle plate across to her.

"I have eaten that," she says. "I ate it for five months."

Tansy calls it marrying late. Nobody enters the draw for it and nobody reads it from a lectern. She moves into the place on deck 148 the week after Knotnight, into rooms she last slept in at twenty-nine. The House keeps her seat, since election is for life, and she keeps paying the box.

## Six: eighty-one
*Nell is eighty-one. Tomas is eighty-eight and Liesl eighty-five.*

Dov died last year at a hundred and twenty, and Marguerite this spring, at the same age, in the flat in Hartsease where all three of their children were born.

The clerk of the Register read it out in the block's hall at Hartsease, as the clerk does, with the family standing. Dov's share had gone the year before to a great-great-grandson. Marguerite's goes to Ida, who is three, Liesl's great-granddaughter, born to a place and shareless since birth. The place goes down its own line to the eldest descendant of household age without a household, who is Petra, twenty-four, Tomas's granddaughter.

Nell stands between her brother and her sister and listens. By the Register's own count she has been the longest-waiting shareless person in the Varga family for seventy years. The clerk doesn't pause at her name, because it isn't on his page.

Had she been born a glide and a row away she might have had something. Chervil would by now be lending her a waiting wallet. Tansy sends those dividends to the commons, 5.4 gigawatts of them, and says the Mill table is the same for everyone. It is. Those dividends come to nearly what Tansy's commons spends on all its third chairs together.

On Draw Day she goes down to Hartsease to help Petra move in. Her old room has been let to lodgers for fifty years and smells of somebody else's soap. The table is the same table. It has two chairs on the long side and a third at the end, which doesn't match, because Dov bought it second-hand the winter before she was born, when Sidonie was still alive and it was going to be the heir's.

"Which was yours?" Petra asks, and then hears herself.

"That one," says Nell. "Keep it. You'll want it if you're drawn."

Petra is twenty-four and has just been handed seventy-eight square metres and the right to two children. She says, very seriously, that she and her intended have agreed to enter every year for all twenty-five and never once do what Great-grandmother did. Tomas, who is eighty-eight and grew up a third short from the year he turned seven, looks at the third chair and doesn't say anything.

## Seven: a hundred and twelve
*Nell is a hundred and twelve. This is the present.*

Jonah died last winter at a hundred and nineteen, after forty-seven years of marrying late. His place went down his line. His children are seventy-eight and seventy-five and have had households for half a century, and his grandchildren have theirs, so the eldest descendant without one was a great-grandson, Emeka, who is fourteen. The place is his, held in trust by his mother until he is of age, with its rooms let for him meanwhile.

Emeka's mother came to see Nell the week after the reading. She was kind about it and had plainly been dreading it. Tansy gives a widow from across the line a year's grace in the place. After that Nell could stay as long as she liked, as a lodger, in the back room, at the going rate, which a trust is bound to charge, because a trust can't give away a child's rents. Twenty square metres is two kays a day. The box pays her 3.9.

She was back in Library House before the year was out. Her seat had been kept for forty-eight years, and her room had had eleven keepers in it since. The House eats together, and the hall has a person cooking. She has the commons, the box, and what she banked in sixty years of work, which in Tansy came back exactly as it went. It is enough for a small lamp over a shelf of herbs and one world, on Thursdays.

On Draw Day the House's two newest keepers take her down to the rail of Ring 5, because she asked, and because nobody at Library House has heard as many draws. A keeper from Borage reads this year. Five thousand and ninety permits, each in the name of a household that ended. She has worked out, over a hundred and four years of these, what it is that she won't leave. No permit will be read in her name, because she heads no household to end. No child will be named for her, because children are named for the dead whose shares they carry, and she never carried one.

In the sixth hour the keeper reads a permit in the name of a household on deck 301 that ended, drawn for the household of Petra and Mattis Brandt, Hartsease, deck fifty-five. Petra is fifty-five and has entered for twenty-one years. "She kept the chair," Nell says, out loud, to nobody, and the two young keepers look at each other.

Afterwards they take her along the Quaysides to Strain & Vintage and she lets them order. The list is the length of a wine cellar's. Two-thirds of the way down, between a mild *Fusarium* from a good year and a koji-aged cut with a crust like old cheese, is line 61,204 of the strain library, sixty-five years in the vault since the season it fed the drum, and still listed the way the library wrote it. People who order it mostly think Varga is a place.

## What the sketch assumes
**Choices among options the docs leave open:**
- **Tansy's kind.** A drawn-holdings drum (families.md), with a keep of half the child's commons, the forfeit of the draw, a later place allowed, and about one in ten of its placeless-born placed.
- **Shares.** Tansy puts the placeless-born last in the family's line for a share, so Nell is shareless for life (families.md § The placeless-born). It forbids interest (economy.md § Saving and interest).
- **Draw Day's order:** thirds, then adoptions, then placings by marriage, then the houses' elections. Tansy's schools take eight-year-olds to hear it.
- **Parting.** With ninety years of adult life, many couples part once their children have households. The place stays with whoever holds it.
- **The Register's clerk reads a share and a place** to the family at a death, as Sorrel's does at the Down Halls (sorrel.md).

**New here (proposals):**
- **The doors, divided.** Of ~5,000 placings a year in Tansy: ~3,300 by adoption into an heirless place (one heirless household in fourteen), ~1,500 by twenty-five years on an elected crew, ~200 by marriage through the draw.
- **Marriage across the line goes through the draw.** A couple across the line enters the same draw as couples seeking a third, at the same odds, and a win places the partner from outside. The door by marriage can then be no wider than the draw.
- **Marrying late.** Most marriages between the placeless-born and the born to a place are second marriages, after the partner's children have households, when they cost a line nothing. A widow or widower from across the line has a year's grace in the place and no claim on it.
- **Service is rationed at the election.** Hardly anyone dies on the back crews, so a crew that placed everyone who served would be a wide door. Crews elect, about one in thirty of those with no other door.
- **Houses and the box.** Houses hold about one place in fifty, which is enough to house every placeless-born adult past their twenties, and a drum with that many sits near par. A house keeps a box, as guilds and friendly societies did: members pay in while they work and draw when they stop.
- **The Emptying,** Tansy's dissolution of its houses about nine hundred years ago, and the refounding.
- **The certificate.** Standing is settled by birth. A drum writes a certificate for one of its people who lodges elsewhere, answering for their commons and for any child born to them there. A host drum's door is for its own.
- **Waiting shares.** About 3% of a drum's shares are unheld at any time, waiting for a child (people.md's family-tree model). In Tansy and most drums their dividends fall to the commons. Chervil lends them to its placeless-born elders as waiting wallets.
- **Keepers from outside.** Tansy hires the keepers of its draw from other drums for fixed terms.
- **Smaller things.** "Born outside"; "third chair" and "carrying a chair"; a strain listed under its keeper's name.

## Questions it raises
The questions about rules (whether houses hold shares, what a trust owes a widow from across the line, what a certificate is worth to a host, how the crews elect, who audits a box) are in the open questions of families.md and economy.md. The sketch's own:

- **A promise unsigned.** Sidonie died with the naming unsigned and Nell was born outside. Whether a drum lets a family prove a promise after the death, and how many third chairs are promises that failed.
- **The other eight.** Nell is elected to a house, marries late and has a box. What the same life looks like for a third chair whom no house elects. (deliberately open)

## Method
- **Sums:** `models/placeless_life.py`, output in `models/out/placeless_life.txt`.
- **Hartsease:** decks 52–57, r = 3,000 − 4(n − 1) = 2,796–2,776 m, 0.93 g. Jonah's place on deck 148: 2,412 m, 0.80 g. Library House on decks 30–31: 0.96 g.
- **The class:** births outside the allowance are 3% of replacement births (families.md), one child in 33.
- **The keep:** 0.55 kW × 8,766 h × 18 years ≈ 86,800 kWh, 11.0 wallet-years at 7,890 kWh. Wallets: (4 × 0.9 − 0.55) kW ÷ 5 = 0.61 kW = 14.6 kWh a head a day. A placeless-born couple earns 2 × 17 = 34 kWh a day.
- **Income:** full time is 3.1 kWh × 2,000 h ÷ 365 ≈ 17 kWh a day (economy.md); with a share, 38.6.
- **The draw:** room is 5.7% of ~1.67 M births, ~94,600 slots a year, half the households' to name and half the drum's. Less 1,500 placings for service, ~45,800 permits are drawn, ~5,090 a station ring, ~7.1 hours at five seconds each. A household's chance by the draw alone is 5.7% (one in eighteen) over ~25 years, ~0.23% a year (one in 440). Entered for thirty years, until Jonah is 67, 1 − (1 − 1 ÷ 440)³⁰ ≈ 7%. Marriage placings: 200 a year, ~22 a ring; adoptions 3,300 a year, ~367 a ring, 7% of the ~47,300 places that end without an heir.
- **The crews:** 1,500 elected a year from ~45,000 a year who won't be placed another way: one in thirty.
- **Houses:** placeless-born past 28 are ~77% of 5.4 M, ~4.1 M; at 35 m² each they fill ~1.86 M places of 78 m², 2.1% of 90 M. With them out of the count of households, the drum holds ~195.9 M people born to a place and ~5.3 M placeless-born, 0.6% over par. Dissolved, they make 3.7 M householder slots against ~89,600 thirds by leave a year: 42 years. A tenth of heirless places a year rebuilds them in ~390 years. At ~40 places a house, ~46,000 houses.
- **The box:** 15% of 17 kWh a day from 31 to 85, paid out from 85 to 120: 2.55 × 54 ÷ 35 ≈ 3.9 kWh a day.
- **Waiting shares:** ~3% of 200 M shares, ~6 M, × 0.9 kW ≈ 5.4 GW; the placeless-born's commons, 5.4 M × 1.1 kW ≈ 5.9 GW.
- **Rent:** 20 m² × 0.1 kWh per m² a day (economy.md) = 2 kWh a day.
- **Ages:** Marguerite was 39 and Dov 40 at Nell's birth, so they die at 120 when she is 81 and 80. Tomas was born when Dov was 33; his eldest child at 32 and that child's eldest at 32 make Petra 24 when Nell is 81. Jonah is eight years older than Nell; his children were born when he was 41 and 44, so at his death at 119 they are 78 and 75, his grandchildren in their forties, and his eldest great-grandchild about 14: one of the places in seven that people.md's family-tree model sends to a child.
- **The present:** Nell's seventh Draw Day. The Eleven Days were 74 years ago (ashgrove.md), when she was 38, and the *Neurospora* year 65 years ago, when she was 47.
