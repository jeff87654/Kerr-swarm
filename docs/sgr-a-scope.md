# Scoping the world to Sgr A*

*Feasibility check from a working session, Sept 2026; revised the same month for the full swarm and for a hole held at 30% of Eddington as it grows. Builds on hole-choice.md (conversion efficiency by hole) and swarm-rings.md (orange-slice layout). Assumes Sgr A* (4.3 × 10⁶ M☉) is fed into a thin-disk state, collectors convert ~32–36%, and people need ~3.1 kWe, 6.7 t of heavy elements and ~2.2 t of hydrogen each (3 km nation drums; 3.3 t in the 2 km drums first assumed). **The mature 30% civilization's layout is in full-swarm.md** (the Lantern and the Shade). This doc keeps the feeding levels, disk control, metal supply, why rings fail, and the layer-era design used below ~10% of Eddington. Revised Oct 29, 2026 after a consistency audit.*

## Summary
- **Rings are out.** At parsec scale the swarm's own gravity dominates. Stacked rigid bands attract each other hundreds to thousands of times harder than their orbits can resist, so they collapse together. Rings need orbits shorter than ~16 years (full-swarm.md).
- **This applies to any well-fed hole, not just Sgr A*.** At a given share of the Eddington limit, the layout physics is the same for every hole mass (see below). The real fork is starved vs. fed, not small vs. big.
- **Layer era (below ~10% of Eddington): the habitats form a warm layer.** Units fly free on slightly eccentric, tilted, crossing orbits (Toomre stability). At 3% of Eddington they need random speeds of ~7–11 km/s, and the layer is ~3–5% of the radius thick.
  - **Collectors ride on the habitats,** like solar panels on a habitat around a star. They're oversized ~2× for random shading, and neighbours share power over short beams.
  - **Habitats link into rafts**: flat, rigid platforms of drums joined by truss and vactrain, facing the hole and manoeuvring as one unit. The physical cap is ~12 AU across (~10²³ people). The practical sweet spot is ~0.02–0.1 AU across (~3 × 10¹⁷–7 × 10¹⁸ people, 1.4–35 billion nation drums), where eclipses stay short enough for flywheels.
- **Lantern era (from ~3–8% up; the 30% target):** collectors hover in a closed shell, held up mostly by its own trapped infrared, and the habitats live outside it, in its shade, on beamed power (full-swarm.md).
- **Metal decides the feeding rate.**
  - Mining the nuclear star cluster yields ~4.5 × 10⁵ M☉ of heavy elements, enough for ~1.3 × 10³² people at ~3% of Eddington.
  - 30% of Eddington needs ~4.3 × 10⁶ M☉ for ~1.27 × 10³³ people, about a quarter of the heavy elements in the nuclear stellar disk's living stars.
  - Held at 30%, the civilization then grows with the hole, doubling every ~0.5 Gyr, and needs ~0.01–0.03 M☉/yr of metal to keep pace (matter-delivery.md).
- **30% of Eddington is the preferred target:**
  - as bright as the whole Milky Way;
  - the top of the disk's steady window;
  - a civilization small enough that any two people can exchange messages within ~16 years (through the Lantern's relays; ~20 around it), widening slowly as the hole grows.
- **The feed is held at 30% as the hole grows.** Throttling it would lower the share of Eddington, and with it the Lantern's lift (full-swarm.md).

## Any fed hole: mass sets the scale, not the layout
At a fixed share of Eddington, luminosity (and so population) scales with hole mass. So does the swarm's mass, and the swarm radius grows as √M.
- **The same for every hole mass:**
  - swarm mass ÷ hole mass, nearly (~0.13 at 3%, ~1.1–1.3 at 30%, ~3.5–4.5 at the limit; a bit lower around small holes, whose light converts less well);
  - layer thickness as a share of radius (~5% at 3%);
  - the trapped-light support for a closed Lantern;
  - the verdict on rings.
- **Rings need fast orbits.** Stacked bands (~650 kg/m²) stay apart only if their orbital period is under ~16 years (derivation in full-swarm.md). Starved holes with small swarms qualify (the original 10 M☉ design orbits in weeks to years). Any hole fed at a few percent of Eddington doesn't: every such swarm sits past 100 AU, with orbits of centuries or longer.
- **Rafts work around any fed hole.** Slow orbits mean weak tides, so a raft's own weight (~12 AU cap) is the main limit everywhere.

At 3% of Eddington:

| | 10 M☉ | 10⁴ M☉ | Sgr A* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swarm radius | ~120 AU | ~3,700 AU | ~76,000 AU (0.37 pc) |
| Orbital period | ~400 yr | ~2,200 yr | ~10,000 yr |
| Orbital speed | ~9 km/s | ~49 km/s | ~224 km/s |
| People | ~2.5 × 10²⁶ | ~3 × 10²⁹ | ~1.3 × 10³² |
| Heavy elements | ~0.8 M☉ | ~1,000 M☉ | ~4.5 × 10⁵ M☉ |
| Likely source | ~60 Sun-like stars | a large globular cluster (old, metal-poor) | the nuclear star cluster |
| Ring attraction ÷ restoring force | ~25 | ~140 | ~600 |
| Tidal cap on raft size | ~12 AU | ~70 AU | ~300 AU |
| Collector efficiency (disk light) | ~28% (soft X-rays) | ~32% (soft X-rays / extreme UV) | ~34% (extreme UV) |

- The 10 M☉ column assumes the soft, thin-disk state, which binaries keep down to ~2% of Eddington once they've been brighter; below that a stellar hole is in its hard state and needs X-ray tiles (full-swarm.md).
- **So the design fork is starved vs. fed:**
  - **Starved hole** (≪0.1% Eddington): small, bright, fast-orbiting swarm. Rings work. A mid-sized hole gives the best efficiency (~43%, infrared).
  - **Fed hole, layer era** (~1% to ~3–8% of Eddington): a vast swarm on slow orbits. Warm layer and rafts, ~28–34% efficiency (a stellar hole below ~2% drops to X-ray tiles, ~17–22%).
  - **Fed hole, Lantern era** (from ~3–8%): collectors hover in a closed shell, habitats live in its shade (full-swarm.md).
  - Hole mass then just picks how big the civilization is and where its metal comes from.

## The 30% civilization
Full design in full-swarm.md. In brief:
- **Conversation.** The civilization is ~2.5 pc across. A message to the far side takes ~8 years through the Lantern's relays (~10 going around it, since the Lantern is opaque), and a reply comes back within ~16 (~20). Anyone can hold a lifetime correspondence with anyone. Neighbouring rafts, AU apart, are minutes to hours away by light.
  - At 3% of Eddington, the round trip is ~5 years.
  - At the limit, ~30 years.
- **Scale.** ~1.3 × 10³³ people. The civilization radiates as much as the entire Milky Way, from a region ~2.5 pc across.
- **A self-gravitating swarm.** The Shade outweighs the hole ~1.3×, so orbits are set mostly by the swarm's own mass. Dynamically it is a nuclear star cluster made of habitats. By the shell theorem its mass exerts no pull on the Lantern inside it.
- **Resource frontier.** The heavy elements come from the nuclear stellar disk, 100–200 pc out, whose hydrogen also extends the fuel supply far past the star cluster's. Those mining outposts sit centuries of light-delay beyond the conversational core, a natural cultural and political divide. They can live on their stars' light, so neither side can coerce the other on any human timescale (governance.md).
- **Growth.** The feed is held at 30% as the hole grows, so the civilization grows with it: the hole spins up for ~150–170 Myr (growing ~40%), then doubles every ~0.5 Gyr. The Lantern, the Shade and message delays widen as the square root of the hole's mass: replies across the civilization take ~22 years once the hole has doubled (full-swarm.md, Growth at 30%).

## Feeding and disk control
**Mass flow** (L = η Ṁ c²; η depends on spin):

| | 3% of Eddington | 30% | 100% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spin 0 (η 5.7%) | 0.005 M☉/yr | 0.05 M☉/yr | 0.17 M☉/yr |
| Spin 0.9 (η 15.6%, planning) | 0.0018 M☉/yr (an Earth every ~14 h) | 0.018 M☉/yr (every ~1.4 h) | 0.06 M☉/yr (every ~25 min) |
| Spin 0.998 (η 32%) | 0.0009 M☉/yr | 0.009 M☉/yr | 0.03 M☉/yr |

- **Feeding spins the hole up** toward ~0.998, raising efficiency over time: at 30% it gets there in ~150–170 Myr.
- **Supply.** The nuclear star cluster's stars, stripped, leave ~2.1 × 10⁷ M☉ of hydrogen and helium; its white dwarfs, ~10–15% of its mass, are kept as ore (matter-delivery.md).
  - Held at 30%, that feeds the hole and shields the growing population for ~820 Myr, while the hole grows ~3.5×.
  - Then fuel is imported: the Galaxy's own inflow to the central parsecs is ~0.03 M☉/yr today, and the frontier's stream can carry more.
- **Growth.** At the limit the hole doubles every ~60 Myr.

**Why 100% is harder to run:**
- **Flicker.** Above roughly a third of Eddington, the radiation-pressure-dominated inner disk goes unstable. The stellar-mass binary GRS 1915+105 "heartbeats" on timescales of minutes; scaled to Sgr A*'s mass, that's years. Power swings by factors of several would need storage, trimming, or collectors that can ride them out.
- **Winds.** Near the limit, radiation pressure drives off a large share of the inflow as fast winds (~0.1c). So the feed must exceed what actually falls in, and the Lantern takes a particle wind carrying up to a few percent of the luminosity.
- **Beaming.** The inner disk puffs up and funnels light toward the poles, so illumination varies across the swarm even more than a thin disk's already does (full-swarm.md). Past the limit, extra feed mostly vanishes into the hole with its light, so efficiency falls.
- **Where to inject.**
  - Gas left beyond roughly a thousand gravitational radii (tens of AU) fragments into stars, as the Galactic Center's young stellar disk did. So the feed has to be delivered close in.
  - **Response time** set by where it's delivered:
    - ~100 r_g (~4 AU, where the near-Eddington disk is puffy): the disk answers in ~months;
    - ~1,000 r_g (~40 AU, thin disk): it answers in ~2,000–20,000 years.
  - Close injection gives nimble control, but in a relativistic, brutally bright zone. Far injection is gentle but sluggish, so output must be planned millennia ahead.
- **Steady window.** Real black-hole binaries keep steady thin disks from ~1% to ~30% of Eddington. 30% is the practical ceiling; running at the limit means designing around flicker, wind and beaming.

## Why the orange slice fails here
- Two overlapping sheets of surface density Σ attract at 2πGΣ whatever their separation, as long as the gap is small compared with their width. People-bands average ~650 kg/m², giving ~3 × 10⁻⁷ m/s².
- Around a starved 10 M☉ hole, orbits are fast (weeks to years), so a band nudged toward its neighbor is pulled back far harder than that. Around Sgr A* at parsec scale, orbits take millennia and the restoring force is 600–4,000× weaker than the pull between bands.
- Holding stacked bands apart with struts or thrusters costs a large fraction of their mass. Near the knots, where hundreds of bands stack, it's hopeless.
- A cold, thin layer of free-flying drums is no better: neighbours pull each other into clumps. It stays smooth only if the drums carry random motions (Toomre criterion σ ≳ 3.4 GΣ/Ω). Those random motions make the layer thick and its orbits cross.
- No coherent, non-crossing shell of orbits exists: a shell rotating as a whole moves at orbital speed only at its equator.

## Collectors on the habitats (layer era)
- **Size:** ~12 m² of sky per person (12.4 kW of light at 1 kW/m²), doubled for random shading (below), so a nation drum (~200 M) carries ~5,000 km² of collector (a disk ~80 km across), against its own ~30 km² cross-section. That's ~75 kg per person at ~3 kg/m².
- **No neat tiling.** Crossing orbits mean collectors overlap at random. Total collector area of 2× the sky (optical depth 2) catches ~86% of the light, and each unit is lit ~43% of the time on average. Doubling cheap collectors, and feeding the hole ~15% harder, is cheaper than beaming across a parsec.
- **Uneven sun.** Each unit bobs through the layer over one orbit (millennia), spending long stretches near the sunny inner face and long stretches shaded by others. The disk also shines mostly toward its poles, so units near its plane are dim for their whole orbit. Neighbours share power over short beams. "Sun rights" in the inner face are a political fact of life.
- **Heat.** Everything caught (~86% of the light) eventually leaves the swarm's outer surface as ~360 K infrared, peaking near 8 µm. That includes the electricity, once habitats have used it. The remaining ~14% escapes as the original light. The layer is somewhat opaque to infrared, so units deep in it see others' warm backs and run their radiators hotter; a modest efficiency cost.
- **From ~3–8% of Eddington,** a closed Lantern can hold itself up on its trapped infrared, and the Lantern layout takes over (full-swarm.md).

## Rafts: how many habitats can be linked
A raft is a flat, rigid platform of drums (~one nation drum per ~5,000 km²) joined by truss and vactrain, facing the hole and flying as a single unit in the warm layer. It's flat because it has to face the light, and because a 3-D lump would shade and overheat its own interior. In the Shade, rafts face the Lantern instead, on beamed power with no eclipses; see full-swarm.md.

**Physical limits** (raft areal density ~360 kg/m²: 8.9 t per person over 25 m² of oversized collector):
- **Its own weight is the binding limit.** A raft's gravity pulls it inward, loading the truss in compression. For a uniform disk, the central compression is 4GΣ²R × 0.57.
  - With the rings' truss budget (10% of mass at 1 MJ/kg, buckling-limited): max ~12 AU across, ~10²³ people, ~150 Earth masses.
  - With a 2% truss: ~2.5 AU, ~4 × 10²¹ people, ~6 Earth masses.
  - Spinning a raft (rim speed a few hundred m/s) could relieve the compression and go bigger, but there's no reason to.
- **Traffic isn't the limit.** Whatever its size, a raft meets another about twice per orbital radian (~800 years at 3% Eddington), and has to slide about its own width.
  - Encounters are predictable centuries ahead. Along-track phasing with light-pressure steering alone (~20% of the light's push) shifts a raft ~8 AU per encounter interval.
  - Hydrogen-fed ion thrusters on ~1% of power shift it ~100 AU.
- **Tides from the hole don't matter** around Sgr A* (~10⁻¹⁰ m/s² across an AU). Around a fed 10 M☉ hole they cap rafts at ~12 AU, about the same as their own weight does.

**Practical limits: eclipses and travel.** Every raft spends about half its time shaded by others passing between it and the hole. An eclipse lasts about the raft's width divided by the random speed (~11 km/s at 3%):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 AU (1.5 M km) | 7 × 10¹⁶ | 350 million | 1% of the Moon | 1 day | 2 days | ~40 kg | 0.55 AU (~7 weeks) |
| 0.02 AU | 2.8 × 10¹⁷ | 1.4 billion | 3% of the Moon | 2 days | 3 days | ~80 kg | 0.85 AU (~10 weeks) |
| 0.1 AU | 7 × 10¹⁸ | 35 billion | 1 Moon | 9 days | 16 days | ~390 kg | 2.4 AU (~7 months) |
| 1 AU | 7 × 10²⁰ | 3.5 trillion | 85 Moons | 3 months | 5 months | too heavy; needs beamed power | 11 AU (~2.6 years) |
| 12 AU (cap) | 10²³ | 500 trillion | ~150 Earths | 2.9 years | 5 years | — | ~60 AU (~14 years) |

- **The sweet spot is ~0.02–0.1 AU across.** Eclipses of days to about two weeks are bridged by flywheels, like the knot nights in energy-budget.md. The truss costs ~0.1% of mass.
- **Easy travel saturates anyway.** On a raft, a day's vactrain trip reaches ~4 × 10¹⁷ people, a week ~2 × 10¹⁹, a month ~3 × 10²⁰. Beyond ~0.1 AU, extra linking adds only trips of weeks to months.
- **Rafts vs. rings.** A raft grows reach in two directions, so beyond a day's trip it beats a single ring. The ring world, with ring-hopping, matched it for about a week and stayed one continuous network. Rafts give deeper local connectivity and shallower global connectivity.
- **Between rafts, travel is spaceflight.** Neighbouring rafts are AU away and drift at tens of km/s relative to each other, so a trip takes months. Each raft is effectively an island world; inter-raft travel is closer to emigration, though messages cross in minutes to hours.
- **Count:** ~10¹³–10¹⁵ rafts at 3% Eddington.
- Rafts of a Moon's mass or more scatter each other gravitationally in encounters. That helps keep the layer warm, but it limits how far ahead traffic control can predict.

## The Lantern: statites and flux
Moved to full-swarm.md, with corrections to the first estimates:
- **The disk light's net push is ~0.25 of its momentum, not ⅓,** once collectors beam their power outward.
- **But a closed shell is held up mainly by its own trapped infrared,** which pushes 5–20× harder than that, evenly at every latitude. At 30% it carries ~10–40 kg/m². The first scoping's worry that collectors only "nearly float" at 30% was backwards: the Lantern has weight to spare.
- **The disk shines toward its poles** (~2.6× the average), so polar collectors run hotter.
- **Flux trade, recomputed** with the planning optics (0.75) and all caught light leaving the outer skin. For the layer era's collectors at uniform flux:

| Flux at the collectors | 1 kW/m² | 3 kW/m² | 10 kW/m² | 30 kW/m² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radiator temperature | ~360 K | ~470 K | ~640 K | ~840 K |
| Collector efficiency | ~35% | ~32% | ~27% | ~20% |
| Radius (at 3% Eddington) | 0.37 pc | 0.21 pc | 0.12 pc | 0.07 pc |

- ~1–3 kW/m² is still the sweet spot. The Lantern runs at ~2 kW/m² average.

### Why collectors inside and habitats outside
Not to run the furnaces hotter. Furnace temperature is set by how hard the mirrors concentrate the light, and that limit is the brightness of the glowing disk's surface. That brightness is the same from any distance, so moving the collectors in doesn't raise it.

The ordering comes from shade and from heat rejection:
- **Collectors must be the first surface the light meets.** Anything inside the shell intercepts light meant for the collectors and sits in the full glare of the disk's ultraviolet and X-rays.
- **A closed shell's interior is an oven.** Every inward-facing surface sees only other inward-facing surfaces, so nothing inside can radiate heat away. Habitats inside would have nowhere to dump their ~kilowatt per person; all waste heat has to leave through the shell's outer skin.
- **Outside, habitats get shade and a cold sky.** The Lantern blocks the hole's radiation. Their radiators face space, except for the Lantern's warm skin. full-swarm.md works out how rafts cope with it.

## Other consequences
- **The central parsec's corpses.** Perhaps ~10⁴ stellar black holes, plus neutron stars, can't be mined; they must be herded out or fed to Sgr A*. White dwarfs can be: they're nearly pure carbon and oxygen (matter-delivery.md).
- **Leftover hydrogen and helium** from stripped stars is fuel for hundreds of millions of years at 30%, free shielding, and free propellant for traffic control.
- **Winds.** Disks near Eddington blow fast outflows (~0.1c) carrying up to a few percent of the luminosity. In the Lantern era the collectors take them; in the layer era rafts need shielding, or can harvest the wind.
- **Seen from outside:** an infrared ember.
  - At 3% of Eddington it is ~0.75 pc across, at ~360 K, and gives ~10% of the Milky Way's output.
  - At 30% it is ~2.5 pc across and matches the whole galaxy. Most of the light leaves the Lantern's skin at ~420 K (hotter toward the poles), seen through the Shade, which radiates the beamed fifth at ~300 K. It peaks near 7–10 µm.
  - It is ~100–1,000× brighter than the star cluster it replaced, and dominates the galaxy's mid-infrared.
  - Astronomers would first take it for a dust-buried active nucleus. The tells are its narrow range of temperatures, its polar brightening, and the hard light leaking through.

## Suggested scoping
- **Start:** Sgr A* at ~3% of Eddington (~0.002 M☉/yr) after mining the nuclear star cluster: ~1.3 × 10³² people in a warm layer ~0.37 pc out, in rafts carrying their own collectors.
- **Mature civilization: ~30% of Eddington (~0.018 M☉/yr), held there as the hole grows; full-swarm.md.**
  - ~1.3 × 10³³ people in the Shade, 0.9–1.25 pc out, powered by the Lantern at ~0.8 pc, doubling every ~0.5 Gyr with the hole.
  - Mining about a quarter of the heavy elements in the nuclear stellar disk's living stars to get there, then ~0.01–0.03 M☉/yr to keep pace; metal is the binding constraint.
  - The disk sits at the top of its steady window.
  - Any two people can exchange messages within ~16 years, through the Lantern's relays.
- The same design works for a fed 10 M☉ or 10⁴ M☉ hole, at smaller scale.

## Open questions
- Layer era: power sharing between shaded and sunny rafts (short beams, markets, or scheduling orbits to avoid shade).
- Traffic control across the layer and the Shade: how far ahead encounters can be predicted, and who arbitrates. For the Shade, governance.md sketches thin rules of the road.
- How much the layer's infrared opacity raises radiator temperatures.
- Life on an island raft: inter-raft migration, trade and culture drift at months-to-years of separation.
- The core and the frontier: governance between a conversational core and mining outposts centuries of light-delay away (see governance.md).
- Feed logistics: who runs the injection stream close to the hole, and how output changes are planned given the disk's lag. Far injection doubles as a check on power (governance.md).
- The handover from layer to Lantern: who moves out, and when.
- The civilization's bootstrap: how the first collectors were powered before the hole was fed.
