# Economy: a stationary state in the Shade

*Design proposal from a working session, Oct 2026. How people in the Shade earn, spend, own, save and trade, inside the limits the other docs set: a fixed share of the beam (daily-life.md's 2 kWe), a fixed share of metal (~6.7 t, habitat-design.md), a closed population (matter-delivery.md), rafts that reach each other only by light (full-swarm.md), and a Lantern months to years away. Builds on daily-life.md's commons and wallet, with prices in kWh as there. The institutions and names here (the dividend, the place, the share, birth rights, the heat cap, the ledger) are proposals; the limits under them are the physics of the other docs. Revised Oct 2026 for the 3 km nation drum of ~200 M (raft-habitat.md): more births and new places per drum, and a smaller Hollow per person. Revised 1 Nov 2026 (Jeff's decisions; people.md): places are household land, like farms, ~150 M to a nation drum; each person's wallet comes with a share, freed by a death in the family.*

## Summary
- **A stationary state.** Energy is a flow fixed per person, metal a stock fixed per person, and the population is closed (growth ~10⁻⁹ a year). Nothing compounds. In shape it's an agrarian economy, with metal for land and the beam for the harvest, rents and status, and machines doing all the farming.
- **Post-labour, not post-scarcity.** Machines make everything; physics sets the budget.
- **Every price is an energy cost, a rent or someone's time.** Made things cost their kWh (daily-life.md's price list). Fixed things earn rents: floor, the Hollow, high decks, places for children. People's time costs what others will pay for it.
- **Four ledgers:** kWh, a yearly flow, is the money; mass, a fixed stock, is property; shares, one per person at par, carry the wallet and pass to children; places, about three for every four people, are household land.
- **Income is a dividend plus work.** The commons (~1.1 kW, for everyone) and an equal beam dividend (~0.9 kW, the wallet, one per share) cover a whole life. Work is how a wallet grows. If people spend a quarter of their wallets on each other's time, an hour costs ~3 kWh, a little more than a Vat-table dinner.
- **Children are the dearest thing.** A nation drum gains a new share about once every three and a half years; all other births wait on deaths. In most drums a death frees a share for a newborn in the family, so a household's births follow its own deaths; some keep a pool with one birth right per share (people.md). Whether such rights can be sold is the sharpest choice in the setting.
- **Savings earn nothing.** With growth at ~10⁻⁹ a year, interest is ~zero: a kWh lent for a thousand years comes back a kWh. Fortunes don't compound; they're rents or reputations. The only capital that grows is information.
- **Each scale trades something different:** the drum services, the raft goods, the Shade information and beam, the frontier metal, on million-year contracts.
- **The Lantern is utility, bank and sovereign.** Its beam ledger settles every payment between rafts. Light delay makes the Dark a sentence rather than a siege: an unscheduled cut lasts at least twice the delay (6 months to 2.8 years), longer than a raft can live through, so a cut's length has to be set when it's ordered. Glow sails, heat engines run on the Lantern's own glow, would let a raft away from the disk plane outlast a cut on rations and make it a siege again (governance.md).
- **The rich buy light, speed, new mass, Hollow hours, high decks, other people's time and, if rights are sold, children.** Nobody can buy more air, more in-hull heat than the drum's radiators shed, or metal that hasn't arrived.

## Who holds what
| Layer | People | Holds | Decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Lantern | None | The power, batch compute, the relays | Beam per raft; cuts |
| A raft | 10¹⁹–10²² | The beam contract; truss, rectennas, radiators, tubes, cyclers, data halls, the Yards | Beam per drum; cycler speeds; its drum size |
| A drum | ~200 M | The commons, the food plant, the reserve, fixed goods, the pool of places and shares | The commons' size; who gets fixed goods; birth rights |
| A person | 1 | One share; from about 30, usually a place | Their wallet |

- **The commons is the drum's budget:** ~55% of the beam, spent in kind on everyone, with a share or without. Drums set it differently; one runs a bright Hollow and lean wallets, its neighbour the reverse. The Glide is free, so residents can swap homes into the drum that suits them.

## What's scarce
| | Kind | Per person, at 2 kW | Supply | Priced by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Electricity | Flow | ~2 kWe (17,500 kWh a year): 1.1 commons, 0.9 wallet | The Lantern's output ÷ population | It's the unit |
| In-hull heat | Flow, capped | ~0.8 kW of 300 K radiator | The raft's back layer (raft-habitat.md) | A premium once the cap binds |
| Metal | Stock | ~6.7 t: 100 m² of floor (~35 m² of it home) and the rest of a share | One new share per nation drum every ~3.6 years | Rent |
| Belongings | Stock | ~2.5 t of furnishings, belongings and greenery, shared with workplaces and public rooms | Fixed | Remaking (~25 kWh/kg), plus rent if mass is short |
| The Hollow | Area | 0.18 m²: about three-quarters of an hour a week at beach density | Fixed | Ration or auction |
| Human time | Time | ~16 waking hours a day | Fixed | Wages |

- **Cheap:** the Mill table, alcohol, the Glide, freight, copies of anything, dye and pattern, hot water, hydrogen.

## Money: the kWh
- **Prices are in kWh** (daily-life.md). A kWh is a claim on the raft's beam. A raft books its beam a year ahead (full-swarm.md) and issues claims to match, so money is a booking.
- **The year is the unit.** A raft knows its harvest a year out. Energy keeps for hours to days (flywheels, the methanol tank farm), so a saved kWh is a loan to someone who spends it now.
- **One currency, no exchange rates.** A kWh is a kWh on every raft. Local things (floor, Hollow hours, time) are priced differently from raft to raft, but the unit is the same; between rafts there are only delays.
- **kWh cost most at lamp-noon and least in the lamp-night.** The beam is steady and demand isn't. Lights, kitchens and evening worlds peak in the habitat's day; batch work, fabrication and slow vat lines take the night (daily-life.md). Time-of-day prices fill the night.
- **Inflation is overbooking.** A raft that issues more claims than its beam has defaulted. A cut is a default imposed from outside.
- **Two kinds of kWh: the heat cap (proposal).**
  - A kWh spent inside the hull ends as 300 K heat on the hull's radiators. One spent in a data hall, the Yards or the tubes leaves from hotter panels on the raft's back.
  - The hull's radiators carry ~0.8 kW per person, set when the raft was laid out (raft-habitat.md), and the 2-kW budget already fills them. Any shift of wallets into the hull meets the cap, so a cool kWh costs more than a hot one. A heat pump lifting the excess to 350 K panels costs ~0.33 kWh per kWh, if those panels have room.
  - A private 10 m² garden at overcast-day brightness takes a fifth of a wallet (daily-life.md) and a quarter of a person's in-hull heat. Bright homes are limited by the drum's radiators, not just by wallets.

## Income
### The dividend (proposal)
- **The raft's beam, less the commons, is split equally among shares:** ~0.9 kW, 21.6 kWh a day, ~7,900 kWh a year. It's daily-life.md's wallet. A person without a share gets the commons but no dividend (see Children).
- **A child's dividend goes to their household** until they come of age.
- **It can be spent, given or lent, but not pledged past the booking year.** Otherwise a poor resident could sell decades of dividend for an express leg or a third child.
- **The drum pays its own people** (teachers, nurses, stewards, councils) from a levy on the dividend; the commons' 1.1 kW stays physical. If a tenth of adults work full time for the drum, the levy is ~7% of a dividend.

### Work
- **Machines make everything:** the Mill and the Vats, the Yards, the data halls, rectennas, radiators, lifts, the Glide, the Lantern.
- **People sell what people want from people:**
  - care: children, the sick, the old;
  - teaching and mentoring;
  - kitchens and hospitality: a meal cooked by a person is the everyday luxury;
  - craft: mending, tailoring, custom fabrication, design;
  - the Garden: herbs, orchards and any animals;
  - stewardship: strain libraries and phage watch (food-system.md), Quayside quarantine, the reserves and the Dark drills;
  - governance: drum councils, courts, raft assemblies, beam diplomats;
  - culture: performance, sport, shared worlds, research.
- **Machines could do most of these.** People pay for the person, the way audiences still pay four players for a string quartet (Baumol, Earth anchors).
- **Nobody has to work.** People work for wallet, standing, the people they serve, and birth rights, if those are sold.

### The price of an hour
Paying for someone's time moves claims; it doesn't use energy. A claim paid to a tutor is spent on energy later, by the tutor. So if residents spend a share of their money on each other's time, money income averages ~7,900 kWh a year ÷ (1 − that share), while energy use stays at ~7,900.

| Share of wallets spent on people's time | 10% | 25% | 40% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spent per resident a year | ~880 kWh | ~2,600 kWh | ~5,300 kWh |
| Price of an hour if adults sell 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 hours a year | 2.1 / 1.0 / 0.5 kWh | 6.2 / **3.1** / 1.5 kWh | 12 / 6.2 / 3.1 kWh |

- **Planning case: a quarter of wallets and ~1,000 hours per adult give ~3 kWh an hour,** a little more than a Vat-table dinner (2.48 kWh). A cook for a dinner party of four, three hours, costs about as much as the four dinners.
- **A full-time worker (2,000 hours) earns ~6,200 kWh a year** on top of the dividend, ~1.8× a non-worker's income. A sought-after surgeon, teacher or performer earns more.

### The floor and the ceiling
- **The floor is the commons:** the Mill table, air, lit decks, clinics, schools, lifts and the Glide. An empty wallet means no personal compute, no long trips and no new things (daily-life.md), but nobody goes hungry or dark.
- **The ceiling is physical.** Extra kWh can't go into the hull past the heat cap, can't buy floor nobody lets, and can't buy metal that hasn't arrived. Great wealth goes into remote things (compute, speed, new mass), other people's time, rents and, if they're sold, children.

## Property
### The share (proposal)
- **Every person at par holds one share:** ~6.7 t of habitat (100 m² of floor and part of the food plant, shield, reserve and raft), their part of the beam (~2 kWe: the commons and the wallet), and ~3 m² of the raft's back. A drum of 200 M holds 200 M shares.
- **It can't be sold or pledged.** Selling it would mean selling your air.
- **Its yields can be traded:** wallet kWh, heat allowance and Hollow hours.
- **A death frees it, and it passes to a newborn,** in most drums within the family (people.md). A child born ahead of a death has no share until one frees. Pool drums return every share to the pool and run birth rights instead (see Children).

### The place (proposal)
- **A place is household land, like a farm:** ~47 m² of home, about 1⅓ people's share, so a nation drum of 200 M is divided into ~150 M places. The rest of the floor (workplaces, schools, halls, streets, parks and the Keel) belongs to the commons.
- **Children and young adults live in a parent's or grandparent's place.** Most people get their own around 30, usually a great-grandparent's (people.md).
- **It can't be sold or pledged.** Its rooms can be let.
- **At death it passes to an heir,** so nobody is turned out when the householders die. Places with no heir return to the drum's pool. Pool drums return every place to the pool and allot places by age or auction.

### Metal is the land
- **New shares almost never come.** After the hole spins up, growth is ~1.4 × 10⁻⁹ a year (matter-delivery.md): one new share per nation drum every ~3.6 years, or every ~1.3 while the hole spins up, and a new place every ~4.8. All other births wait on deaths.
- **New metal can't be bought at any human price.** A share's metal from stars costs 7–15 billion kWh, 400,000–900,000 years of a 2-kW budget. From gas it can cost as little as ~28,000 kWh, but gas arrives only so fast.
- **Supply is fixed on every human timescale.** Payloads fall for ~1 Myr, so every kilogram that will arrive in the next million years is already on its way. Metal's price is a rent, set by demand against a fixed stock, as Ricardo and George described for land.
- **Building is cheap by comparison.** Turning a share's metal into habitat costs ~0.75 TJ (~205,000 kWh, ~26 years of a wallet); rebuilding a square metre of floor ~1,250 kWh. So flats are reused, not rebuilt, and most are older than anyone living in them.

### Homes
- **Floor comes per person, homes per household.** Each share's 100 m² includes ~35 m² of home (raft-habitat.md), held as places of ~47 m². A couple raising two children in their two places has ~94 m², ~23 m² a head; an old couple whose children have their own places has the same ~94 m² for two.
- **Floor is let and swapped, not built.** An elder alone in a family flat lets rooms; a growing family or a young lodger rents them. A move between drums is a swap of homes, across a metro of ~1.6 × 10¹⁰ people within a free 20-minute glide (raft-transport.md).
- **In the crowded 2-kW world** (daily-life.md), each share would carry ~55 m² of floor: ~19 m² of home at the same proportions.

### Belongings
- **Mass is a stock too.** Each share carries ~2.5 t of furnishings, belongings and greenery, across home, workplace and public rooms (habitat-design.md). Owning more than one's share means someone owns less.
- **New things are remade from returned ones** at ~25 kWh/kg (daily-life.md); passing an heirloom on costs nothing. The second-hand market is the main market for things, and new mass is the display good.

### The Hollow and the high decks
- **The Hollow gives everyone about three-quarters of an hour a week.** Its ~35 km² floor holds ~1.4 M people at a crowded beach's ~25 m² each, ~0.7% of the drum. Open 16 hours a day, that's ~7 minutes per resident per day; at park density, ~12 minutes a week. (A 2 km drum's Hollow gave about an hour a week.)
  - So Hollow hours are rationed by deck or auctioned, and traded. A Hollow hour is the classic gift.
  - daily-life.md's resident spends an hour there every day, ~9× an equal share: a high-decker's privilege, or hours bought from others.
- **High decks** hold ~30 M of a drum's ~200 M (raft-habitat.md): the lowest dose, the lightest step, the Hollow at the door.
- **Who collects the rents decides the society.** Proposal: the drum owns fixed goods, auctions their use, and returns the rents through the commons or the dividend. That's Henry George's single tax, with metal for land.

| Fixed goods are | Rents go to | The society |
|---|---|---|
| Auctioned by the drum (proposal) | The commons and the dividend | Equal dividends, unequal flats, no landlords |
| Allotted by age or service | Nobody | Elders up, families down; standing by seniority |
| Owned and inherited | Families | A high-deck gentry living on rents, generation after generation |

## Children
- **Births equal deaths,** so a drum's births each year are its population ÷ lifespan:

| Lifespan | 80 yr | 120 yr | 200 yr | 400 yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Births per nation drum per year | 2.5 M | 1.7 M | 1.0 M | 500,000 |
| Share under 18 | 22% | 15% | 9% | 4.5% |

- **Drums choose how births follow deaths** (people.md). Each gets a fixed share of the beam, so overbreeding thins only its own shares.
- **Holdings, the common system.** A death frees a share, and it passes to a newborn in the family, often named for the dead; it also frees a place for a young adult in the family. With 120-year lives and children around 30, the dying generation is the young couples' great-grandparents: a couple's 16 great-grandparents free, on average, a place for each partner and a share for each of two children. Couples marry when they hold places, around 30.
- **A child born ahead of a death has no share** until one frees in the family, so the household divides its wallets: a third child takes a family of four from 21.6 kWh a head to ~17. Because wallets come only from deaths, an extra child's wallet is the family's own cost.
- **Birth rights, in pool drums.** Every share returns to the pool at death and carries one claim to a share for a child, so a couple has two children: replacement, as daily-life.md assumes. A birth draws a share from the pool now, and the parent's own share refills it at their death; the pool keeps a buffer for the gap. Places return to the pool too, and go to adults by age or auction.
- **Can rights be sold?**

| | Tradable | Giftable only | Unused rights go to a lottery |
|---|---|---|---|
| A third child comes from | Buying a right | A childless sibling or friend | Winning one |
| Big families are | Rich | Well connected | Lucky |
| The danger | Poor residents sell the right to their own child | Pressure on the childless | Nobody can plan a third |

- **Boulding's 1964 proposal is the tradable version** (Earth anchors).
- **Long lives make children rarer, not fewer per couple.** At 400 years a drum sees ~1,400 births a day, and one resident in 22 is a child.
- **A shareless child is the hardest case.** In a pool drum, a birth without a right either takes a share from the pool at someone else's expense or grows up without one. How the drum answers the parents is the setting's sharpest law. In a holdings drum the child thins its own household until a death in the family frees a share.
- **The shareless get the commons and work.** Nobody goes hungry or dark, because the commons is per head; what they lack is the wallet. At the planning price of ~3 kWh an hour, full-time work earns ~17 kWh a day, ~80% of a wallet, and in a drum like Chervil (~6 kWh an hour) more than a wallet. So a shareless adult who works full time lives about as well as someone with a share who doesn't. They're the Shade's younger sons and servants in husbandry, working because they must beside people who work for standing. In a thin drum they crowd the market for hours, so labour there is cheap.
- **Overbreeding still leaks.** Matching wallets to deaths puts an extra child's wallet half (0.9 kW) on the family, but its commons half (1.1 kW) still falls on the whole drum. That's why drums still need norms.
- **A child's purse** (a partial wallet the drum pays to shareless children) is a drum's choice. Every kWh of it is paid by everyone else for someone else's extra child, as in Russia's repartitional commune (Earth anchors), so most drums pay nothing beyond the commons, and the few generous ones run thinner.

## Saving and interest
- **No growth, no interest.** In Samuelson's consumption-loan model, money's interest rate equals the population's growth rate (Earth anchors). Here that's ~10⁻⁹ a year: a kWh lent for a thousand years comes back as 1.0000014 kWh.
- **There's little to invest in.** The beam is fixed, the metal is fixed, and after hundreds of millions of years the technology has stopped improving (if it hasn't, efficiency gains are the one investment that pays). Saving moves consumption between people and across a life: the young borrow, the old lend, and it nets to zero.
- **Fortunes don't compound.** Wealth is a rent (if fixed goods are owned), a reputation or a collection. There's no capital to accumulate.
- **Information is the one stock that grows:** designs, recipes, culture genomes, music, science. It costs nothing to copy.
- **Big risks are covered in kind, by the drum and the raft:** the ~55-day survival reserve and 90-day larder (food-system.md), strain libraries against blights, and fast cyclers against cuts (raft-transport.md).

## Markets by scale
| Scale | People | Delay | What moves | What's traded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drum | ~200 M | Minutes by cab | People, food, things | Services, homes, Hollow hours |
| Glide metro | ~1.6 × 10¹⁰ within 20 minutes | 10–20 minutes | People, daily | Jobs, schools, markets: Earth's population, a free ride away |
| Raft | 10¹⁹–10²² | Days to years by cycler; under a minute to ~13 minutes by light | Goods; few people | Goods; beam between drums |
| Shade | ~10³³ in 10¹¹–10¹³ rafts | Minutes to ~10 years by light | Information only | Designs, recipes, culture genomes, art, science; beam, through the Lantern |
| Frontier | Outposts | Centuries by light; ~1 Myr by payload | Metal in, beam out | Million-year contracts |

- **A raft is one market for goods.** Moving a kilogram 0.1 AU at 5 km/s costs ~0.035 kWh, ~0.1% of making it, and takes ~35 days.
- **The Shade is one market for information and beam, and none for goods.** Rafts pass at 100–300 km/s, AU apart, so goods almost never move between them (food-system.md). People move only as emigrants, and a share doesn't travel: an emigrant needs one where they land, so the natural form is a swap.
- **Information can't carry royalties at this scale.** A hit enjoyed by 10¹⁹ people on one raft, at a hundredth of a kWh each, would pay its author ~10¹⁷ kWh: a year's wallets for ~10¹³ people, ~1,500 Earths. The heat cap, places and the Hollow stop the author spending it on themselves, but it would buy everything else for sale. So information is paid for as a public good, by commissions and prizes from the commons, patronage and reputation, and copies are free.
- **Drums specialise slowly.** With ~1.6 × 10¹⁰ people a free 20-minute glide away, a drum known for a hospital, a school, a kitchen or an orchard draws from the whole metro. But floor costs ~1,250 kWh a m² to rebuild, so a drum's mix of homes and workplaces changes over centuries.

## The Lantern: utility, bank and sovereign
- **A natural monopoly.** One opaque, machine-run shell makes the power for 10³³ people (full-swarm.md), and every raft's beam contract is with it.
- **Its ledger is the bank.** A payment between rafts is a beam transfer: the payer's booking falls and the payee's rises. Commissions, prizes and loans between rafts all settle there.
- **No spot market.** A booking lands after the light delay, 3 months to 1.4 years, and a request takes as long to arrive. The shortest power contract is ~0.5–3 years out.
- **Without glow sails, the Dark is a sentence, not a siege.** A cut takes effect when the last beam photons arrive. If the raft's answer is what ends it, the dark lasts at least twice the delay:

| Raft's distance from the Lantern | 0.08 pc (inner edge) | 0.27 pc (mid-Shade) | 0.43 pc (outer edge) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One way by light | ~3 months | ~11 months | ~1.4 years |
| Shortest unscheduled cut | ~6 months | ~1.8 years | ~2.8 years |

  - A raft lasts ~55 days on its survival reserve, or ~4–7 months with fast cyclers (raft-transport.md). So an unscheduled cut kills, except perhaps an inner raft running fast cyclers.
  - A cut's length has to be set when it's ordered (thirty days of Dark, then the beam returns), and announced ahead if the target is meant to prepare. Sanctions are sentences passed a year or more in advance, not sieges that end at surrender.
  - That makes inner rafts with fast cyclers the hardest to coerce.
  - **Glow sails change this.** The Lantern can't stop glowing, and heat engines on its glow carry a raft on rations (~0.4 kW per person) for as long as a cut lasts, except near the disk plane. A sailed raft can be besieged and can surrender, and starving it means darkening a whole region of the Lantern (governance.md).
- **Beam follows shares, not heads (proposal).** If beam followed headcount, a raft could buy power by packing in people on smaller floors, and every raft would crowd. Tied to shares, a fixed count set when the habitat was built, a raft's beam is fixed, and crowding only dilutes it.
- **Who runs it** is the founding question (full-swarm.md, governance.md):

| The Lantern is run as | Strength | Danger |
|---|---|---|
| A constitution in hardware: fixed rules, beam by share, cuts only by slow public process | Nobody can capture it | Nobody can fix it |
| A cooperative of rafts | Accountable | 10¹¹–10¹³ members, voting by letters that take up to ~10 years |
| An order of keepers, the few who tend its machines and read its code (minds stay biological; governance.md) | Competent and continuous | Everyone's power and money run through a few people's hands |

## The frontier
- **The core pays in light; the frontier pays in metal.** The beam reaches the outposts ~330–650 years after it's sent; their metal reaches the Shade ~1 Myr after launch (matter-delivery.md).
- **Contracts outlive languages.** The metal arriving today was launched before any living culture began. To the core it's weather, not trade: a fixed stream it can't speed up or renegotiate.
- **It costs a few watts per person,** the commons' growth line.
- **The core's leverage is the beam, and it's weak.** A cut lands centuries after it's sent and only slows the mining: outposts can live on their stars' light, and one that fuses its own hydrogen needs no beam at all (matter-delivery.md, governance.md).

## A life on the ledger
| Stage | What happens | Ledger |
|---|---|---|
| Birth | Takes a share freed by a death in the family, and often the dead's name (in pool drums, a share from the pool and a parent's birth right); a child born ahead of a death waits for one | +1 person |
| Childhood | A parent's or grandparent's place, on the Low decks near the stations; school and clinic from the commons; the dividend goes to the household | — |
| Coming of age | The full dividend in one's own name, for those with a share; work and lodging for those without | 21.6 kWh a day |
| About 30 | A place of one's own, usually a great-grandparent's; marriage | A place |
| Working life | Work for wallet and standing; save for express legs, a sofa, Hollow hours; raise two children on shares freed by the great-grandparents' deaths | Wages in; in pool drums, a birth right spent, given or sold |
| Later life | Up to the high decks, if the drum allots them by age; let rooms; perhaps hand the place to an heir early; hand down heirlooms | Rents in |

## Rich and poor
| | Empty wallet | Typical | Rich |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food | The Mill table | The Vat table most days | Orchard fruit, cultivated steak, a cook |
| Light | Lit decks, public parks | Home at ~200 lux | A private garden at overcast-day brightness |
| Travel | The Glide | Slow cyclers, a long trip a year | Express legs |
| Things | Hand-me-downs, mended | A few new things a year | New furniture; heirloom collections |
| Compute | Public only | ~10 kWh a day | Whole worlds |
| Space | A room in the family's place | A family flat | A high deck, rented rooms, Hollow hours |
| Service | Robots | Robots; a person for occasions | People: cooks, tutors, tailors |
| Children | Two, or a third without a share | Two | Three or more, if shares or rights allow |

- **Poverty is a thin wallet, not hunger or the dark.**
- **The plainest signs of wealth** are light (daily-life.md), speed, new mass and people.
- **Rafts differ more than residents.** Beam per share is equal everywhere, but rafts differ in drum standard, cycler speed (how long they'd last in the Dark), age (the oldest end up deepest inside) and distance from the Lantern.

## If minds run as software
- **Not in this setting, for now.** Minds stay biological (governance.md). This section is what would change if they didn't.
- **At a brain's ~20 W,** the light behind one 2-kW life (~8.3 kW, ~2.6 kWe on the Lantern) would run ~130 minds, with no place and no hull. One mind costs ~0.8% of a person's light.
- **That's energy-budget.md's sharp exchange rate.** Anyone who could choose would face it.
- **Going up.** A person who moves to the Lantern frees a share for a child and costs the civilization ~1% of what they did. If that's possible, every pressure in this doc points up, and the line between the Shade and machine country (full-swarm.md) becomes an emigration route.
- If it isn't, compute stays a consumer good: the biggest line in every wallet (daily-life.md).

## Culture (proposals)
- **Gifts are reckoned in kWh** (daily-life.md). The grandest are a Hollow hour and a birth right.
- **The live and the handmade are the luxuries:** a meal cooked by a person, a coat cut by a tailor, a quartet in heliox.
- **The booking turns the year.** The day a raft learns its beam for the year ahead is the nearest thing to a harvest festival.
- **Children may be named for the dead whose shares they carry.**
- **Heat theft.** Unmetered lamps show up as heat on the hull's radiators, so a drum finds them by their warmth.
- **Bootleg liquor** is still the food plant's methanol (food-system.md's gin problem).

## Earth anchors
- **Mill's stationary state (1848).** Mill argued that an economy with fixed capital and population needn't stop human progress: "a stationary condition of capital and population implies no stationary state of human improvement." The Shade is that economy, built.
- **Ricardo and George on rent.** Land in fixed supply earns a rent set by demand, not cost. Henry George's *Progress and Poverty* (1879) proposed taxing that rent away. Here metal is the land.
- **Technocracy's energy certificate (1930s).** Technocracy Inc. proposed money denominated in energy: an equal share of the continent's energy for each adult, valid for ~14–15 months, impossible to save, lend or give away, and paying no interest. The Shade's kWh is the same idea with transfers allowed, since without them nobody could pay for anyone's time.
- **Boulding's marketable licences (1964).** Kenneth Boulding proposed issuing licences to have children, enough for replacement and traded in tenths of a child; Herman Daly later backed the idea.
- **Samuelson's "biological" rate of interest (1958).** In his consumption-loan model with money, interest equals the population's growth rate.
- **Hajnal's European marriage pattern (1965).** In northwest Europe a couple married when it could set up its own household, usually on a holding of its own, so people married late and many never did. Places work the same way.
- **Retirement contracts.** Long-lived Central European farmers often handed the farm to the heir before death and kept a room and their keep under a contract, the *Altenteil* (people.md).
- **Russia's repartitional commune.** The *obshchina* re-divided village land among households by the number of mouths or workers, and historians have long argued that it rewarded big families. A wallet paid for every child would do the same, which is why shares come from deaths.
- **Baumol's cost disease (1966). A string quartet takes the same four players and forty minutes it did in the 1820s, so live performance gets dearer next to everything machines make. In the Shade machines make everything.

## Method
- Flows: daily-life.md's 2-kW split over 8,766 hours a year.
- Shares: 6.7 t and ~0.75 TJ to build (habitat-design.md, energy-budget.md); floor at 50 kg/m² and ~25 kWh/kg; new metal 25–55 PJ from stars, 0.1 TJ–0.4 PJ from gas (matter-delivery.md).
- New shares per drum: ~200 M × growth rate (1.4 × 10⁻⁹ a year after spin-up, 4 × 10⁻⁹ during); new places at three-quarters of that.
- Places: people per place = L ÷ (L − A), with lifespan L ≈ 120 years and a place of one's own at A ≈ 30: 1.33, so 200 M ÷ 1.33 ≈ 150 M places of 35 × 1.33 ≈ 47 m² of home (people.md).
- The shareless: four wallets across five people ≈ 17.3 kWh a head; full-time work 3.1 kWh × 2,000 hours ÷ 365 ≈ 17 kWh a day.
- Births: population ÷ lifespan; share under 18 = 18 ÷ lifespan.
- The Hollow: 35 km² ÷ 25 m² (beach) or 100 m² (park), open 16 hours a day, ÷ ~200 M.
- Price of time: money income = 7,890 kWh ÷ (1 − s); spending on time = s × money income; price = spending ÷ (hours per adult × 85% adults).
- Heat pump: (350 ÷ 300 − 1) ÷ 0.5 of Carnot.
- The Dark: shortest unscheduled cut = 2 × the one-way light time at 0.08, 0.27 and 0.43 pc from the Lantern.
- Royalties: 10¹⁹ × 0.01 kWh ÷ 7,890 kWh per wallet-year; Earth at 8.2 × 10⁹ people.
- Minds: light per person × 0.98 × 0.324 ÷ 20 W.
- Freight: 1% of ½v² at 5 km/s, per kg.
