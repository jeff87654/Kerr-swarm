# Work: who works, at what, and for whom

*Design proposal from a working session, 10 Nov 2026. What the Shade's people do for a living when machines make everything: the tech level, the kinds of work that are left, who does how much of it, who employs them, and where they keep shop. Builds on economy.md (the dividend, the price of an hour, the levy, rent), people.md and families.md (the placeless, the fitting and what the placeless-born live on, free drums), governance.md (machines under standing rules; the minds on the Shade's compute), raft-habitat.md (the floor budget, the hull's two loops), sorrel.md and ashgrove.md (Sorrel's crews and trades), third-chair.md (a placeless-born working life) and open-drums.md (Chervil's dear hours). Jeff's decisions of 10 Nov 2026: the plant is automation under standing rules, and the minds on the Shade's compute are their own society; how much anyone works differs by the person, and the placeless work much the most; shopfronts are auctioned or sold; and a rule that the survival load be workable by hand is one drum's, which most drums don't follow. Proposals: that the drum is Sorrel and its rule dates from the Eleven Days; the three reasons a person does a job; the hiring year; every figure; and the names (a wallet's work, the watch, the hand rule).*

## Summary
- **Nothing needs a human hand.** The plant makes the food, air, goods, power and transport under standing rules, and every skill is in an archive. Work is what people will pay each other for, plus what a drum won't leave to the plant.
- **The plant isn't the minds.** The machines that make and route things are automation. The minds on the Shade's compute are their own society and out of focus: none holds a drum's post, and nothing in a drum is priced in a mind's hours.
- **A person does a job for one of three reasons:** somebody wants a person, the drum wants someone answerable, or the thing itself still moves (blights, disease, people). A fourth is local: Sorrel's hand rule.
- **Work differs by the person.** The mean stays economy.md's ~1,000 hours an adult, a 19-hour week. Behind it, about a quarter of adults sell no hours in a year and about a tenth sell more than a wallet's worth.
- **A wallet's work is ~2,550 hours a year:** seven hours a day, every day, at ~3 kWh an hour. It is what an adult with no share works to live as a neighbour with one does who works none.
- **The placeless work much the most.** In a free drum they are one adult in forty with no share: they work about a wallet's worth, sell one paid hour in sixteen and could fill three in ten of its paid posts. In a fitted drum, which is most drums, they are one adult in 400 with a waiting wallet, and work full time by choice.
- **Employers are households, houses, the drum, the raft and patrons.** There are no firms, since there is nothing to invest in and information earns no royalties. Posts and hirings run from one Booking Day to the next.
- **Shopfronts are the floor that can be bought.** A drum auctions their leases or sells them outright, ~3 m² a person. One on a neighbourhood arcade costs about a year of a wallet; one on the Quaysides, over twenty.
- **The market for hours is a metro wide,** ~1.6 × 10¹⁰ people. A drum can keep its posts for its own people; whether it can hold the price of a private hour is open.
- **Sorrel's hand rule.** Since the Eleven Days, whatever is on Sorrel's survival load can be worked by hand with the data halls dark, and its crews drill for it. Most drums leave that to the plant.

## The tech level
- **Mature and all but frozen.** The designs are older than any drum, and economy.md has the technology no longer improving. What's left is slow efficiency work (element-cycles.md's deep reprocessing, food-system.md's reactors): a line in the commons, not an industry. So there is no research sector, no new products and no growth to work toward.
- **Making is automatic.** The Mill and the Vats, the Yards, the cabs, the Glide, the rectennas and radiators run without a human hand, and robots do what hands could (economy.md § Work).
- **Knowing is archived.** Every design and skill is on file, and a tutor can teach any of it. Nobody has to know anything for the drum to run.
- **The plant isn't the minds (Jeff, 10 Nov 2026).** The machines that make and route things are automation under standing rules, as governance.md has the Lantern's. The minds that run on the Shade's compute are their own society (governance.md § Minds, machines and the settlement). So no drum's post is held by one, and nothing in a drum is priced in a mind's hours. A wallet buys compute at its kWh, and whatever answers, an assistant or a tutor, comes with it; who that is stays where governance.md leaves it.
- **What compute sets the price against.** A typical wallet's 9.6 kWh a day of compute is twenty brains' worth round the clock, at a brain's 20 W, and an hour of 20 W costs 0.02 kWh. The same resident buys ~2.3 hours a day of other people's time at ~3 kWh. So a person's hour sells at ~150 times that only where the person is the point, or a rule keeps the job for one.
- **Three things still move.**
  - *The vats' biology.* Phages and blights evolve, so strain libraries, the Gate and the Board's watch are work with stakes (food-system.md).
  - *Disease.* 200 M people share one air and ~45% glide out and back daily (people.md).
  - *People.* Law, quarrels, taste and what arrives by light from ~3 × 10¹³ rafts.
- **What no tech level buys** is economy.md's ceiling: more metal, more beam, more in-hull heat.

| | The plant does | People do |
|---|---|---|
| Food | The Mill, the Vats, the racks; the Mill Halls' cooking | The strain library, the Gate, the Board's watch; cooks; tea and blossom by hand, as luxuries |
| Air, water, heat, power | All of it | The watch; in Sorrel, crews who can run the survival load by hand |
| Things | The Yards and the fabricators | Design, fitting, mending, dealing in old things |
| Getting around | Cabs, the Glide, cyclers, routing | The Routing Room's and Stall Desk's watch; who decides what a crowded line costs |
| Bodies | Proofreading, diagnosis, the clinic's machines | Midwives, nurses, the clinician who answers for a decision |
| Learning | Tutors, archives, batch work | Schools, masters and apprentices, scholars, whoever sifts what arrives by light |
| Rule | The Lantern's standing rules; every ledger | Every office |

## Why a person does it
| Reason | Work | Paid by | In the docs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Somebody wants a person | Care, teaching, kitchens, craft and mending, performance, sport, dealing, company | Wallets | Anselm's shop, Marit's hall, Wren's flute teacher, Noon's nine cooks |
| The drum wants someone answerable | Offices and courts, the Register, the clinics, the watch over the plant | The levy, or unpaid by lot and election | Teo at the Gate, the Routing Room, the Board's watch room, the Small Court |
| The thing still moves | Strain libraries, phage watch, public health, scholarship | The levy, commissions, patrons | The Strain Library, the Batch Desk, Lightfall |
| The hand rule (Sorrel) | The Reserve, the air plant, the radiators, the jacket and moat | The levy | The Reserve's monthly drills, the Pump Shop, Hedda's crews |

- **Wanted.** economy.md's list stands: people pay for the person, as audiences still pay four players for a quartet. It is most of the hours, and it is a taste, so drums differ: Chervil spends ~40% of its wallets on people against the planning quarter (open-drums.md).
- **Answerable (the watch).** The plant can route ninety million rides an hour, but somebody a drum can name decides how dear a crowded line deck should be, holds or destroys a culture at the Gate, and signs a child into the Register. These are posts, and most drums keep them even where the plant would do the task better.
- **Still moving.** The one technical work that isn't keeping watch. It is small, and it is where a drum's expert knowledge lives in people.

## Who works, and how much
- **The mean is economy.md's.** A quarter of wallets spent on people and ~1,000 hours sold per adult give ~3.1 kWh an hour: 1.7 × 10¹¹ hours a year in a nation drum, ~85 M full-time equivalents, a 19-hour week averaged over every adult. It is about what the United States works per adult today, all of it delivered as service, and each resident buys ~2.3 hours of someone's time a day.
- **A wallet's work.** At that price a wallet (21.6 kWh a day) is ~2,550 hours a year: 49 hours a week, or seven hours every day. Full-time work at 2,000 hours earns ~17 kWh a day, four-fifths of one (economy.md).
- **It differs by the person (Jeff).** A planning spread that keeps the mean:

| | Of adults | Hours a year | Of all paid hours | Who |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sell no hours | ~25% | 0 | 0 | Most of the old who have gone up; parents of small children; people the dividend suits |
| Some | ~33% | ~500 | ~17% | A day or two a week, a season, a shop kept half-days, for an express leg or a sofa |
| A working life | ~31% | ~1,750 | ~54% | A post, a trade, a hall: Teo at the Gate, a cook, a teacher |
| More than a wallet's work | ~11% | ~2,650 | ~29% | A free drum's placeless; people buying a shopfront, a high deck or a right; people whose work is their life |

- **What a drum sells sets how hard it works.** Lovage auctions its high decks and Hollow hours and sells birth rights, so a wage buys standing there and people chase it. Sorrel allots them by age, so there is less to earn for, and rank comes from posts held and years served (ashgrove.md).
- **It follows the life, loosely.** The years between school and a place, 18 to the late 20s and ~17 M people a drum, are when most people work most, lodging where they work (people.md's servants in husbandry) and learning a trade from someone who has it. Householders with small children work least. In Sorrel a working life ends at the Up Hall, at about a hundred, and the old let their spare rooms mostly for hours rather than kays (sorrel.md).
- **Nobody with a wallet has to work** (economy.md), and in a fitted drum nearly every adult has one. What work buys is in economy.md's table of rich and poor: speed, light, new mass, other people's time, and standing.

## The placeless
- **They work much the most (Jeff).** How much depends on the drum, because what they live on does (families.md § What the placeless-born live on).
- **In a free drum** about one adult in forty is placeless, ~4 M people, nearly all placeless-born and shareless (people.md). The wage is their whole wallet, and the years after work have to be banked from it or paid into a house's box (families.md § Free drums). Planning takes them at a wallet's work each: ~6% of the drum's paid hours, 2.7 times everyone else's average, and ~5 M full-time equivalents, enough to fill three in ten of its ~18 M paid posts. Long service on a crew is one of the doors there.
- **In a fitted drum,** which is most drums, they are about one adult in 400, ~0.4 M people, and each holds a waiting wallet for life. Nothing forces them to work, and no work earns a placing, since the door is the lot alone. They still work more than their neighbours, for three reasons:
  - *No household to keep.* They raise no children, and those who chose a house live in a working body: a crew, a college, a hall guild (people.md).
  - *Nothing coming in later.* A householder grows old on the rooms of a place. A placeless-born elder has the wallet and no rooms to let (families.md § Old age).
  - *Something to buy.* A shopfront is floor they can own (Shopfronts).
- **Planning takes a fitted drum's placeless at full time,** ~2,000 hours, as Nell Varga works at Tansy's strain library (third-chair.md). That is twice the average adult's hours and half a percent of the drum's.
- **What they do.** people.md gives them a drum's offices of trust, except that the keepers of a draw are hired from another drum (families.md § Keepers from outside). The rest keep shops, serve in halls and households, and take the hard posts.
- **What it means.** In a free drum the steady, full-time work falls most on the people with the least stake in it: no place, no children, no share. In a fitted drum they are seven in a block of 3,000, and no drum's labour turns on them.
- **Service drums are the far end.** There the shareless pay for their own commons, and for their children, out of ~3,000 hours a year (families.md § Service drums).

## Who employs
- **Households.** A trade runs in a family: a bought shopfront can be left to a child, and Sorrel's Hours have kept theirs for generations, the crews in Wolf, the teachers in Lark, the cab engineers in Swift (sorrel.md). Young adults lodge and work in other households, and an old couple will let a room for hours of help.
- **Houses.** Crews, colleges, hall guilds and the medical guild (people.md) hold a craft, train it and elect into it. They are where a placeless career is made.
- **The block and the drum.** Posts paid from the levy, ~18 M full-time equivalents in a nation drum, about a fifth of all paid hours: teachers, nurses, stewards, clerks, the watch.
- **The raft.** The truss, the traffic, the cyclers, the data halls, the Yards and the beam contract (governance.md). It is the one career that takes a person far from home.
- **Patrons.** The rich buy people: cooks, tutors, tailors (economy.md). A household's retinue is display, as it was on Earth.
- **Partnerships.** A kitchen like Noon, with nine cooks and a terrace rent, is its partners' venture and shares its takings. Where a drum forbids interest, that is how one is funded (economy.md § Saving and interest).
- **No firms.** The beam and the metal are fixed, the plant is the commons', and information earns no royalties (economy.md). So nothing owns capital and hires labour to work it.
- **The hiring year is the booking year (proposal).** A raft learns its beam and a drum sets its levy on Booking Day, and a wallet can't be pledged past it. So posts are filled and servants and apprentices hired from one Booking Day to the next, with the hiring in the week before.

## Shopfronts
- **What one is.** Commons floor let or sold to a keeper for a trade: a shop, a kitchen, a workroom, a studio, a stall on the Quaysides.
- **How much.** Of each person's 100 m², 15 is workplace and 15 is schools, clinics, shops and halls (raft-habitat.md). Planning takes ~3 m² a person as shopfronts: ~600 km² in a nation drum, ~9,000 m² in a block of 3,000, which is 150–200 fronts.
- **Auctioned or sold (Jeff).** A drum auctions a shopfront's lease, with the rent going to the commons, or sells it outright, after which it is bought, sold, let and left like any belonging. Many do both.
- **What they cost (proposal).** A shopfront's rent is what its trade can bear. If two-fifths of private hours are sold from shopfronts and a keeper pays a tenth of the takings, the average is ~0.08 kWh per m² a day, a little under a room's (economy.md § Homes). Position moves it about fivefold either way, from a neighbourhood arcade to the Quaysides:

| A 40 m² shopfront | Rent, kWh a day | Bought outright | In wallet-years |
|---|---|---|---|
| On a neighbourhood arcade | ~0.6 | ~7,400 kWh | ~1 |
| Average | ~3 | ~37,000 kWh | ~5 |
| On the Quaysides or the Hollow's edge | ~15 | ~185,000 kWh | ~23 |

- **The one floor money can buy.** A place and a share can't be sold. A shopfront can, and it yields rent even where a drum forbids interest. In a drum that sells them, shopfronts are where savings go and the nearest thing in the Shade to capital, and a drum that sells its Quaysides gets shop landlords beside its room-letting elders.
- **Not a place.** A shopfront carries no allowance. A child born over the shop is born to whatever place its parents hold, or to none (families.md).
- **The placeless can own one.** A front on a neighbourhood arcade costs about what a year of a wallet's work earns, so a placeless-born cook or mender who saves for some years can keep shop under their own name.
- **A shop over which someone lives is nearly a home to pass on.** families.md's one limit on what a drum gives its placeless-born is that it can't look like a home to pass on, since that is the cue people have children on. A bought shopfront with a bed behind it comes close. In a fitted drum the implant makes that harmless. A free drum has to rule on it: nobody sleeps over the shop, or a front goes back to the drum at its keeper's death.
- **Houses hold them freely.** A shopfront a house holds costs no household its children, so there the old argument over mortmain (people.md) is about rents, not births.

## One market, a metro wide
- **Hours travel.** ~1.6 × 10¹⁰ people live within a free 20-minute glide (raft-transport.md), and Sorrel's Ring Six commutes to Chervil every morning (sorrel.md). So the price of a private hour tends to one price across the metro.
- **Posts don't.** What a drum pays for itself it can keep for its own people: its posts, its halls, its schools. That is how Chervil's custom of hiring its own first keeps an hour there at about twice Sorrel's (open-drums.md). Whether it can do the same for a tailor's hour, with Sorrel's tailors a glide away, is open.
- **Stars.** With a metro in reach, the hours of the most sought-after surgeon, teacher or player go by auction far above 3 kWh (economy.md § The price of an hour). A person's own time is the one road to a fortune that needs no property, and what it buys is still capped by economy.md's ceiling.

## Sorrel's hand rule
- **The rule (proposal: Sorrel's, since the Eleven Days).** Whatever is on Sorrel's survival load can be worked by hand with the data halls dark, and Sorrel keeps crews who have done it: the Reserve's fuel cells and banked oxygen, the air plant's scrubbers, the coolant pumps, the radiator field, the jacket and the moat.
- **It is one drum's rule (Jeff).** Most drums don't keep it. Their survival plant runs on its own local controls, and what breaks in a Dark waits for the beam. They still keep back crews (families.md; Ochre in wander-year.md), for the watch and for mending.
- **Why it isn't physics.** In a Dark a drum runs on 50–100 W a head and no compute (full-swarm.md), and the only general workers still running are people, fed from the larder. But a drum could as well bank a watt a head for its robots. The rule is a choice Sorrel made after eleven days of stairs and device light.
- **What it asks, behind the drum.** In a Dark the food plant stops, so the hot loop carries nothing, and an idle panel can cool to ammonia's 195 K freezing point within an hour or two. The room loop is left carrying ~31–41 GW of body heat and survival load on panels sized for 90, and unless more than half of it is shut in, the rooms lose about five degrees a day. So Sorrel's valves have wheels, its hot loop can be drained by hand, and its back crews have done both in drills.
- **What it has made of Sorrel.** The Reserve's crews drill every month, the Pump Shop rebuilds pumps by hand inside their old casings, and Wolf keeps the back, jacket and moat crews (sorrel.md). Hedda Dace was nineteen in the Eleven Days and has spent forty of the years since on the back crews (ashgrove.md).
- **What it costs.** A few tens of thousands of posts, out of the levy's ~18 M. The dose is the real price: back crews work under the ~1 Sv career cap, which is why their years count toward going up (sorrel.md).

## Earth anchors
- **Keynes's fifteen-hour week (1930).** In "Economic Possibilities for our Grandchildren" Keynes expected a century of growth to bring three-hour shifts and a fifteen-hour week, with work spread thin so that everyone had some. The Shade's planning mean is nineteen hours, and it is not spread thin.
- **Servants as display.** Where goods are cheap, rank shows in people. Britain taxed "non-essential" male servants (butlers, footmen, valets, grooms) from 1777, as a luxury of the rich. Baumol's quartet (economy.md) is the same point from the players' side.
- **Servants in husbandry.** In early modern England about three in five people aged 15–24 were servants, 13.4% of everyone in Kussmaul's parish listings: unmarried, hired by the year and lodged in the employer's household, between leaving home and a holding of their own. They were hired at yearly statute or mop fairs, around Michaelmas or Martinmas, for the coming year.
- **Burgage.** A plot in a medieval English borough was held for a money rent and could be sold, divided or left by will, when land outside the walls mostly could not. Towns were where a family's children without land went to live by a trade. A drum's shopfronts are its borough.
- **Parish offices.** About 50,000 parish officers were in post in England at any one time in the seventeenth century: churchwardens, constables, overseers. Goldie stresses how widely they were drawn; village studies mostly find the offices settling on a core of better-off householders who served again and again. A drum's posts will do the same unless the lot keeps them open.
- **Service for standing.** Rome's auxiliaries served twenty-five years for citizenship (families.md § Earth anchors). A free drum's crews offer the same door; a fitted drum's don't.
- **The dike watch.** Dutch water boards run some of the most instrumented flood defences on Earth and still train volunteers to walk the dikes at high water; a 1990s statute required every board to keep a dike-defence corps and drill it. Sorrel's hand rule is the same instinct.
- **Superstars.** Rosen (1981): when one performer can serve a whole market, small differences in talent earn very large differences in pay.

## Open questions
- How much of a wallet people spend on each other once compute can keep them company. The planning quarter is a taste, not a derivation; at a twentieth, work shrinks to posts and the placeless.
- Whether Chervil's dear hour can hold for anything but its posts and halls (One market, a metro wide).
- Who may buy a shopfront: residents only, as a borough's burgesses were, or anyone in the metro.
- Whether a keeper may live over the shop or leave it by will, in a free drum, where that is nearly a home to pass on (Shopfronts).
- How much a fitted drum's placeless really work, with a wallet for life and no door to earn. Planning takes full time.
- How drums fill their posts (by lot, election, examination or purchase), and how they keep offices from settling on a few families.
- Whether Sorrel's hand rule asks drill days of every adult or only of its crews.
- What the raft's own service looks like: its crews, who books its beam and who it answers to (governance.md § The raft and the orbit family). (deliberately open)

## Method
- **Hours** (economy.md): wallet 0.9 kW × 8,766 h = 7,890 kWh a year; money income 7,890 ÷ (1 − 0.25) = 10,520; spent on time 2,630; price 2,630 ÷ (1,000 h × 85% adults) = 3.09 kWh. Paid hours 170 M adults × 1,000 = 1.7 × 10¹¹, ÷ 2,000 = 85 M full-time equivalents; 1,000 ÷ 52.2 = 19.2 hours a week; 2,630 ÷ 3.09 ÷ 365 = 2.3 hours bought a day.
- **The United States:** ~153 M people at work in an average week of 2023, averaging 38.5 hours (BLS), is ~3.1 × 10¹¹ hours a year, ~1,150 for each of ~265 M adults.
- **A wallet's work:** 7,890 ÷ 3.09 = 2,550 hours; ÷ 52.2 = 49 a week; ÷ 365 = 7.0 a day.
- **The spread:** 0.25 × 0 + 0.33 × 500 + 0.31 × 1,750 + 0.11 × 2,650 = 999 hours; shares of hours 17%, 54% and 29%. The shares are a planning sketch, chosen to keep the mean.
- **The placeless, in a free drum:** 170 M ÷ 40 = 4.25 M adults × 2,550 h = 1.08 × 10¹⁰ hours, 6.4% of paid hours, 5.4 M full-time equivalents; everyone else averages (1.7 × 10¹¹ − 1.08 × 10¹⁰) ÷ 165.75 M = 960 hours. **In a fitted drum:** 170 M ÷ 400 = 0.43 M adults × 2,000 h = 8.5 × 10⁸ hours, 0.5% of paid hours. The shares of adults are people.md's.
- **Posts:** levy 7% × 7,890 kWh × 200 M = 1.1 × 10¹¹ kWh ÷ (2,000 h × 3.09) = 17.9 M full-time equivalents, 21% of paid hours; a free drum's placeless, 5.4 ÷ 17.9 = 30%.
- **Compute:** 9.6 kWh ÷ (20 W × 24 h) = 20; 3.09 ÷ 0.02 = 155.
- **Young adults:** ten years of age × 200 M ÷ 120 ≈ 17 M.
- **Shopfronts:** 3 m² × 200 M = 600 km², 3% of the floor. Private wages 2,630 kWh × 200 M × 79% = 4.2 × 10¹¹ kWh a year; two-fifths taken in shopfronts, a tenth of that paid as rent: 1.7 × 10¹⁰ kWh ÷ 6 × 10⁸ m² ÷ 365 = 0.076 kWh per m² a day. A neighbourhood arcade ÷ 5, the Quaysides × 5. Bought outright at economy.md's ~3% yield: a year's rent ÷ 0.03. A wallet-year is 7,890 kWh.
- **The back in a Dark** (raft-habitat.md's loops): the room loop sheds 327 W/m² × 275 km² = 90 GW at 283 K and the hot loop 605 W/m² × 115 km² = 70 GW at 330 K. A Dark's load is bodies at ~106 W plus the survival load's 50–100 W, × 200 M = 31–41 GW, which needs 35–46% of the room loop. An idle panel on other rafts' stray glow alone (10–20% of a 300 K panel's load, 41–83 W/m²) settles at 169–201 K; ammonia freezes at 195 K. A panel of 4–7 kJ/K per m² with no load cools from 330 K to 195 K in C ÷ (3εσ) × (195⁻³ − 330⁻³) ≈ 47–82 minutes. With no panel shut in, 54 GW of excess cooling against ~5 t a person of hull contents at 0.8 kJ/kg·K and 0.14 t of water is ~5 K a day. All rough.
