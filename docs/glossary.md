## The hole and the sky

### Sgr A*

The Milky Way's central black hole, ~4.3 × 10⁶ M☉. In the baseline it is fed at 30% of its Eddington luminosity, spun up to ~0.998, and held there as it grows. Nobody in the Shade ever sees it. See full-swarm.md, sgr-a-scope.md.

Matches: Sgr A*

### Eddington limit

The luminosity at which a hole's light pushes infalling gas out as hard as gravity pulls it in. The civilization runs its hole at 30% of it: the top of the disk's steady window, and enough for the Lantern to hold itself up. Throttling below that share lowers the Lantern's lift, so the feed grows with the hole. See sgr-a-scope.md, full-swarm.md.

Matches: Eddington

### The Lantern

A closed shell of hovering collectors ~0.82 pc from the hole that catches ~98% of the disk's light and turns ~32% of it into electricity (~2.75 kWe for each person in the Shade), all of it beamed out by microwave. It is held up mainly by infrared trapped inside it, weighs ~50,000 M☉, carries the fastest messages, remakes its own electronics (which live in ~8–20 MGy a year, so no compute runs there), and nobody lives on it. Seen from the Shade it's a black disk 85–130° across that glows in the infrared. See full-swarm.md, governance.md.

Matches: Lantern

### The Shade

The inhabited zone outside the Lantern, ~0.9–1.25 pc from the hole: ~1.87 × 10³³ people living the 2 kWe life, in rafts on crossing orbits that take ~35,000–39,000 years. It weighs ~9.3 × 10⁶ M☉, ~2.2× the hole, and ~6.3 × 10⁶ M☉ of that is heavy elements, 6.7 t a person. See full-swarm.md, daily-life.md.

Matches: Shade

### The gap

The space between the Lantern and the Shade, which every beam crosses. Anything light enough (watchers, relays, screens, weapons) can hover in it on the Lantern's glow, so it is natural contested ground. See governance.md.

Matches: none

### The glow

The infrared the Lantern's skin can't help giving off, ~70% of the disk's light: ~1.4 kW/m² on average, ~3.6 over the poles, almost nothing over the disk plane. Glow sails run on it. See governance.md.

Matches: the glow

### The frontier

Mining outposts in the nuclear stellar disk, 100–200 pc out, and for growth in the inner bulge out to ~0.4 kpc: centuries to ~1,300 years of light-delay away. They supply the Shade's metal. Outposts can live on their own stars' light, so neither core nor frontier can coerce the other. See matter-delivery.md, governance.md.

Matches: frontier

### The great year

A calendar unit of 39,000 years, about one orbit of the Shade (orbits take ~35,000–39,000 years, a little less toward the outer edge). Nothing in the sky marks it: orbits are kept round, so a raft's distance from the Lantern changes by under a percent, and nobody feels it pass. See full-swarm.md.

Matches: great year

### Orbit family

Rafts on nearly the same orbit, which stay neighbours while everything else passes at 100–300 km/s. It is the Shade's natural region; the Lantern's patches are its only fixed geography. See governance.md.

Matches: orbit family

### Rules of the road

Traffic rules that keep rafts' orbits nearly round (free eccentricity under ~0.003, not counting the ~0.001–0.003 the Lantern's glow forces on inclined orbits), cutting collision courses ~10×. A miss between rafts releases 10³³–10³⁶ J. They also make every raft keep a steady prograde push (~4–5 × 10⁻¹² m/s²), so it climbs as the hole grows instead of sinking into the Lantern. See full-swarm.md, governance.md.

Matches: rules of the road

## Habitats

### Raft

A flat platform of drums standing side by side, axes toward the Lantern: rectennas in front, radiators behind and the mid-raft between. Rafts run from ~0.1 AU (~6 × 10¹⁹ people) to a ~1.6 AU cap (~1.5 × 10²²), laid out at ~3 m² per person. See raft-transport.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: raft, rafts

### Deck drum

The standard habitat: a squat spinning cylinder, 1 g at the rim and 0.5 g at its innermost deck, with concentric decks every 4 m and a 50 m hydrogen jacket. About 6 t of heavy elements per person at any size. Sizes run from the town drum (0.5 km, ~0.9 M people) to the world drum (10 km, ~7 B). See habitat-design.md.

Matches: deck drum, town drum, world drum

### Nation drum

The baseline deck drum: 3 km in radius, 3.75 km long, ~375 decks and ~200 M people, turning once every 110 s. It was 2 km and ~60 M people until Oct 2026. Each owns a ~24.5 km square cell of raft. See habitat-design.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: nation drum, nation drums

### The Keel

A nation drum's rim decks (1–15, ~1 g): food plant, water works, methanol tank farm, survival reserve, recyclers, and the station rings where travellers leave. Nobody lives there, since the rim is the least shielded. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Keel

### Low, Middle and High decks

The drum's residential bands. Low decks (0.98–0.85 g) hold families, schools and visitors; Middle decks (0.85–0.6 g) most homes and work; High decks (0.6–0.5 g), with a low dose (the Middle decks' is lowest) and nearest the Hollow, are elders' flats and the prestige address. "Money lives up; the way out is down." See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Low decks, Middle decks, High decks

### The Hollow

The open core of a drum, inside half its radius: in a nation drum, 3 km across and 3.75 km long, a park and civic heart with a 0.5 g floor of ~35 km² (0.18 m² a person) and the far bank 3 km overhead. Hollow hours are rationed or sold. See raft-habitat.md, economy.md.

Matches: Hollow

### Sunline

The lamp along a drum's axis that lights the Hollow: full-sun brightness for ~6 hours around midday on a 2 kWe budget, ramping over 3 hours either side. It stops ~450 m short of each end wall so people can fly near the axis by day. See raft-habitat.md, daily-life.md.

Matches: sunline

### The Spindle

The drum's axis, where within ~75 m you weigh under 3% of a g: flying country. By day people fly only in the flying halls at its ends; the rest opens in the lamp-night. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Spindle

### Flying halls

The netted ends of the Spindle, within 75 m of the axis and out to ~300 m from each end wall, where people can fly while the sunline is lit. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: flying hall, flying halls

### Station rings

Platforms around the Keel's rim deck, one under each of the ~9 levels of glide lanes, where cabs hand travellers to glide cars. See raft-habitat.md, drum-transport.md.

Matches: station ring, station rings, Station Ring

### The Quaysides

The blocks around the station rings, the busiest in a drum: markets, freight, and quarantine for anything biological, since in a drum fed by monoculture vats a phage is a famine. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Quaysides

### Hydrogen jacket

The drum's cosmic-ray shield: ~50 m of liquid hydrogen at 20 K (~3.5 t/m²) over hull and end caps, with ~100 m more over the front cap in the Shade against the corona's MeV tail: ~3.2 t per person in a nation drum. Behind it, the moat and the decks, residents take ~1 mSv/yr. Cryopanels in its guard vacuum freeze anything the hull leaks, so nothing vents. See habitat-design.md, element-cycles.md.

Matches: hydrogen jacket

### The moat

About 0.5 m of the drum's own water (~51 g/cm²) held outside the jacket over its sides and front cap, two-thirds of its water. It keeps the Lantern's hard X-rays from heating the 20 K jacket and replaced the iron parasol. See habitat-design.md.

Matches: moat

### Heliox

The drums' air: 21 kPa of oxygen in ~80 kPa of helium. Sound runs ~1.8× faster, voices and wind instruments sound higher, and helium chills skin, so habitats run warm. See habitat-design.md.

Matches: heliox

### Rectenna

A receiving antenna that turns the Lantern's microwave beam back into electricity, at ~90%. A raft's front layer is a field of them, ~2.3 m² per person at 2 kWe. See full-swarm.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: rectenna, rectennas

### Mid-raft

The 3.75 km deep layer between a raft's rectennas and radiators, where the drums stand, along with the truss, power lines, glide lanes, tubes, data halls and Yards. It is flooded with the Lantern's hard X-rays, lethal to an unshielded person within minutes. See raft-habitat.md, raft-transport.md.

Matches: mid-raft

### Data halls

Water-walled vaults in the mid-raft that run all of a raft's compute, interactive and batch, on beamed power: the Lantern is months to years away by light, and its electronics live in ~8–20 MGy a year. See full-swarm.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: data halls, data hall

### The Yards

A raft's industry and recycling, in the mid-raft (~58 GW per nation drum's cell), radiating from hotter panels than the hull's. See raft-habitat.md, element-cycles.md.

Matches: Yards

## Getting around

### The Lattice

Travel inside a drum: six-seat cabs with gimballed cabins, one party each, that run radially in lift cores and horizontally on line decks, door to door with no changes. A random trip takes ~6 minutes on average and ~12 at worst. See drum-transport.md.

Matches: Lattice

### Line decks

Fifteen decks, one in every 25, that carry the Lattice's horizontal tubes. See drum-transport.md.

Matches: line deck, line decks

### The Glide

Free travel between neighbouring drums: a car let go from one drum's rim coasts along a shared tangent line and is caught by the next drum's rim at exactly its speed. For nation drums, 140 s a hop at 174 m/s. It needs almost no power, so it runs through the Dark. See raft-transport.md.

Matches: Glide

### Quay

The band round each drum's waist, outside the jacket and moat, where cradles let glide cars go and catch them. See raft-transport.md, habitat-design.md.

Matches: quay, quays

### Cyclers

Sleeper trains that never stop, looping on trunk tubes across a raft at up to ~20 km/s; passengers board and leave by capsule. Fast cyclers are also a raft's biggest store of energy, braked a little at a time to stretch a beam cut. See raft-transport.md.

Matches: cycler, cyclers

## Energy and money

### Common budget

A comfortable common life: ~3.1 kWe per person delivered (~1.0 in the habitat, ~0.5 for travel, ~1.6 remote), which would take ~13.4 kW of the disk's light in the full swarm, enough for ~1.21 × 10³³ people. It is a reference budget. The Shade's people live daily-life.md's leaner 2 kWe life, ~8.7 kW of light each, which sets the population (~1.87 × 10³³) and the raft layout. See energy-budget.md, daily-life.md.

Matches: common budget

### kWh

The money: a claim on a raft's beam, booked a year ahead. One currency on every raft, no exchange rates; prices run highest at lamp-noon and lowest in the lamp-night. See economy.md, daily-life.md.

Matches: kWh

### The commons and the wallet

How a person's ~2 kWe splits. The commons (~1.1 kW) is the drum's budget, spent in kind on air, food, light and services; the wallet (~0.9 kW) is each person's own, paid as an equal beam dividend on each share and topped up by work. See daily-life.md, economy.md.

Matches: commons, wallet, wallets

### The place

Household land, like a farm, passed whole to one heir: one household's home of ~78 m², about 2¼ people's share, so a nation drum of 200 M has ~90 M places. Children and young adults live in a parent's or grandparent's place; an heir usually inherits a great-grandparent's place in their late 20s, and a spouse marries in. Children belong to a place: couples have them once they have one. It can't be sold or pledged, but its rooms can be let. See economy.md, people.md.

Matches: none

### The share

Each person's claim on their drum: the wallet (~21.6 kWh a day) on top of the commons that everyone gets, with ~6.7 t of habitat and ~3 m² of the raft's back behind it. One per person at par. A death frees a share, and in most drums it passes within the family: first to the longest-waiting shareless member, otherwise to the next child born, who is often named for the dead. Most children are born before their share frees and carry it from a few years old; a household with more children than shares lives thinner. The shareless get the commons and live on family wallets and work; most shareless adults are placeless-born. See economy.md, people.md.

Matches: shareless

### Birth rights

In pool drums, each share carries one birth right, so a couple has two children; a nation drum gains a new share only about once every three and a half years. A right unspent at its holder's death returns to the pool and is reissued by lottery to couples who have spent theirs (proposal). A child born without a right is the pool drums' placeless-born. Whether rights can be sold is the sharpest choice in the setting. Most drums use holdings instead. See economy.md, people.md.

Matches: birth right, birth rights

### Holdings

The common way drums hold births to deaths: places and shares pass in the family. A couple's last death frees their place for one heir, usually a great-grandchild, and each death frees a share for a newborn; a child born ahead of a death has no share until one frees; and couples have children once they have a place, so a drum's extra children become the placeless rather than more households. Nobody waits on a death to have a child. A household has two children by right and a third by leave, and a child born outside that allowance is placeless-born. See people.md, economy.md, families.md.

Matches: holdings

### The posted price

What most steady drums charge a household for the keep of a child born outside its allowance, from the Mill-table credit (~0.23 kW, a tenth of a wallet) up to the child's whole ~1.1 kW of commons, posted at every station ring and charged until the child is grown or placed. It keeps the cost of a household's choice off its neighbours, as an English parish's bastardy bond did. It doesn't hold a drum's numbers; who is born to a place does that. Rung 1 of the counted-drum ladder, and not counting. Open drums post none. See people.md, economy.md, families.md.

Matches: posted price, the keep

### The draw

A lottery for third children (proposal): each year a drum issues as many permits as it has room it hasn't given another way, and draws them among couples with two children and none born outside the allowance; a drawn third pays nothing. Nearly every couple enters, and one in eight to sixteen ever wins. A couple across the line enters the same draw, and a win places the partner born outside. It is the way of giving leave under which every household has the same chance to lose, and the one most old drums use; pool drums run theirs from unspent rights. See families.md, people.md.

Matches: Draw Day, third-child draw, draw for thirds

### Born to a place

A child born inside its household's allowance: two children by right and a third by leave (proposal). It can inherit a place, be named heir to one or marry into one, and its own first two children are born to a place in turn. Leave is issued only as fast as the childless and the early dead leave room, about one birth in eighteen, so a drum's numbers are held by standing and not by a price. See families.md, people.md.

Matches: born to a place

### Leave

What a third child needs (proposal). The heirless give it by naming an heir to their place; the rest of a drum's room goes by the draw, by sale or, in open drums, by the block's leave. About one family in eight is given a third. See families.md.

Matches: by leave, the block's leave

### The placeless-born

People born outside a household's allowance (proposal): registered, proofread, fed and schooled like anyone, but with no claim on a place and, in most drums, last in the family's line for a share. They are placed, and count from then on as born to a place, by adoption into an heirless place, by a marriage the drum allows, by long service, or by a later place when their parents' place comes. Every placing spends the room a third child would have had, so about one in ten is placed. About one person in forty in a steady drum. With no share, they work for ~80% of a wallet from eighteen, and old age rests on a house's box, kin or savings. Not the wild-born, who are born outside the clinics. See families.md, people.md, third-chair.md.

Matches: placeless-born

### The line outside

The children of the placeless-born, and theirs (proposal). The placeless-born have no place and no draw to lose, so nothing holds them to an allowance. Most have no children, but where the few who do become a people apart, their line can take a tenth of a drum's births in ~500–1,500 years. Drums keep them mixed in, place their children, charge a keep or fit them. See families.md.

Matches: the line outside, line outside

### Portions

In portion drums (proposal), the place passes whole to one heir and the other children each carry a portion of the family's savings, belongings and rents to their marriage, and heirs marry the best portion going, as Irish farmers married fortunes. A third child shrinks its siblings' portions, which decides who carries the cost of a big family but barely slows the wish. See families.md.

Matches: portion drum, portion drums

### Split estates

Drums (proposal) where a household's place and shares are one estate, split equally among the children as each marries, with a minimum a couple must hold to have children, as nineteenth-century Württemberg made the poor prove their means to marry. Rare and short-lived: the minimum creeps down and the numbers swing. See families.md.

Matches: split estate, split estates, split-estate drum, split-estate drums

### Sibling households

Drums (proposal) where a place stays with its sibling group for life, partners visit and never move, and children belong to their mother's place, as among the Mosuo. Uncles raise their sisters' children, and when a generation outgrows the place the excess leave for houses. See families.md.

Matches: sibling household, sibling households

### Room drums

Holdings drums that count rented rooms as places (Jeff, 6 Nov 2026), so a couple without a place can raise children born to one: young and crowded, with rents two to four times a steady drum's and a renting class, running well above par and reaching the Count sooner than their neighbours. Other drums ban such births, with varying severity, or frown on them: the child is placeless-born, and grows up poorer. See families.md.

Matches: room drum, room drums

### The drum's own children

A rare rule (proposal): children drawn by lot from every family's banked embryos to replace a drum's childless and early dead, carried by volunteers and raised by them or by tables. In a young Shade it was the one rule under which wanting more children gained a family nothing; now it takes the gift of a third out of every family's hands. See families.md.

Matches: drum's own children

### The placeless

Adults who never head a household: mostly the placeless-born, with the few born to a place whom no place or marriage reached, and people who choose a house instead. They can marry, foster, teach and keep offices of trust, but raise no children unless a place comes to them. About one adult in forty in a steady drum; many more in a drum that has loosened its line. See people.md.

Matches: placeless

### Houses

Homes for the placeless held by bodies that don't die (orders, crews, colleges, hall guilds), entered by election, with no heir and no nursery. Every place a house holds is one fewer household with children, so what houses may hold is a drum's quiet birth policy. About one place in fifty houses every placeless-born adult, and a drum with that many sits near par, not ~2.7% over. A house keeps a box. See people.md, economy.md, families.md.

Matches: none

### Waiting shares

Shares that are unheld between a death and the child they wait for (proposal): about 3% of a drum's shares at any time, ~6 M in a nation drum, as many as there are children waiting for a share. In most drums their dividends (~5.4 GW) fall to the commons. A few, Chervil among them, lend them to placeless-born elders as waiting wallets, each held until the share's child is born. See families.md, economy.md.

Matches: waiting share, waiting shares, waiting wallet, waiting wallets

### The box

A house's common fund (proposal): members pay in about fifteen parts in a hundred of their pay while they work and draw ~4 kWh a day once they stop, as guilds and friendly societies kept a box. For someone with no share and no rooms to let, it is most of old age. See families.md, third-chair.md.

Matches: none

### The certificate

What a drum writes for one of its people who lodges in another (proposal): the home drum answers for their commons and for any child born to them there. Standing is settled by birth, so a host drum's door and its leave are for its own. See families.md.

Matches: none

### Marrying late

A second marriage between someone born to a place, whose children already have households, and a placeless-born partner (proposal). It costs a line nothing, so it is how most marriages across the line happen, and it ends with a year's grace in the place when the holder dies. See families.md, third-chair.md.

Matches: marrying late

### Third chair

Tansy's word for a child born outside its household's allowance; a household with one is carrying a chair. See third-chair.md.

Matches: third chair, third chairs

### Proofreading

The Shade-wide norm, not a law: every embryo is sequenced and its new mutations are put back to match its parents, along with known disease variants. It clears mutation load and keeps the Shade one species. See people.md.

Matches: proofreading, proofread, unproofread

### The wild-born

Children conceived and carried outside the clinics: unproofread, unregistered, often shareless. Some are lawbreakers' children, some refusers'. See people.md.

Matches: wild-born

### Counted drums

Drums that license births and enforce it (rung 3 and above of people.md's ladder; a keep or a penalty is not counting), about 1% of drums at a quiet time and many more after a raft-wide shock. A drum counts when it has to shrink, since people will wait for a third child and won't wait for a first. Most fit a contraceptive implant at fourteen (the fitting) and stop births for a decade or two; the darkest are rare and shunned. Named for the Count, the board at every station ring showing people against shares. See people.md.

Matches: counted drum, counted drums, the Count, the fitting

### The right of the Glide

The norm on most rafts that anyone may glide out of any drum. It keeps most counted drums mild. See people.md.

Matches: right of the Glide

### Open drums

Drums that hold births to deaths without coercion and keep their rings open both ways, about a quarter of drums at a quiet time. They run a Margin under par, post a Ledger, leave the giving of third children to their blocks, and spend more of their wallets on each other's time. See open-drums.md.

Matches: open drum, open drums

### The Margin

The 2–4% of an open drum's shares it keeps unused on purpose, with the places that go with them held by the drum as guest flats, refuge, first homes lent to young couples for up to five years (by lottery for the best flats) and homes for life lent to placeless couples. It isn't spent on births except by supermajority after a disaster. See open-drums.md.

Matches: the Margin, Margin flat, Margin flats

### The Ledger

An open drum's board at every station ring: shares, people, the Margin, places, births, deaths, guests, each newborn's name beside the dead whose share they carry, and each new householder beside the dead whose place they took. The open counterpart of the Count. See open-drums.md.

Matches: the Ledger

### Guest-right

A compact among open drums, joined by most steady drums, that any traveller may stay three nights in a Margin flat and eat at the halls. With cheap cyclers it makes the wander year possible. See open-drums.md.

Matches: guest-right, wander year

### The Dark

A beam cut. The raft drops to a survival load and, without glow sails, lasts ~55 days (~90 if its tank farm is full). Light delay means a cut can't be called off in time, so it is a sentence whose length is set when it's ordered, not a siege. See governance.md, economy.md.

Matches: Dark

### The Burn

Focusing the Lantern's beam to destroy a raft outright. Nothing in the hardware need forbid it, unless the patches were built as independent sub-arrays that can't focus tighter than a raft's beacons; otherwise it is held back by horror, as the one crime against all that unites the other rafts. See governance.md.

Matches: Burn

### Survival reserve

What carries a drum through the Dark: ~50–100 W per person of air handling, from fuel cells burning shield hydrogen with ~60 kg of banked oxygen each, for ~55 days, until the cabin air's oxygen margin runs out. A full tank farm's oxygen, breathed instead of burned, stretches that to ~90 days, when the food runs out. See full-swarm.md, food-system.md.

Matches: survival reserve

### Glow sails

Heat engines run on the Lantern's glow, stowed and deployed when the beam stops. Away from the disk plane they carry a raft on rations (~0.4 kW per person) for as long as a cut lasts; whether rafts may carry them is the setting's right to bear arms. See governance.md.

Matches: glow sail, glow sails

### Dim years

Long dims of the disk, when the hole's flicker outlasts the methanol tank farm and power is rationed. The farm carries a 20% dip for a month on ~95 kg of methanol a person, with its own liquid oxygen to burn it in and tanks that capture its CO₂, so nothing vents. See full-swarm.md.

Matches: dim years

## Food

### The Mill

Abiotic chemistry, the first food tier: electrolysis and methanol synthesis feeding enzyme cascades and chain growth that make starch, sugar and fat (~45–48% of the electricity ends as food energy at planning values) and amino acids. About two-thirds of calories. See food-system.md.

Matches: Mill

### The Vats

The second food tier: microbes, fungi and cell culture fed by the Mill, making protein, texture, dairy, egg, meat and flavour. About a third of calories. See food-system.md.

Matches: Vats

### The Garden

The third food tier: LED-lit plants (and any animals) for crisp produce, herbs and whole foods. About 2% of calories but ~17% of food power. See food-system.md.

Matches: Garden

### Mill table and Vat table

The two everyday cuisines. A Mill-table day (Mill foods, ferments, amino acids, the herb pot) costs ~5.5 kWh and is served free from the commons; a Vat-table day adds the Vats' texture and flavour (dairy, egg, gluten, meat) and ~100 g of produce for ~7.75 kWh. See cuisine.md.

Matches: Mill table, Vat table

### The Nine

A seasoning of the nine essential amino acids, set on every Mill table, their bitterness masked with salt and glutamate. "Have some Nine" is said to anyone who looks peaky. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: the Nine

### The Crosswise Guide

The Herb Row Eating Society's restaurant guide. It rates places in apple-seed stars, after the star of seeds in an apple cut crosswise: one is worth the glide, two worth a cycler, three worth going dark for. The Ladle marks the best commons food. See eating-out.md.

Matches: Crosswise Guide, Crosswise

### Waste-heat cooking

Slow cooking on the food plant's free 65 °C loop: onsen-style custards, amazake, black garlic, broths held below a simmer. Searing and frying draw power; holding costs nothing. See eating-out.md.

Matches: waste-heat cooking

## Earlier eras

### Ring era

The swarm's first layout: habitats on rigid rings around the starved Sgr A* before it was fed, ~10²⁵ people at ~18 AU on 13-day orbits, catching the hole's natural ~230 L☉ at ~37%. It ended when feeding began, since the feed goes in at 4–40 AU. Rings hold only while orbits take less than ~16 years. The worked ring designs in swarm-rings.md and ring-weave.md are a design study for a 10 M☉ hole, where that caps a swarm at ~3 × 10²⁴ people. See hole-choice.md, full-swarm.md.

Matches: ring era

### Orange slice

The ring era's layout: rings tilted one band-width (~0.6°) apart around a shared axis, like the segments of an orange, all crossing at the axis's two ends (the knots). swarm-rings.md works it out for a 10 M☉ hole, as a design study. See hole-choice.md, swarm-rings.md.

Matches: orange slice

### Knot nights

In a ring layout, the nights a habitat spends in the shadow of the ring inside it. In the orange slice every part of a ring passes through a knot twice an orbit, and each night lasts about a sixth of an orbit. Around the starved Sgr A*, on 13-day orbits, they were short: hours while the rings were sparse, and about two days mid-band once they filled the sky at ~10²⁵ people. In the 10 M☉ design study they run 12–17 days at 10²². The full swarm has no nights. See hole-choice.md, energy-budget.md, ring-weave.md.

Matches: knot night, knot nights

### Ring weave

An alternative to the orange slice in the 10 M☉ design study: ring planes tilted evenly in all directions, no two closer than ~7°, which cuts the worst night to under a day at the cost of ~27% of the light. See ring-weave.md.

Matches: ring weave, the weave

### X-ray tiles

The 10 M☉ design study's collectors: a silicon-carbide skin and an iron plate driving Stirling engines, turning a starved stellar hole's X-rays into electricity at ~22%. See xray-power.md.

Matches: X-ray tile, X-ray tiles

### Layer era

The era while feeding is under way (from a hot flow to ~3–8% of Eddington), when drums fly on crossing orbits in a warm layer and rafts carry their own collectors. It ends when a closed Lantern can hold itself up. See sgr-a-scope.md, full-swarm.md.

Matches: layer era

## Herb Row and Ashgrove

### Ashgrove

A neighbourhood of ~3,000 people on decks 73–78 of Sorrel, home of the Dace family in the narrative sketch. Its middle is the Court, an atrium six decks tall. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Ashgrove

### Sorrel

A nation drum of 200 million on Herb Row of Merrow. It allots its high decks and Hollow hours by age, and its birth rights can be given but not sold. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Sorrel

### Lovage

Sorrel's neighbour, one glide away. It auctions its high decks and Hollow hours, spends the rents on a bigger commons (a sunline at full brightness for twelve hours), and lets birth rights be sold. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Lovage

### Rue

A counted drum across the rows from Sorrel, nine years into a birth stop after a blight hit its overspent households. See people.md, ashgrove.md.

Matches: Rue

### Chervil

An open drum across the rows from Sorrel on Herb Row, 3% under par since a birth stop ~340 years ago. See open-drums.md.

Matches: Chervil

### Tansy

A steady drum one glide from Sorrel along Herb Row, known for its strawberries, the longest strain library on the Row, and the Row's only henhouse. A drawn-holdings drum: its houses hold about one place in fifty, and its draw is kept by keepers from other drums. Home of Nell Varga. See eating-out.md, third-chair.md.

Matches: Tansy

### The Emptying

Tansy's dissolution of its houses about nine hundred years ago: room for ~3.7 M children at once, forty-two years of leave, and ~4 M people put into lodgings. Tansy ran 2.7% over par for three centuries and took four more to found its houses again. See third-chair.md, families.md.

Matches: the Emptying

### Yarrow and Borage

Drums further along Herb Row: Yarrow a maker drum (home of Nine, a Mill-only tasting menu), Borage a fair drum (home of Lightfall, which cooks recipes received by laser). See eating-out.md.

Matches: Yarrow, Borage

### Merrow

A 0.1 AU raft of ~6 × 10¹⁹ people in the middle of the Shade, home to Sorrel. After the Eleven Days it ran its main cycler trunks fast, with slow lines beside them. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Merrow

### The Eleven Days

A beam gap within living memory in Merrow, officially from a failed patch, which the Lantern's daily hand-offs should have covered within hours; what really caused it is left open. Afterwards the raft ran its main cycler trunks fast as a store of energy, with slow lines beside them. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Eleven Days

### Knotnight

A yearly festival kept in Sorrel, Chervil and other drums: one evening when the lamps go down and a neighbourhood makes the year's larder together, as if a long dark were coming. It remembers the layer era, when a raft could sit in other rafts' shadow for weeks. Its name comes from the ring era's knot nights, which were short. See ashgrove.md, open-drums.md.

Matches: Knotnight

### The Window

Galleries behind the drum, outside the jacket, glazed with ~1.5 m of water, where every Sorrel child sees the stars once. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: the Window

### Selkie

Spelt's yearly mother starter, built from the spoons of starter that wanderers leave in its crock room and registered with the strain library. Every wanderer gets a heel of it for the road. See wander-year.md.

## Inside Sorrel

### Rings and Hours

Sorrel's address system: deck, Ring and Hour ("seventy-seven, Three, Bee"). Nine Rings run along the axis, each named for the station ring beneath it, from Ring One at the power end to Ring Nine at the heat end. Twelve Hours go round, 30° wedges counted spinward and named for animals nobody in Sorrel has seen, from Hare to Lark; each faces its opposite across the Hollow. See sorrel.md.

Matches: none

### The Round

The twelve Hours' yearly race right round deck 201 of Sorrel, on the Round Arcade: the Heavy Round run spinward and the Light Round antispinward. Stag wins the Heavy and Hare the Light. See sorrel.md.

Matches: the Round, Heavy Round, Light Round

### Down Halls

Sorrel's funeral halls in the Keel, one per Ring. In Sorrel someone who has retired has "gone up" and someone who has died has "gone down": the dead ride down to a Down Hall, then to the oxidisers and back into the cycles. See sorrel.md, element-cycles.md.

Matches: Down Hall, Down Halls

### The Summer

Sorrel's only season, about 1,100 years ago: forty days when a fault in the back cap's rotary joint left the drum three or four degrees warm. Summer Day is kept with cold food. See sorrel.md.

Matches: the Summer, Summer Day

### Register of Places

Sorrel's record of every place, share and name, unbroken for about 23,000 years. The old Count board hangs in its hall. See sorrel.md.

Matches: Register of Places

### Bench Island

The pavilion that comes up through the bed of Sorrel's Hollow lake, with the jetty and the elders' bench. See sorrel.md.

Matches: Bench Island
