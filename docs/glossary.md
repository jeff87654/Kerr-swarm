# Glossary

*Short definitions of the setting's named things, for quick reference. The docs they point to are the canon; where a definition here and a doc disagree, the doc wins and this page should be fixed. Most names are proposals (see each doc's notes). On the website, each term's first mention on a page links here, and each entry lists the docs that use it.*

*Format, for editing: each entry is a `###` heading, a definition, and an optional "Matches:" line listing the exact words the website links (comma-separated; entries with no capital letters match any case; "none" turns linking off). Without one, the heading is matched, minus a leading "The".*

## The hole and the sky

### Sgr A*

The Milky Way's central black hole, ~4.3 × 10⁶ M☉. In the baseline it is fed at 30% of its Eddington luminosity, spun up to ~0.998, and held there as it grows. Nobody in the Shade ever sees it. See full-swarm.md, sgr-a-scope.md.

Matches: Sgr A*

### Eddington limit

The luminosity at which a hole's light pushes infalling gas out as hard as gravity pulls it in. The civilization runs its hole at 30% of it: the top of the disk's steady window, and enough for the Lantern to hold itself up. Throttling below that share lowers the Lantern's lift, so the feed grows with the hole. See sgr-a-scope.md, full-swarm.md.

Matches: Eddington

### The Lantern

A closed shell of hovering collectors ~0.82 pc from the hole that catches ~98% of the disk's light and turns ~32% of it into electricity, beamed out by microwave. It is held up mainly by infrared trapped inside it, weighs ~50,000 M☉, runs the batch compute and carries the fastest messages, and nobody lives on it. Seen from the Shade it's a black disk 85–130° across that glows in the infrared. See full-swarm.md, governance.md.

Matches: Lantern

### The Shade

The inhabited zone outside the Lantern, ~0.9–1.25 pc from the hole: ~1.27 × 10³³ people in rafts on crossing orbits that take ~39,000 years. Its mass is ~1.3× the hole's. See full-swarm.md.

Matches: Shade

### The gap

The space between the Lantern and the Shade, which every beam crosses. Anything light enough (watchers, relays, screens, weapons) can hover in it on the Lantern's glow, so it is natural contested ground. See governance.md.

Matches: none

### The glow

The infrared the Lantern's skin can't help giving off, ~74% of the disk's light: ~1.5 kW/m² on average, ~4 over the poles, almost nothing over the disk plane. Glow sails run on it. See governance.md.

Matches: the glow

### The frontier

Mining outposts in the nuclear stellar disk, 100–200 pc out and centuries of light-delay away, which supply the metal for growth. Outposts can live on their own stars' light, so neither core nor frontier can coerce the other. See matter-delivery.md, governance.md.

Matches: frontier

### The great year

One orbit of the Shade, ~39,000 years. Over it an eccentric raft drifts between the warmer inner edge and the outer edge, and a polar orbit crosses the disk plane twice. See full-swarm.md.

Matches: great year

### Orbit family

Rafts on nearly the same orbit, which stay neighbours while everything else passes at 100–300 km/s. It is the Shade's natural region; the Lantern's patches are its only fixed geography. See governance.md.

Matches: orbit family

### Rules of the road

Traffic rules that keep rafts' orbits nearly round (eccentricity under ~0.003), cutting collision courses ~10×. A miss between rafts releases 10³³–10³⁶ J. See full-swarm.md, governance.md.

Matches: rules of the road

## Habitats

### Raft

A flat platform of drums standing side by side, axes toward the Lantern: rectennas in front, radiators behind and the mid-raft between. Rafts run from ~0.1 AU (~6 × 10¹⁹ people) to a ~1.6 AU cap (~1.5 × 10²²), laid out at ~3 m² per person. See raft-transport.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: raft, rafts

### Deck drum

The standard habitat: a squat spinning cylinder, 1 g at the rim and 0.5 g at its innermost deck, with concentric decks every 4 m and a 50 m hydrogen jacket. About 6 t of heavy elements per person at any size. Sizes run from the town drum (0.5 km, ~0.9 M people) to the world drum (10 km, ~7 B). See habitat-design.md.

Matches: deck drum, town drum, world drum

### Nation drum

The baseline deck drum: 3 km in radius, 3.75 km long, ~375 decks and ~200 M people, turning once every 110 s. It was 2 km and ~60 M people until Oct 2026. Each owns a ~24.5 km square cell of raft. See habitat-design.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: nation drum, nation drums

### The Keel

A nation drum's rim decks (1–15, ~1 g): food plant, water works, methanol tank farm, survival reserve, recyclers, and the station rings where travellers leave. Nobody lives there, since the rim is the least shielded. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Keel

### Low, Middle and High decks

The drum's residential bands. Low decks (0.98–0.85 g) hold families, schools and visitors; Middle decks (0.85–0.6 g) most homes and work; High decks (0.6–0.5 g), with the lowest dose and nearest the Hollow, are elders' flats and the prestige address. "Money lives up; the way out is down." See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Low decks, Middle decks, High decks

### The Hollow

The open core of a drum, inside half its radius: in a nation drum, 3 km across and 3.75 km long, a park and civic heart with a 0.5 g floor of ~35 km² (0.18 m² a person) and the far bank 3 km overhead. Hollow hours are rationed or sold. See raft-habitat.md, economy.md.

Matches: Hollow

### Sunline

The lamp along a drum's axis that lights the Hollow: full-sun brightness for ~6 hours around midday on a 2 kWe budget, ramping over 3 hours either side. It stops ~450 m short of each end wall so people can fly near the axis by day. See raft-habitat.md, daily-life.md.

Matches: sunline

### The Spindle

The drum's axis, where within ~75 m you weigh under 3% of a g: flying country. By day people fly only in the flying halls at its ends; the rest opens in the lamp-night. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Spindle

### Flying halls

The netted ends of the Spindle, within 75 m of the axis and out to ~300 m from each end wall, where people can fly while the sunline is lit. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: flying hall, flying halls

### Station rings

Platforms around the Keel's rim deck, one under each of the ~9 levels of glide lanes, where cabs hand travellers to glide cars. See raft-habitat.md, drum-transport.md.

Matches: station ring, station rings, Station Ring

### The Quaysides

The blocks around the station rings, the busiest in a drum: markets, freight, and quarantine for anything biological, since in a drum fed by monoculture vats a phage is a famine. See raft-habitat.md.

Matches: Quaysides

### Hydrogen jacket

The drum's cosmic-ray shield: ~50 m of liquid hydrogen at 20 K (~3.5 t/m²) over hull and end caps, ~2.2 t per person in a nation drum. Cryopanels in its guard vacuum freeze anything the hull leaks, so nothing vents. See habitat-design.md, element-cycles.md.

Matches: hydrogen jacket

### The moat

About 0.5 m of the drum's own water (~51 g/cm²) held outside the jacket over its sides and front cap, two-thirds of its water. It keeps the Lantern's hard X-rays from heating the 20 K jacket and replaced the iron parasol. See habitat-design.md.

Matches: moat

### Heliox

The drums' air: 21 kPa of oxygen in ~80 kPa of helium. Sound runs ~1.8× faster, voices and wind instruments sound higher, and helium chills skin, so habitats run warm. See habitat-design.md.

Matches: heliox

### Rectenna

A receiving antenna that turns the Lantern's microwave beam back into electricity, at ~90%. A raft's front layer is a field of them, ~2 m² per person at 2 kWe. See full-swarm.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: rectenna, rectennas

### Mid-raft

The 3.75 km deep layer between a raft's rectennas and radiators, where the drums stand, along with the truss, power lines, glide lanes, tubes, data halls and Yards. It is flooded with the Lantern's hard X-rays, lethal to an unshielded person within minutes. See raft-habitat.md, raft-transport.md.

Matches: mid-raft

### Data halls

Water-walled vaults in the mid-raft that run a raft's interactive compute on beamed power, since the Lantern is months to years away by light. See full-swarm.md, raft-habitat.md.

Matches: data halls, data hall

### The Yards

A raft's industry and recycling, in the mid-raft (~58 GW per nation drum's cell), radiating from hotter panels than the hull's. See raft-habitat.md, element-cycles.md.

Matches: Yards

## Getting around

### The Lattice

Travel inside a drum: six-seat cabs with gimballed cabins, one party each, that run radially in lift cores and horizontally on line decks, door to door with no changes. A random trip takes ~6 minutes on average and ~12 at worst. See drum-transport.md.

Matches: Lattice

### Line decks

Fifteen decks, one in every 25, that carry the Lattice's horizontal tubes. See drum-transport.md.

Matches: line deck, line decks

### The Glide

Free travel between neighbouring drums: a car let go from one drum's rim coasts along a shared tangent line and is caught by the next drum's rim at exactly its speed. For nation drums, 140 s a hop at 174 m/s. It needs almost no power, so it runs through the Dark. See raft-transport.md.

Matches: Glide

### Quay

The band round each drum's waist, outside the jacket and moat, where cradles let glide cars go and catch them. See raft-transport.md, habitat-design.md.

Matches: quay, quays

### Cyclers

Sleeper trains that never stop, looping on trunk tubes across a raft at up to ~20 km/s; passengers board and leave by capsule. Fast cyclers are also a raft's biggest store of energy, braked a little at a time to stretch a beam cut. See raft-transport.md.

Matches: cycler, cyclers

## Energy and money

### Common budget

A comfortable common life: ~3.1 kWe per person delivered (~1.0 in the habitat, ~0.5 for travel, ~1.6 remote), which takes ~12.8 kW of the disk's light in the full swarm. daily-life.md works through a leaner 2 kWe life, which sets the raft layout. See energy-budget.md.

Matches: common budget

### kWh

The money: a claim on a raft's beam, booked a year ahead. One currency on every raft, no exchange rates; prices run highest at lamp-noon and lowest in the lamp-night. See economy.md, daily-life.md.

Matches: kWh

### The commons and the wallet

How a person's ~2 kWe splits. The commons (~1.1 kW) is the drum's budget, spent in kind on air, food, light and services; the wallet (~0.9 kW) is each person's own, paid as an equal beam dividend on each share and topped up by work. See daily-life.md, economy.md.

Matches: commons, wallet, wallets

### The place

Household land, like a farm: ~47 m² of home, about 1⅓ people's share, so a nation drum of 200 M has ~150 M places. Children and young adults live in a parent's or grandparent's place; most people get their own around 30, usually a great-grandparent's. It can't be sold or pledged, but its rooms can be let. See economy.md, people.md.

Matches: none

### The share

Each person's claim on their drum: the wallet (~21.6 kWh a day) on top of the commons that everyone gets, with ~6.7 t of habitat and ~3 m² of the raft's back behind it. One per person at par. A death frees a share, and in most drums it passes to a newborn in the family, who is often named for the dead. A child born ahead of a death has none until one frees: the shareless get the commons and live on family wallets and work. See economy.md, people.md.

Matches: shareless

### Birth rights

In pool drums, each share carries one birth right, so a couple has two children; a nation drum gains a new share only about once every three and a half years. Whether rights can be sold is the sharpest choice in the setting. Most drums use holdings instead. See economy.md, people.md.

Matches: birth right, birth rights

### Holdings

The common way drums hold births to deaths: places and shares pass in the family. A death frees a place for a young adult, usually a great-grandchild, and a share for a newborn; a child born ahead of a death has no share until one frees; and people marry when they hold places, around 30. See people.md, economy.md.

Matches: holdings

### Proofreading

The Shade-wide norm, not a law: every embryo is sequenced and its new mutations are put back to match its parents, along with known disease variants. It clears mutation load and keeps the Shade one species. See people.md.

Matches: proofreading, proofread, unproofread

### The wild-born

Children conceived and carried outside the clinics: unproofread, unregistered, often shareless. Some are lawbreakers' children, some refusers'. See people.md.

Matches: wild-born

### Counted drums

Drums that license births and enforce it, about 1% of drums at a quiet time and many more after a raft-wide shock. Most fit a contraceptive implant at fourteen (the fitting) and stop births for a decade or two; the darkest are rare and shunned. Named for the Count, the board at every station ring showing people against shares. See people.md.

Matches: counted drum, counted drums, the Count, the fitting

### The right of the Glide

The norm on most rafts that anyone may glide out of any drum. It keeps most counted drums mild. See people.md.

Matches: right of the Glide

### Open drums

Drums that hold births to deaths without coercion and keep their rings open both ways, about a quarter of drums at a quiet time. They run a Margin under par, post a Ledger, and spend more of their wallets on each other's time. See open-drums.md.

Matches: open drum, open drums

### The Margin

The 2–4% of an open drum's shares it keeps unused on purpose, with the places that go with them held by the drum as guest flats, refuge and first homes lent to young couples until they inherit. It isn't spent on births except by supermajority after a disaster. See open-drums.md.

Matches: the Margin, Margin flat, Margin flats

### The Ledger

An open drum's board at every station ring: shares, people, the Margin, places, births, deaths, guests, and each newborn's name beside the dead whose share they carry. The open counterpart of the Count. See open-drums.md.

Matches: the Ledger

### Guest-right

A compact among open drums, joined by most steady drums, that any traveller may stay three nights in a Margin flat and eat at the halls. With cheap cyclers it makes the wander year possible. See open-drums.md.

Matches: guest-right, wander year

### The Dark

A beam cut. The raft drops to a survival load and, without glow sails, lasts ~55 days. Light delay means a cut can't be called off in time, so it is a sentence whose length is set when it's ordered, not a siege. See governance.md, economy.md.

Matches: Dark

### The Burn

Focusing the Lantern's beam to destroy a raft outright. Nothing in the hardware forbids it; it is held back by horror, as the one crime against all that unites the other rafts. See governance.md.

Matches: Burn

### Survival reserve

What carries a drum through the Dark: ~50–100 W per person of air handling, from fuel cells burning shield hydrogen with ~35 kg of banked oxygen each, for ~55 days. See full-swarm.md.

Matches: survival reserve

### Glow sails

Heat engines run on the Lantern's glow, stowed and deployed when the beam stops. Away from the disk plane they carry a raft on rations (~0.4 kW per person) for as long as a cut lasts; whether rafts may carry them is the setting's right to bear arms. See governance.md.

Matches: glow sail, glow sails

### Dim years

Long dims of the disk, when the hole's flicker outlasts the methanol tank farm and power is rationed. See full-swarm.md.

Matches: dim years

## Food

### The Mill

Abiotic chemistry, the first food tier: electrolysis and methanol synthesis feeding enzyme cascades and chain growth that make starch, sugar and fat (~45–48% of the electricity ends as food energy at planning values) and amino acids. About two-thirds of calories. See food-system.md.

Matches: Mill

### The Vats

The second food tier: microbes, fungi and cell culture fed by the Mill, making protein, texture, dairy, egg, meat and flavour. About a third of calories. See food-system.md.

Matches: Vats

### The Garden

The third food tier: LED-lit plants (and any animals) for crisp produce, herbs and whole foods. About 2% of calories but ~17% of food power. See food-system.md.

Matches: Garden

### Mill table and Vat table

The two everyday cuisines. A Mill-table day (Mill foods, ferments, amino acids, the herb pot) costs ~5.5 kWh and is served free from the commons; a Vat-table day adds the Vats' texture and flavour (dairy, egg, gluten, meat) and ~100 g of produce for ~7.75 kWh. See cuisine.md.

Matches: Mill table, Vat table

### The Nine

A seasoning of the nine essential amino acids, set on every Mill table, their bitterness masked with salt and glutamate. "Have some Nine" is said to anyone who looks peaky. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: the Nine

### The Crosswise Guide

The Herb Row Eating Society's restaurant guide. It rates places in apple-seed stars, after the star of seeds in an apple cut crosswise: one is worth the glide, two worth a cycler, three worth going dark for. The Ladle marks the best commons food. See eating-out.md.

Matches: Crosswise Guide, Crosswise

### Waste-heat cooking

Slow cooking on the food plant's free 65 °C loop: onsen-style custards, amazake, black garlic, broths held below a simmer. Searing and frying draw power; holding costs nothing. See eating-out.md.

Matches: waste-heat cooking

## Earlier eras

### Ring era

The first layout, around a starved hole: habitats on rigid rings orbiting it. Rings hold only while orbits take less than ~16 years, which caps a ring swarm at ~3 × 10²⁴ people around 10 M☉. See swarm-rings.md.

Matches: ring era

### Orange slice

The ring-era layout: rings tilted one band-width (~0.6°) apart around a shared axis, like the segments of an orange, all crossing at the axis's two ends (the knots). See swarm-rings.md.

Matches: orange slice

### Knot nights

In the ring era, the nights a habitat spends in the shadow of the ring inside it. In the orange slice every part of a ring passes through a knot twice an orbit; around a 10 M☉ hole at 10²² people the nights run 12–17 days. The full swarm has no nights. See energy-budget.md, ring-weave.md.

Matches: knot night, knot nights

### Ring weave

An alternative to the orange slice: ring planes tilted evenly in all directions, no two closer than ~7°, which cuts the worst night to under a day at the cost of ~27% of the light. See ring-weave.md.

Matches: ring weave, the weave

### X-ray tiles

The ring era's collectors: a silicon-carbide skin and an iron plate driving Stirling engines, turning a starved stellar hole's X-rays into electricity at ~22%. See xray-power.md.

Matches: X-ray tile, X-ray tiles

### Layer era

The era while feeding is under way (from a hot flow to ~3–8% of Eddington), when drums fly on crossing orbits in a warm layer and rafts carry their own collectors. It ends when a closed Lantern can hold itself up. See sgr-a-scope.md, full-swarm.md.

Matches: layer era

## Herb Row and Ashgrove

### Ashgrove

A neighbourhood of ~3,000 people on decks 73–78 of Sorrel, home of the Dace family in the narrative sketch. Its middle is the Court, an atrium six decks tall. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Ashgrove

### Sorrel

A nation drum of 200 million on Herb Row of Merrow. It allots its high decks and Hollow hours by age, and its birth rights can be given but not sold. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Sorrel

### Lovage

Sorrel's neighbour, one glide away. It auctions its high decks and Hollow hours, spends the rents on a bigger commons (a sunline at full brightness for twelve hours), and lets birth rights be sold. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Lovage

### Rue

A counted drum across the rows from Sorrel, nine years into a birth stop after a blight hit its overspent households. See people.md, ashgrove.md.

Matches: Rue

### Chervil

An open drum across the rows from Sorrel on Herb Row, 3% under par since a birth stop ~340 years ago. See open-drums.md.

Matches: Chervil

### Tansy

A steady drum one glide from Sorrel along Herb Row, known for its strawberries, the longest strain library on the Row, and the Row's only henhouse. See eating-out.md.

Matches: Tansy

### Yarrow and Borage

Drums further along Herb Row: Yarrow a maker drum (home of Nine, a Mill-only tasting menu), Borage a fair drum (home of Lightfall, which cooks recipes received by laser). See eating-out.md.

Matches: Yarrow, Borage

### Merrow

A 0.1 AU raft of ~6 × 10¹⁹ people in the middle of the Shade, home to Sorrel. After the Eleven Days it ran its cyclers fast. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Merrow

### The Eleven Days

A beam gap within living memory in Merrow, after which the raft ran its cyclers fast as a store of energy. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: Eleven Days

### The Window

Galleries behind the drum, outside the jacket, where every Sorrel child sees the stars once. See ashgrove.md.

Matches: the Window

## Across Merrow

### The Passing

A festival held while another raft passes close to Merrow: choirs and kitchens trade songs and recipes with it across the light delay. A raft comes within ~2 AU about once in four years. See wander-year.md.

Matches: the Passing

### Selkie

A raft that passed Merrow at ~2 AU, 16.6 light-minutes away, during Marit Vell's wander year. See wander-year.md.

Matches: Selkie

### The raft loaf

Spelt's yearly mother starter, built from the spoons of starter that wanderers leave in its crock room and registered with the strain library. Every wanderer gets a heel of it for the road. See wander-year.md.

Matches: raft loaf

### Sloe and Kittiwake

Drums on Merrow's Hedge and Gull Rows: Sloe a wood drum of silent woods, where people carry slates; Kittiwake a maker drum that makes wing-sleeves and teaches flying across the axis. See wander-year.md.

Matches: Sloe, Kittiwake

### Gloaming and Squall

Neighbours on Merrow's Weather Row: Gloaming a fair drum that paints dusks and returns its terrace rents; Squall a steady drum with a wave-making swell on its Hollow lake. See wander-year.md.

Matches: Gloaming, Squall

### Carillon and Dulse

Hall drums across Merrow: Carillon on Bell Row, a drum of choirs; Dulse on Kelp Row, on the far side, which pulls noodles on third-g terraces. See wander-year.md.

Matches: Carillon, Dulse

### Madder and Ochre

Neighbours on Merrow's Pigment Row, which grinds colours for the raft: Madder a counted drum that has just kept a Margin and gone open; Ochre a steady drum whose back crews take a guest for a shift. See wander-year.md.

Matches: Madder, Ochre

### Spelt

A maker drum on Merrow's Grain Row, home of Pell the baker and the raft loaf. See wander-year.md.

Matches: Spelt

## Inside Sorrel

### Rings and Hours

Sorrel's address system: deck, Ring and Hour ("seventy-seven, Three, Bee"). Nine Rings run along the axis, each named for the station ring beneath it, from Ring One at the power end to Ring Nine at the heat end. Twelve Hours go round, 30° wedges counted spinward and named for animals nobody in Sorrel has seen, from Hare to Lark; each faces its opposite across the Hollow. See sorrel.md.

Matches: none

### The Round

The twelve Hours' yearly race right round deck 201 of Sorrel, on the Round Arcade: the Heavy Round run spinward and the Light Round antispinward. Stag wins the Heavy and Hare the Light. See sorrel.md.

Matches: the Round, Heavy Round, Light Round

### Down Halls

Sorrel's funeral halls in the Keel, one per Ring. In Sorrel someone who has retired has "gone up" and someone who has died has "gone down": the dead ride down to a Down Hall, then to the oxidisers and back into the cycles. See sorrel.md, element-cycles.md.

Matches: Down Hall, Down Halls

### The Summer

Sorrel's only season, about 1,100 years ago: forty days when a fault in the back cap's rotary joint left the drum three or four degrees warm. Summer Day is kept with cold food. See sorrel.md.

Matches: the Summer, Summer Day

### Register of Places

Sorrel's record of every place, heir and name, unbroken for about 23,000 years. The old Count board hangs in its hall. See sorrel.md.

Matches: Register of Places

### Bench Island

The pavilion that comes up through the bed of Sorrel's Hollow lake, with the jetty and the elders' bench. See sorrel.md.

Matches: Bench Island
