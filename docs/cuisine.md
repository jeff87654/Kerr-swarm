# Cuisine: the Mill table and the Vat table

*Design notes from a working session, Sept 2026. Builds on food-system.md (tiers, planning efficiencies, per-serving table), energy-budget.md (knot nights in the ring era) and full-swarm.md (the mature swarm, which has no nights). Prices use "extraordinary effort" efficiencies (what a civilization of up to ~10³³ people could reach by pouring effort into food chemistry), counted per calorie eaten. Item and meal costs are production energy; day totals are all-in (5% waste, 25 W air/waste loops, 30 W kitchens), as in food-system.md.*

## Summary
- At extraordinary effort the Mill reaches ~67% (sugar, starch, fat) and ~55% (amino acids); the best Vats ~45% (~35% per calorie eaten); the Garden ~5%.
- A **Mill-table** day (Mill, ferments, amino acids, the herb pot) costs **5.5 kWh all-in (230 W)**. A **Vat-table** day (Mill, the best Vats, ~100 g of produce) costs **7.75 kWh (323 W)**. food-system.md's common day re-prices from 10.9 to ~8.0 kWh.
- The Vats don't sell nutrition: Mill amino acids are cheaper protein than anything living cells make. They sell texture and flavor, closing three of the Mill table's four gaps (fiber, elastic dough, egg). Juicy crunch stays in the Garden.
- The Vat table's biggest bill is the precision line (dairy, egg, gluten), not meat. Ribosome reactors would cut it by ~0.7 kWh/day.
- Food is ~7–10% of the common energy budget and ~25–35% of the lean one.
- Culture hooks: knot night is a winter in a ~5 × 10²² ring swarm, while the full swarm has no night at all, only the threat of a beam cut; a blight is a line-by-line fallback to the Mill table; ethanol is nearly free, and the bootlegger's alternative is the food plant's methanol.

## Pricing rules
- **Energy only counts grams.** A day's food is about 500 g of dry matter. Vitamins, aromas, spice molecules, colors, enzymes and starter cultures add up to well under a gram, so they cost almost nothing whichever tier makes them. Gums and emulsifiers, at a few grams a day, cost a watt or two. The same applies to the Garden: 5 g of fresh herbs is about 1 W, while a 150 g side salad is about 25 W (40 W at planning values). In lean times the salad goes dark, but the garnish stays; the herb pot is the last of the Garden to go.
- **Fermentation is nearly free.** The ~50% "living-cell tax" is the cost of growing cells with oxygen. Yeast turning sugar into ethanol, or lactic bacteria turning it into lactic acid, keep about 95% of the sugar's energy in the product. Leavening, brewing, souring and cultured funk are Mill-grade processes.
- **Water and air are free.** All the energy is in the dry matter. Broths, gels (a starch jelly is ~90% water) and foams make a bowl big and filling for almost nothing.
- **Heat is the other cost.** Boiling a liter from the vats' 65 °C waste-heat tap takes 0.045 kWh (0.10 kWh from cold). A 3 L pot held at a rolling boil for ten minutes is ~0.3 kWh, about a third of what 100 g of noodles cost. Lids, pressure cookers and one-pot methods win. Low, slow steps (holding broths, proofing, fermenting at 40–70 °C) run on waste heat; searing and frying draw power.
- **Accounting note.** Protein's food energy (4 kcal/g) is only ~70% of its combustion energy, because the nitrogen leaves the body as urea. Microbial yields are normally quoted on combustion energy, so food-system.md's protein-heavy Vat lines are 25–30% optimistic per calorie eaten: roughly +35 W on the common diet and +15 W on the ration. No ranking changes. Everything here is per calorie eaten.

## What extraordinary effort buys
| Tier | Planning (food-system.md) | Extraordinary effort | Rough ceiling | What sets the ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mill: sugar, starch, fat | 45–48% | 65–70% | 90%+ | Only engineering. Building glucose from CO₂ and water takes barely more energy than the glucose holds |
| Mill: amino acids | ~35% (estimate) | ~55% | 85%+ | More steps than sugar; the nitrogen comes back almost free from urea |
| Best Vats (methylotroph protein) | 35% | ~45% (~35% per kcal eaten) | ~55% | Cells burn much of their feed to build themselves |
| Garden | 3% | ~5% | ~8% | About 10 photons per CO₂ fixed; the plant then respires about a third of what it fixes |

- The tiers get closer but don't merge. A Garden calorie still costs ~13× a Mill calorie; the best Vat calorie ~1.5× (1.9× per calorie eaten).
- The bigger payoff is scope. Roughly in order of difficulty, the Mill could learn to make:
  1. **Amino acids in the right handedness.** Chemistry makes mirror-image mixtures; people need the L forms. Ajinomoto made MSG by straight chemical synthesis in the 1960s, and immobilized enzymes have separated amino-acid mirror forms industrially since 1969. This alone makes a Mill-only diet nutritionally complete.
  2. **Fibers, gums and emulsifiers.** Enzymes build amylose and cellulose from sugar phosphates (cellulose was first made in a flask in 1991). Fructan fibers come from sucrose; polydextrose from glucose, sorbitol and citric acid. Methylcellulose and HPMC (modified cellulose) are the binders in plant-based burgers and gluten-free bread. Mono- and diglycerides and sucrose esters are fat plus glycerol or sugar.
  3. **The essential fats.** Linoleic and α-linolenic acid by precise chemical synthesis. DHA is needed only by the half-gram, so the Vats supply it for under 1 W.
  4. **Periodic proteins.** The Mill makes a short peptide and enzymes string copies into a chain. Collagen-like repeats give gelatin (gummies, aspic, marshmallow); elastin-like repeats give spring; silk-like repeats give a crystalline chew. Enzymes already form peptide bonds industrially: thermolysin makes aspartame.
  5. **Ribosome reactors (cell-free protein synthesis).** The big one. Translation spends four high-energy phosphate bonds per peptide bond, under a tenth of the protein's energy, so a specific protein could cost ~20% more than its amino acids (~46% overall) instead of the several-fold premium living cells charge. The obstacle is lifetime: today's cell-free systems make less than their own weight in protein before they stall, and they need to make dozens of times their weight. Solved, casein, egg white, gluten and muscle proteins become Mill-grade, and most of the Vat-grade kitchen list moves into the Mill.
  6. **Osmotic produce (speculative).** Millimeter cells with regenerated-cellulose walls (dialysis-tubing material), filled with ~10% sugar, acids and aroma. Osmosis swells each to ~0.7 MPa, in the range of plant turgor, so a block would crunch and burst like fruit. Spherified alginate "caviar" is a crude ancestor. In the Shade this is a recent invention, served as "orchard pearls" in Lovage (eating-out.md).
- What stays expensive after all that is living structure (marbling, whole fish, bone-in cuts, a real apple) and provenance. In the limit the tiers stop deciding what food can exist and start deciding where it came from: vanilla versus vanillin.

## The Mill table
**Pantry**
- Starch in any amylose ratio and digestibility: waxy for mochi and sticky rice, high-amylose for glass noodles, slow and resistant grades for blood sugar and fiber.
- Every sugar, plus caramels and flavored "palm sugar" and "molasses".
- Fats made to spec: butter, ghee, lard- and tallow-style (for flaky pastry and frying), cocoa butter, olive- and toasted-sesame-style, and the essential fats.
- All twenty amino acids; fibers, gums and emulsifiers; acids and salts.
- A flavorist's whole library: capsaicin, sanshool's tingle, menthol, wasabi's allyl isothiocyanate, smoke phenols, roast pyrazines, meaty sulfur compounds (from cysteine plus ribose), cheese acids, jasmine rice's 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline.
- Colors, vitamins, and cultures and enzymes as reusable catalysts.

**How it cooks**
- **Starch cuisines are the template.** Cassava and sago cultures already eat a near-pure starch staple with a small, intense relish: Moluccan papeda, West African fufu, Brazilian farofa, the tapioca crepe. The Mill inherits their toolkit and their classic failure: when the relish is thin, protein runs short.
- **Protein is a seasoning.**
  - The nonessential amino acids (glycine, alanine, proline, serine, glutamate) taste sweet-savory; they're much of why scallops and crab taste the way they do. Cooks use ~40 g a day of them freely in broths, glazes and sweets.
  - The nine essential amino acids are mostly bitter, so they go on at the table, after cooking. Adding them late also keeps lysine from browning away in the Maillard reaction. Kitchen proverb: "don't brown your lysine."
  - That makes a mandatory condiment, **the Nine**: ~20 g a day, masked with salt, glutamate and fat. It's a paste or a fat-coated sprinkle, because leucine and tryptophan barely dissolve, with dipeptides for the stubborn ones (TPN uses the same trick with glycyl-tyrosine and alanyl-glutamine).
  - Functionally, the Nine is a synthetic fish sauce. Real fish sauce is free amino acids in brine, and it has long counted toward the protein supply in parts of Southeast Asia.
- **Crunch comes from oil and sugar:** krupuk-style puffed crackers, crisped starch crumbs, brittles, sugar glass.
- **Sour, funk and rise come from microbes, nearly free:** yeast-raised steamed cakes (Vietnamese bánh bò), sour-fermented starch that puffs in the oven (Brazil's polvilho azedo), lacto-fermented jellies, cheese funk from lipase acting on Mill butterfat.
- **Spice is a molecule rack.** Cooks work like perfumers, in parts per million, and recipes travel as formulas.
- **Texture:** superb bounce, jiggle, shatter, cream and fluff (the Taiwanese "QQ" of tapioca pearls and mochi, which East and Southeast Asian snack cultures already prize); no fiber, juicy crunch, elastic dough or egg.

**Dishes**
- **Staples:** extruded Mill "rice" (made like today's fortified-rice kernels, tuned sticky to fluffy, with jasmine aroma); glass noodles and crystal-dumpling wrappers; tapioca crepes; stretchy starch "swallows" for stews; soft starch-and-HPMC loaves and flatbreads; sour-starch cheese-bread puffs.
- **Savory:** noodle soups in sweet-savory broth with meaty reaction flavor; fat-rich curries and palm-nut-style stews over rice or swallows; cold starch jellies (Chinese liangfen, Korean acorn jelly) dressed with vinegar, chili and garlic; sesame "tofu" (goma-dofu is just kudzu starch and sesame); fried jelly cubes in broth, agedashi-style; chili crisp.
- **Sweet, the Mill's glory:** Karachi halwa, lokum, mochi, kuih lapis; cendol-style jellies in coconut-style cream; toffee, caramel, fudge, pulled sugar, compound chocolate; non-dairy ice cream; methylcellulose foams in place of meringue.
- **Drinks:** amazake-style sweet starch drinks, molecular "coffee" and "tea", sodas, lactic sours, hard-seltzer-style beer, flavored spirits.

**Precedent**
- W.C. Rose identified the essential amino acids in the 1940s–50s by keeping graduate students on starch, sugar, fat, vitamins, minerals and purified amino acids.
- NASA-funded trials in the 1960s kept volunteers on a fully chemical diet for months; that work became Vivonex.
- Amino-acid infant formulas feed allergic infants exclusively, and home TPN patients live on purified nutrients for decades.
- The cautionary tale is pellagra. The Southern sharecropper's "three M" diet (fatback, meal, molasses) was fat, starch and sugar, and it killed on the order of 100,000 Americans in the early 1900s.

## The Vat table
Two points frame it:
- **The Vats close three of the Mill table's four gaps.** Mycelium supplies fiber, precision gluten supplies elastic dough, and precision egg proteins supply egg. Juicy crunch stays in the Garden.
- **The Vats don't sell nutrition.** At these efficiencies, Mill amino acids (55%) are cheaper protein than anything living cells make (20–35%). The Vats sell texture and flavor.

| Line | What it gives the kitchen | Planning (food-system.md) | Best case, per kcal eaten | × Mill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mill: starch, sugar, fat | Bulk calories | 45–48% | 67% | 1 |
| Brewing and souring | Alcohol, lactic sours, leavening | — | 64% | 1.05 |
| Mill amino acids | The Nine, sweet-savory stocks | ~35% | 55% | 1.2 |
| Methylotroph bacteria | Protein flour: curds, surimi, extruded "chicken", protein-boosted noodles | 35% | 35%¹ | 1.9 |
| Plant cells, dark algae | Cocoa, coffee, tea, vanilla, fruit purées; greens and pigments | 20% | 30% | 2.2 |
| Filamentous fungi | Whole-cut chew (steaks, bacon, pulled strands); koji | 25% | 27% | 2.5 |
| Precision yeast | Casein, whey, egg proteins, gelatin, gluten, heme | 15% | 20%² | 3.4 |
| Animal cells | Cultivated fat and meat | 10% | 20% | 3.4 |
| Garden / orchard | Produce and herbs / tree fruit | 3% / 1.5% | 5% / 2.5% | 13 / 27 |

¹ 45% on a combustion basis. ² About 46% if ribosome reactors work. The oil vats shrink to a DHA line (~0.5 g a day, under 1 W), since the Mill now makes linoleic and α-linolenic acid.

**How it cooks**
- **Protein becomes an ingredient again.** Whole protein isn't bitter, so protein flour replaces the Nine at the table, for ~0.3 kWh a day more than eating the Nine: the price of not tasting it. The flour goes into tofu-style curds, extruded "chicken", protein-boosted noodles and crackers, and surimi (kamaboko, fish balls, crab sticks), which is already an engineered gel of protein and starch. The Nine retires to the blight shelf.
- **Gluten is the precision protein that matters most.** It turns Mill starch into open-crumb bread, croissants, hand-pulled noodles and chewy dumpling wrappers. Flour is only ~12% protein, so bread costs about 1.3× a Mill cracker per calorie. Nobody makes gluten this way today, but it's a plausible Vat line.
- **Casein, egg proteins and gelatin do what gums can't:** melt and stretch (pizza), foams that set in the oven (meringue, soufflé, angel food cake), custard, and gelatin's melt at mouth temperature (panna cotta, gummies). Rennet is already made this way: fermentation-produced chymosin was approved in 1990 and curdles most American cheese.
- **Mycelium brings the chew:** sliceable steaks, bacon, pulled strands. Quorn grows Fusarium; Meati grows Neurospora. Koji brings enzymes: miso, soy sauce, amazake, shio-koji marinades, koji-aged "charcuterie".
- **Meat is a hybrid, and a species is a fat.** Meat science has long found that species flavor lives mostly in the fat. A few grams of cultivated pork, beef or chicken fat on a mycelium or protein-flour body reads as that animal; heme adds color and meaty browning.
- **Plant cells supply aromas a flavorist can't fully fake.** Coffee and cocoa each carry hundreds of volatiles. Plant cells also make fruit and tomato purées. Algae and yeasts supply greens and color: lutein for yolks, phycocyanin blue, astaxanthin pink for "salmon".
- **Still missing:** juicy crunch, bone-in and skin-on cuts, whole fish, shell eggs, whole kernels. The Vat table is pastes, doughs, curds, gels, foams and grown or extruded fiber, which already describes most of the world's great processed foods: cheese, bread, tofu, sausage, miso, noodles, chocolate. Tiramisu is Vat-table through and through, at ~1.05 kWh a slice: the price of two tomatoes.

Dairy, egg and gluten are the biggest line, bigger than meat. Ribosome reactors would cut 0.7 kWh a day, about a third of the premium.
- **The Mill table costs the same per calorie at every meal (~1.8 Wh/kcal); the Vat table runs 2.4–2.9.**
- **The Vat day is food-system.md's common day, re-priced.** That diet comes to 8.0 kWh (332 W) at these efficiencies, down from 10.9 at planning values.
- **Food isn't where tight energy bites first.** On the common budget (3.1 kWe) the Mill table is 7% and the Vat table 10%. On the lean budget (0.96 kWe) they're 24% and 34%.

## Calendar and culture

### Night: a winter in the ring era, gone in the full swarm
| Swarm | Orbit | Night, mid-band / edge | Mid-band dawn every | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ~5 × 10²² ring swarm | ~240 d | 40 / 55 d | 120 d | Winter |
| 10²³ ring swarm | ~400 d | 68 / 93 d | 200 d | A winter longer than the oxygen margin |
| 10²⁴ ring swarm | 6.2 yr | 1.0 / 1.4 yr | 3.1 yr | A polar year |
| Full swarm (the Shade) | ~39,000 yr | None | — | No seasons |

Ring rows: 10 M☉, 1 kW/m², ~15 kW of light per person (xray-power.md); nights are 1/6 orbit mid-band and up to ~1/4 at band edges (energy-budget.md). Rings end near ~3 × 10²⁴ people (full-swarm.md), so the generational knot nights once sketched for 10²⁶ and up never happen: a civilization that large lives in the Shade.

- **Inside, nobody sees the knot.** The Hollow's sky and the parks are lamps. Knot night reaches people only through what the habitat chooses to dim, so how visible the night is becomes a cultural decision.
- **At ~5 × 10²² it's winter.** Mid-band nights fit the food plant's ~55-day oxygen margin, so the plant can pause and the larder carries the habitat. By 10²³ the nights outlast the margin, and the dark arcs need power sent along the ring.
  - Dusk is Carnival and kimjang (Korea's communal kimchi-making before winter) at once: eat the fresh cheese and the salad, then make the night's larder together.
  - The night eats preserved food: aged cheese, miso, cured mycelium, hard breads, sugar preserves. The Garden goes dark except the herb pot.
  - By dawn the oxygen is down from 21 to ~16–17 kPa, roughly Mexico City's air.
  - Dawn's feast is pea shoots and microgreens, a week or two after the lamps come back.
- **In the full swarm there is no night.** Power arrives by beam around the clock, on orbits of ~39,000 years. Every rhythm is chosen: the Hollow's lamps keep a 24-hour day because bodies want one, and any season is a festival.
  - The ring era's winter can survive as ritual: a dusk feast and a larder-making on a calendar date, kimjang without the dark.
  - **The only dark is a cut.** A raft whose beam fails or is cut runs its air on a survival reserve and has the same ~55-day oxygen margin a knot-night habitat had, but no promised dawn. The larder, the herb pot and the oxygen reserve become matters of sovereignty, and the winter menu is the siege menu. Away from the disk plane, a raft with glow sails can live on the Mill table for as long as a siege lasts (governance.md).
- **The interchange belt is the market season** (ring era). Where rings run parallel, ring changes are nearly free (swarm-rings.md), so that's when produce and heirloom cultures trade between rings: a week-long fair per pass at ~5 × 10²².
- **In the Shade, food barely travels.** Rafts pass each other at 100–300 km/s, so moving a kilogram between them costs far more than it's worth. Recipes, flavor formulas and culture genomes cross by laser instead. A passing raft is a fair conducted entirely in light.

### The blight menu
- **A blight takes out one line, never the calories.** The Mill carries the day while the line regrows from library stock. It takes ~27 doublings to go from a vial to a production vat: ~3 days for protein flour, about a week for yeast and mycelium, a month or more for animal cells, plus time to sterilize and confirm the phage is gone. A meat blight lasts about a Lent.
- **Each line has its blight menu:**
  - protein flour: the Nine comes back to the table;
  - precision yeast: starch loaves, starch custards, and "blight cheese" (coconut-style fat, starch and cheese aroma, i.e. today's vegan cheese);
  - mycelium: fried crunch stands in for chew;
  - animal cells: no nutrition is lost, only the luxury.
- **Real templates.** Phage is the classic cause of failed cheese and yogurt vats, which is why dairies rotate starter strains. In 2009 a vesivirus got into the hamster-ovary cells at Genzyme's Allston plant and halted Cerezyme and Fabrazyme; Gaucher and Fabry patients were rationed. That was an animal-cell blight, in a drug plant.
- **Blight food becomes memory food.** Depression-era mock apple pie (Ritz crackers, sugar, cream of tartar, cinnamon) is a Mill-table dish avant la lettre. Expect families to cook the blight menu on its anniversary.
- **Redundancy has a taste.** food-system.md keeps several organism families per product, so a Fusarium blight means a Neurospora year (Neurospora is also the mold in Indonesia's red oncom), and the steaks taste different. Blight years become vintages.
- **Heirlooms become public goods.** When a library fails, family sourdoughs, koji lines and cheese cultures are how a habitat reseeds. In the Shade a raft can also rebuild a lost line from a neighbour's genome, sent by laser, so a starter is both an heirloom and a file.

### The gin problem
- **Alcohol is nearly free.** A standard drink is 0.18 kWh: about a pat of butter, a third of a tomato. A 700 mL bottle of gin is 2.8 kWh, less than one orchard apple. Ten drinks a day costs a third of a Mill-table day. The juniper and coriander are milligrams of pinene and linalool.
- **London has done this.** In the Gin Craze (c. 1720–1751) spirits undercut beer ("drunk for a penny, dead drunk for twopence") until the 1751 Act and dearer grain ended it. Here there's no grain to get dearer.
- **The trap is methanol.** Tax or ration ethanol and bootleggers appear (Sweden rationed spirits with personal ration books until 1955). Their feedstock is already in every food plant: ~0.8 kg of methanol per person per day, a dozen or more lethal doses. Denaturing the Mill's own feedstock means scrubbing the additive back out before the catalysts see it. Prohibition's poisoned industrial alcohol shows how that ends.
- **The clinical angle.** Mill starch, sugar and ethanol carry no thiamine, so a Mill-table drinker is a Wernicke's case in waiting. Australia has fortified bread flour with thiamine since 1991 against Wernicke–Korsakoff, and Cambodia has trialed thiamine-fortified fish sauce against infant beriberi. Here thiamine goes into the salt, the Nine, and plausibly the gin; there are real proposals to fortify drinks.
- **No clean answer.** Cheap legal ethanol means more drinking and few bootleggers; dear ethanol means less drinking and more methanol. Each habitat picks a point on that curve, and the choice says a lot about it.

## Open questions
- Do ribosome reactors work? If so, most of the precision line, the Vat table's biggest bill, moves into the Mill.
- Which rituals keep the ring era's winter alive in a swarm with no nights?
- How beam power is priced, and whether the Lantern's operators ever use a cut as a weapon.
- In the ring era, whether habitats dim the Hollow's sky and parks at knot night, making the night visible.
- Alcohol policy, habitat by habitat.
