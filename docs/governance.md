# Governance: who holds the Lantern

*Design proposal from a working session, Oct 2026. Who can make whom do what in the mature civilization (full-swarm.md). It doesn't forecast what ~2 × 10³³ people will believe. It sets out what the physics forces, the leverage it hands out, and what's left to choose. Minds stay biological in this setting (a worldbuilding choice), so the Lantern has machines but no residents. Builds on economy.md (who holds what, beam by share, the Dark as a sentence), raft-habitat.md (the drum as a nation, the raft as a world) and raft-transport.md (cyclers and the Glide in the Dark). Rafts are 0.1 AU across (~6 × 10¹⁹ people) unless noted. The names (glow sails, orbit families, the keepers, the four regimes) are proposals. Revised Oct 2026 for the 3 km nation drum (raft-habitat.md): rafts can reach ~1.6 AU, and present ~3 m² per person to the Lantern. Revised Oct 29, 2026 after a consistency audit. Revised 1 Nov 2026: full-swarm.md now sets the population at daily-life.md's 2 kWe life, ~1.95 × 10³³ people with ~1.70 kWe beamed a head (~2.05 kW of microwaves), in a Shade ~2× the hole's mass. Patches, raft counts, reach, orbit families, glow sails and upkeep are resized; the Burn is restated, since no raft can move more than a few thousand km in the round trip; and climbing as the hole grows joins the rules of the road. Revised 2 Nov 2026 (radiation decisions, Jeff): the Shade weighs ~9.7 × 10⁶ M☉ with the drums' thicker front caps (full-swarm.md). Revised 6 Nov 2026 (consistency audit): the rules of the road cap free eccentricity; the glow's forced part is shared by neighbours.*

## Summary
- **Plural by default.** With ~10¹¹–3 × 10¹³ rafts and years of light-delay, no single sociology will hold. The questions here are narrower: what has to be shared, and who holds the leverage over it.
- **Little has to be shared:** the Lantern's power, trim and relays; the feed; the rules of the road; the frontier's stream; and, if beam follows heads, how many people there are. Everything else belongs to rafts and drums (economy.md).
- **Nothing above the raft governs in real time.** A reply across the Shade takes ~16 years. At 1,000-to-1 delegation a person sits ~11 tiers below a decision for everyone, and the top four tiers span light-years.
- **The raft is the natural state; the orbit family is the natural region.** Rafts pass at 100–300 km/s, so the ~2 × 10⁴ rafts within a light-day of any raft are replaced every 4–11 years. Only rafts on nearly the same orbit stay neighbours. The Lantern's patches are the only fixed geography.
- **The Lantern runs on standing rules.** Nobody lives there, and its nearest patch is 3–16 months away by light. Its rules are its real constitution, and whoever writes, amends and reads them holds the setting's deepest power.
- **Darkening a raft takes a region.** Every patch that can see a raft (5–17% of the shell, ~1.5–5 × 10¹² patches) can feed it, so a total Dark needs all of them to refuse. At most ~6–20 blocs that big fit on the shell.
- **The Dark can't be recalled in time** (economy.md). It lands 3–16 months after it's ordered, and a raft's reserve lasts ~55 days (~90 with a full tank farm).
- **But it needn't kill.** The Lantern can't stop glowing. Glow sails, heat engines run on its waste heat, carry a ration life (~0.4 kW per person) on 3–22 m² of sail per person, ~0.1–0.5% of a person's metal, everywhere except near the disk plane. With them the Dark becomes a siege.
  - Starving a sailed raft takes a whole region of the Lantern going dark. That browns out the 4–15% of rafts nearest the target, in view of a fifth to half of the Shade.
- **The fast levers sit with the Lantern.** The beam bites in months; the feed in months to millennia, and a deep cut sinks the Lantern too. Everyone else's levers (kinetic strikes, upkeep, relays around the shell, the metal stream) take centuries to a million years.
- **The frontier can't be starved.** Its outposts can live on the starlight of the stars they mine (~1–5 × 10³¹ people's worth, ~1–3% of the core), and ~1 Myr of metal is always falling. Neither side can coerce the other on any human timescale.
- **Left open:** what the Lantern answers to, whether rafts may carry sails, how light is shared, who decides how many people there are, and whether the Lantern can mend itself. Four sample regimes follow; over hundreds of millions of years, expect several in turn.

## What has to be shared
| Shared | Why it can't be local | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| The Lantern's power | One shell holds every raft's beam contract (economy.md) | Allocation rules, and a schedule booked a year ahead |
| Its trim and centring | Its drift off-centre e-folds in ~7,000 years, and holding it takes a coordinated trim of the whole shell (full-swarm.md) | A protocol, not a ruler: light crosses the shell in ~5 years |
| The feed | One hole, held at 30% of Eddington | An injection plan set months to millennia ahead (sgr-a-scope.md) |
| Traffic | Orbits cross at every angle | Rules of the road |
| The frontier's stream | Launched 100–200 pc out, caught in the Shade, aimed past the Lantern | Rules on aim, catch and allocation, on million-year contracts |
| Relays | The shortest path across the Shade runs through the Lantern | Optional: going around adds ~2 years to 8 |
| Population | Light and metal are fixed | Shared only if beam follows heads. Canon: each drum gets a fixed allotment and decides for itself (people.md) |

- **Everything else** (homes, food, law, rents, birth rights, the Hollow) is raft and drum business (economy.md). The raft's one say over births is the right of the Glide and sanctions on counted drums that go too far; it holds back a drum's beam only for the worst (people.md). How drums govern themselves varies; open-drums.md sketches the common free pattern: weekly block courts, a yearly assembly drawn by lot, and a vote on Booking Day.
- **The rules of the road can be thin, but they're absolute.** A raft passes within its own width of another only every ~600–1,900 years, predicted centuries ahead, and dodges with ~1 m/s (full-swarm.md). But across the Shade that's from ~2 × 10⁵ to ~4 × 10⁷ collision courses a year even with orbits kept round, and one miss is a 10³³–10³⁶ J blast whose debris can cascade. Five rules carry the weight:
  - **Keep orbits round.** Free eccentricity under ~0.003 cuts collision courses ~10×, since most come from rafts drifting radially into each other face to face. The rule leaves out the ~0.001–0.003 the Lantern's glow forces on inclined orbits (full-swarm.md), which neighbours on like orbits share.
  - **Who yields,** set by custom, as on Earth's seas.
  - **No orbit into the Lantern.** The shell hovers rather than orbits, so anything orbiting meets it at ~150 km/s, ~11 GJ per kg.
  - **Climb as the hole grows.** Infalling fuel and metal exert no torque on a raft, so a free orbit shrinks as the hole grows while the Lantern widens. Every raft keeps a steady prograde push of ~4–5 × 10⁻¹² m/s², a tilt of about half a degree in what its back radiates; an untrimmed raft at the inner edge would meet the shell within ~45 Myr (full-swarm.md). It costs almost nothing, and breaking it bites only after tens of millions of years.
  - **Balance.** A lopsided Shade tugs the hole off-centre, and the Lantern has to follow it (full-swarm.md).

## Scale and delay
| Within | One way by light | Rafts | People |
|---|---|---|---|
| One raft | Under a minute | 1 | ~6 × 10¹⁹ |
| The nearest neighbour (~6 AU) | ~50 minutes | 1 | ~6 × 10¹⁹ |
| A light-day | 1 day | ~2 × 10⁴ | ~10²⁴ |
| A light-month | 1 month | ~5 × 10⁸ | ~3 × 10²⁸ |
| A light-year | 1 year | ~6 × 10¹¹ | ~4 × 10³¹ (2%) |
| The Shade | ~8 years through the Lantern's relays | ~3 × 10¹³ | ~1.95 × 10³³ |

- **Delegation.** At 1,000 to 1, a person sits ~11 tiers below a decision for everyone. Six or seven of those tiers are inside their own raft, where messages take under a minute. The top four span light-years, and each round of deliberation across them takes ~16 years (~20 around the Lantern).
- **So nothing above the raft can govern in real time.** Spain's colonial officials, months from Madrid, had a formula for royal orders that didn't fit local facts: *obedezco pero no cumplo*, "I obey but do not comply." Here the geometry builds it in.

## The raft and the orbit family
- **The raft is the natural state** (raft-habitat.md). It shares a truss, a grid, its cyclers, a beam contract and every dodge. Nothing on it is more than ~13 light-minutes away, even at the ~1.6 AU cap. Its drums are its nations, each with its own commons, reserve and birth rights (economy.md).
- **There are no lasting places.** Rafts pass at 100–300 km/s, so the ~2 × 10⁴ rafts within a light-day of any raft are replaced every 4–11 years. A territory is just space that other rafts cross.
- **The orbit family is the natural region.** Rafts on nearly the same orbit stay together. A 1 AU difference in orbit size drifts them only ~1–3 AU apart per orbit, since the Shade's own weight keeps periods nearly the same across it (within ~10%), and a ~2 m/s nudge undoes it; the beam's own push gives ~20 m/s a millennium.
  - Families are the only lasting neighbours, and the only rafts that can trade goods cheaply: they move at metres to kilometres per second relative to each other, not 100–300 km/s.
  - They're also the natural unit for dodging, for keeping the Shade balanced and for bargaining with the Lantern.
  - **Families are founded, not joined.** Moving a raft onto another family's orbit takes a ~100–200 km/s turn of its whole mass: ~1,400–5,500 years of its entire beam. Sister rafts are launched onto a shared orbit when they're built, at the Shade's outer edge, where the growing hole adds tens of thousands of rafts a year.

## The Lantern
### Run by rules
- **Machine country with no citizens.** Minds stay biological in this setting, so nobody runs the Lantern from inside it. Its skin averages ~420 K, its interior is an oven, and its nearest patch is 3–16 months from the Shade by light (full-swarm.md).
- **So its rules are its constitution.** People govern it only through standing rules: what its machines do about trim and centring, beams and cuts, relays, batch compute, the feed, repair and growth. As Lessig said of software, code is law. The few who can read, amend and audit that code hold the setting's deepest power.
- **Its behaviour is public; its code isn't.** Every raft sees whether its beam arrives and how the sky glows. Checking the rules themselves takes inspectors sent in, or watchers hovering in the gap (see The gap).
- **A protocol, not a ruler.** Trim and centring need the whole shell to act together, but the drift e-folds in ~7,000 years and light crosses the shell in ~5. Rules plus letters suffice, even among enemies: if the shell drifts, every faction on it goes down together.

### No patch can hold a raft hostage
| Raft's distance from the centre | 1.10 Lantern radii (inner edge) | 1.30 | 1.49 (outer edge) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Share of the shell that can see it | 4.7% | 11.5% | 16.5% |
| Raft-sized patches in that view | ~1.5 × 10¹² | ~4 × 10¹² | ~5 × 10¹² |
| Share within 60° of facing it, where beaming works best | 0.5% | 2.5% | 4.5% |
| Blocs big enough to darken it alone | ~21 | ~9 | ~6 |

- **Any patch in view can beam to a raft,** and rafts hand off between patches about daily (full-swarm.md). A total Dark needs every patch in view to refuse.
- **So only a bloc covering a raft's whole view can darken it alone,** and at most ~6–20 such blocs fit on the shell. Whether the Lantern runs as one pool or as owned patches is a political choice; the geometry favours a pool.

### The Dark
- **It can't be recalled in time** (economy.md). A cut lands when the last beamed power arrives, 3–16 months after it's ordered. A restoration takes as long, so if the raft's answer is what ends it, the dark lasts at least 7–32 months.
- **A raft lasts ~55 days on its survival reserve,** ~90 if the cut finds its tank farm full (its liquid oxygen can be breathed, so the 90-day larder runs out first; full-swarm.md), or ~4–7 months with fast cyclers (raft-transport.md).
- **So without sails, the Dark is a sentence.** Its length has to be set when it's ordered, and any due process has to come first (economy.md). Beams should fail on, not off.

### The glow
- **The Lantern can't stop glowing.** Its skin sheds ~73% of the disk's light as infrared: ~1.5 kW/m² on average, ~4 over the poles and almost nothing over the disk plane (full-swarm.md).
- **A glow sail is a heat engine on that glow:** an absorber facing the Lantern at ~250–450 K, an engine at ~55% of Carnot, and radiators at ~170–330 K in the absorber's shadow. A front that absorbs and emits only toward the Lantern (angle-selective) yields ~10–40% more per m² than plain black, most at the outer edge.
- **A ration life needs ~0.4 kW per person:** the Mill table with its air and water loops (~0.23 kW at best-case prices; daily-life.md), lamps, fans and basic services, with no compute and no travel. Cryocooling can pause for as long as the jacket's boil-off can be burned or stored, since nothing vents (element-cycles.md).

| Latitude above the disk plane | 0–15° | 15–30° | 30–60° | 60–90° |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share of rafts there at any moment | 26% | 24% | 37% | 13% |
| Glow on a sail facing the Lantern | ~0.1–0.2 kW/m² | 0.3–0.7 | 0.7–1.8 | 1.0–2.9 |
| Electricity per m² of sail | 0–11 W | 19–32 W | 37–80 W | 56–132 W |
| Sail per person for 0.4 kW | 35–45 m²; none on the plane near the inner edge | 12–22 m² | 5–11 m² | 3–7 m² |
| Radiator per person | 30–55 m² | 15–20 m² | 6–10 m² | 4–6 m² |
| Film mass per person | 20–30 kg | 9–12 kg | 3–6 kg | 2–4 kg |

Each range spans the Shade from its inner edge to its outer edge, where inner rafts' 300 K backs hide ~35% of the Lantern.

- **Sails are cheap.** With engines and structure at two or three times the film's mass, they cost ~0.1–0.5% of a person's 6.7 t of metal outside the belt. They're stowed, and deployed around the raft's rim and over its idle rectennas when the beam stops.
- **They're a lifeboat, not a second grid.** Outside the belt a sail field is 1–7× the raft's own area. If every raft outside the belt deployed one, sails would add ~70–150% of the Lantern's sky to the rafts' ~43%, blocking ~70–85% of sightlines in all, since some overlap, and their warm faces would heat its skin and press on it (full-swarm.md's returned glow).
- **The belt can be starved; the poles can't.** Near the disk plane the glow is too weak to live on, so at any moment a quarter of rafts can't count on sails. A raft on a polar orbit crosses the belt twice an orbit (35,000–39,000 years), for ~3,000 years each time. The ~3% of orbits that stay within 15° of the plane never leave it. A raft's leverage changes with its latitude, over millennia.
- **Denying the glow costs a region.** Thermal glow can't be aimed. To starve a sailed raft, the Lantern has to darken the raft's whole view: turn that cap's collectors into mirrors, so their light crosses the interior to the rest of the shell.
  - That costs the Lantern little power (a percent or two), but it browns out the rafts nearest the target. Those within ~25–45° of it, 4–15% of all rafts at that distance, lose at least half their best-placed patches.
  - It also cools the sky for a fifth to half of the Shade, which all watches it happen.
  - The targeted alternative is a blockade: a screen as big as the raft, ~10¹⁹–10²⁰ kg of film (0.003–0.03% of the raft's mass), flown alongside it at its own 145–215 km/s and held between it and the Lantern, in full view of a raft that can cut it.
- **So sails turn a quiet sanction into a public one.** A beam cut is a line in a schedule; starving a sailed raft is a regional act. Whether rafts may carry sails, must carry them, or may not is this setting's right to bear arms.

### The gap
- **Anything light enough can hover** between the Lantern and the Shade, held up by the glow and, like the Lantern, neutral to drifting in or out. The limit is ~4–7 kg/m² at average latitudes (black to mirrored), ~10–19 over the poles and under 1 over the belt.
- **People can't.** A drum weighs thousands of kg per m² of its cross-section. Watchers, relays, screens and weapons can. Every beam crosses the gap, so it's natural contested ground.

### Compute and relays
- **The Lantern's batch compute is mail-order,** months to years each way (full-swarm.md). Interactive work already runs on the rafts, so the Lantern can hold no one's daily tools hostage, only archives and long jobs.
- **Refusing to relay** adds ~2 years to the longest 8-year messages, and anyone can build relays around the shell or through the gap.

## Leverage
| Lever | Who → whom | Time to bite | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beam cut (the Dark) | Lantern → a raft | 3–16 months to land | Fatal in ~2–7 months without sails; a siege on rations with them, outside the belt |
| Starving a sailed raft | Lantern → a raft and its region | The same | Browns out 4–15% of nearby rafts, in view of a fifth to half of the Shade |
| Glow blockade | Anyone → a raft | As long as it takes to fly a screen alongside | In plain view; the raft can cut it |
| Feed throttle | Injectors → everyone | Months (close injection) to millennia (far) | A deep, lasting cut sinks the Lantern too, over ~6,000–8,000 years |
| Relay refusal | Lantern → the Shade | At once | Adds ~2 years to 8 |
| Upkeep strike | The Shade's foundries → Lantern | As fast as collectors wear out | None, if the Lantern mends itself |
| The Burn | Lantern → a raft | 3–16 months, the light delay; nothing stops it once sent | A patch's beam focuses to a ~6–34 km spot at 1 cm (30–340 km at 5–10 cm), at ~10¹³–10¹⁵ W/m² (~10¹¹–10¹³ at the longer waves). No raft can move more than a few thousand km in the round trip; one patch sweeps it in days. Kills a raft outright, and unites the rest of the Shade against whoever ordered it |
| Kinetic strike | Shade → Lantern | ~800–2,800 years in flight from the inner Shade at 30–100 km/s | A raft punching through wrecks ~10⁻¹⁴ of the shell |
| Re-aimed stream | Frontier → Lantern | ~1 Myr of falling, in view | A payload ~0.4–0.7 km/s low at launch hits the Lantern at ~480 km/s |
| Metal embargo | Frontier → core | ~1 Myr, since that much is in flight | Growth is ~10⁻⁹ a year; the core barely notices |
| Beam cut | Core → frontier | Centuries to land | Slows mining; starves no one |

- **Only the beam and the feed are fast, and both sit with the Lantern or inside it.** The feed is a doomsday switch. It dims everyone, and since the Lantern's lift scales with the share of Eddington (black walls need ~30%, mirrored ~8–15%; full-swarm.md), a deep cut sinks the Lantern too.
- **So the politics turns on two questions:** what restrains the Lantern from the inside, and how far rafts harden themselves against the Dark (sails, fast cyclers, reserves).
- **Mutual vulnerability.** Anything orbiting meets the hovering shell at ~150 km/s. No raft can do real damage alone, but wrecking even 1% of the shell would take very roughly a solar mass of debris, the whole metal of a few million rafts out of ~3 × 10¹³: within reach of a large coalition, over centuries, in plain view.
- **The Lantern is downrange of the stream.** Payloads are aimed to swing past at the Shade's radius, with ~16% more angular momentum than would hit the Lantern (matter-delivery.md). The frontier's aim is a matter of trust.

## Core and frontier
- **Outposts can live on starlight.** The nuclear stellar disk's ~10⁹ M☉ of stars shine ~3–7 × 10⁸ L☉, enough for ~1–5 × 10³¹ people at the 2 kWe life, ~1–3% of the core. Only the speed of mining depends on the core's beam (matter-delivery.md).
- **An embargo bites in a megayear.** ~1 Myr of metal is always falling, and the core grows ~10⁻⁹ a year.
- **So neither side can coerce the other on any human timescale.** Their contracts are trade on geological terms (economy.md), and the frontier is in effect independent.
- **Growth widens the frontier.** The nuclear stellar disk's metal alone would cover ~1.7 Gyr of growth, so growth also draws on the inner bulge, out to ~0.4 kpc: up to ~1,300 years away by light, and ~3–4 Myr of falling (full-swarm.md). That only slows every lever between core and frontier further.

## Checks built into the plumbing
Founders who expect to lose power someday have reason to build in checks, as constitution-makers who fear losing elections write in judicial review (Ginsburg's insurance theory). Here the checks can be physical:
- **Far injection.** Fed at ~1,000 r_g, the disk can't be dimmed faster than over millennia. The price is planning output millennia ahead (sgr-a-scope.md).
- **Beams that fail on.** Faults, lost contact and disputes default to power flowing on the last schedule.
- **Cuts only by the whole view.** A charter can require every patch that sees a raft to agree, as the geometry nearly does already.
- **Sails on every raft,** and fast cyclers (raft-transport.md).
- **Relays around the Lantern** as well as through it.
- **A Lantern that can't mend itself,** so it depends on the Shade's foundries. Upkeep is cheap: the whole shell weighs ~0.5 years of the Shade's recycling, and collectors that last a millennium need ~0.05% of it. So the lever is slow but real.
- **Public schedules.** Beams are booked a year ahead (full-swarm.md), so every cut is in someone's ledger first.
- Which checks the builders chose, and which later regimes tore out, is ready-made history. The Lantern was sealed as a whole during the handover (full-swarm.md), so whoever sealed it wrote its first rules.

## The dials
| Dial | Options | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| What the Lantern answers to | A fixed charter; a cooperative of rafts; an order of keepers; regional blocs (economy.md's options, with minds biological) | Who can change the rules, and how fast |
| Whether the Dark can kill | Sails banned, allowed or required | Whether a cut is a sentence or a siege |
| How light is shared | By share (economy.md's proposal), by head, by contract, by contribution (metal delivered), by inheritance | Whether crowding pays, and whether population is shared business |
| Who decides how many people there are | Each drum within its allotment (canon), each raft, or the whole Shade | people.md's birth systems |
| Whether the Lantern mends itself | Self-maintaining, or dependent on the Shade's foundries | Whether the Shade has a lever on it at all |
| How the rules change | Locked; supermajority over decades; whoever holds the keys | Whether any regime can be locked in |
| Whether fusion exists | No (baseline); yes | A raft with its own reactors can't be darkened (matter-delivery.md) |

- **History cuts both ways on shared waterworks.** Wittfogel argued that irrigation empires bred despotism. Ostrom documented irrigation run by its own users for centuries, as in the huertas of Valencia, whose Water Tribunal by tradition has met weekly for over a thousand years.
- **A setting short of resources per head is one where someone chose more people over richer lives,** or failed to stop it. With light fixed, more people means leaner lives: Parfit's Repugnant Conclusion as a budget line. At the baseline the trade is ~1.5 to 1: the Lantern that gives ~1.95 × 10³³ people the 2 kWe life could give 1.27 × 10³³ the common budget's 3.1 kWe (full-swarm.md).

## Sample regimes (not predictions)
- **The Utility.** The Lantern runs a fixed charter: beam by share, cuts only by a process published years ahead, amendments by supermajority of rafts over decades. Politics lives on the rafts and in orbit families. The great crises are amendments, and fights over who interprets the code.
- **The keepers.** A hereditary or professional order tends the Lantern's machines and reads its code from rafts at the Shade's inner edge, the only people who understand it. Its legitimacy rests on competence and ritual, like Bali's water-temple priests, who scheduled irrigation for whole watersheds (Lansing). The danger is a caste.
- **The great powers.** A handful of blocs divide the shell, each big enough to darken the rafts beneath it. Rafts drift beneath them like ships along foreign coasts, changing sovereigns every few millennia as their orbits carry them on.
- **The Burn is the unspoken weapon.** The Lantern's beams sit deep in their near field across the whole gap, so a patch can focus on a raft and destroy it, and no raft can dodge or shield against it. Nothing in the hardware forbids it. It's held back by horror rather than physics: the Burn is the one act every raft treats as a crime against all, and any bloc that orders it can expect the rest of the Shade to unite against it, by upkeep strike, kinetic strike and relays routed around its patches. It has happened, if at all, rarely enough to be history.
- **The armed peace.** Sails make the Dark survivable, and debris parked on orbits that could be bent into the shell makes the Lantern vulnerable. Formal government stays thin, and order rests on deterrence and long memories.
- **Expect several in turn.** The Lantern will outlive every government that claims it. A charter with no amendment path, enforced by the machines themselves, could lock one in; otherwise the useful choice is which era to set stories in.

## Method
- **Baseline** (full-swarm.md, 1 Nov 2026): luminosity 1.63 × 10³⁷ W; the Lantern at 0.824 pc (2 kW/m² average); the Shade at 0.91–1.23 pc (1.10–1.49 Lantern radii), ~9.7 × 10⁶ M☉ spread evenly through it (8.7 × 10⁶ before the 2 Nov 2026 front caps; the orbit figures below use 8.7); ~1.95 × 10³³ people at 8.3 kW of light each, on daily-life.md's 2 kWe life. Each person's 1.70 kWe beamed leaves the Lantern as ~2.05 kW of microwaves (× 1.03 margin ÷ 90% rectenna ÷ 95% capture), 0.248 of the light, so the skin sheds 0.98 − 0.248 = 0.732. Rafts of 0.1 AU hold ~6 × 10¹⁹ people (raft-transport.md). Until 1 Nov 2026 this doc used the common budget's 1.27 × 10³³ people, 12.8 kW of light and 3.1 kW of microwaves each.
- **Views:** a raft at distance d sees a cap of the shell with cos γ = R/d, a share (1 − R/d)/2. Well placed means a patch's normal within 60° of the line to the raft. Blocs ≈ 1 ÷ that share. A patch is the area that beams one 0.1 AU raft's microwaves (~2.05 kW per person, ~1.2 × 10²³ W) at the Lantern's microwave flux (0.248 × 2 kW/m²): ~2.5 × 10²⁰ m², ~0.12 AU across.
- **Reach:** rafts spread evenly through the Shade's shell, counted within the light-distance of a raft at mid-Shade by Monte Carlo. Nearest neighbour ≈ 0.55 × (raft density)^(−1/3). Turnover = mean chord (4/3 of the radius) ÷ 100–300 km/s.
- **Orbit families:** with k = d ln M_enc/d ln r = 3.4–4.1 across the Shade (the hole plus the part of the Shade inside), d ln Ω/d ln r = (k − 3)/2 = +0.18 to +0.53, so outer orbits run slightly faster. Along-track drift = 2π × (d ln Ω/d ln r) × δa per orbit, corrected by Δv = v × (k + 1)/2 × (δa/a) at 145–215 km/s. (The earlier "~9 AU" and "~0.4 m/s" were Keplerian, which ignored the Shade's own mass.) Joining: 8.9 t per person (full-swarm.md; this line had 9.3 t), Δv 100–200 km/s at exhaust speed equal to Δv, so (e − 1) × 8.9 t of propellant, against the raft's 1.75 kWe per person of beam (1.70 kWe plus the 3% margin; it was 2.7 kWe at the common budget).
- **Light delay:** one way = (d − R)/c, for 0.09–0.41 pc.
- **Glow:** skin exitance = 0.732 × the local disk light, on full-swarm.md's pattern dL/dΩ ∝ cos i (1 + 2.06 cos i). Irradiance on a plate facing the centre by numerical view factors. Inner rafts' 300 K backs (emissivity 0.9) hide 0, 20 and 35% of the Lantern at 1.10, 1.30 and 1.49 radii, counting overlaps, for ~1.95 × 10³³ people at ~3 m² each (full-swarm.md's ~43% of the sky, ~35% of sightlines). Until 1 Nov 2026 the table used 0, 15 and 32%; rerunning it at the new hiding moves the outer edge's yields by a few percent and the rest by less.
- **Sails:** angle-selective fronts emit only into the Lantern's cone, a share (R/d)² of a black body's emission; engine at 55% of Carnot; radiators one-sided at emissivity 0.9. Absorber and radiator temperatures minimise mass at 0.2 kg/m² of absorber and 0.4 kg/m² of radiator, for 0.4 kW per person. Latitude bands are evaluated at 7.5°, 22.5°, 45° and 75°. Time in the belt on an orbit of inclination i: (2/π) arcsin(sin 15° ÷ sin i) of each orbit.
- **Starving a sailed raft:** the target's whole visible cap goes dark. Neighbours at the same distance are counted when at least half their well-placed patches fall inside it. Lantern efficiency at the warmer skins uses full-swarm.md's collector formula.
- **Hovering:** one unit of the average disk light's push holds 7.5 kg/m² at 30% (full-swarm.md). Near the shell the skin's glow pushes a black plate at ⅔ and a mirror at 4/3 of its flux ÷ c.
- **Screen:** the raft's footprint (3 m² per person) at 0.1–1 kg/m².
- **The Burn:** Airy spot 2.44 λz/D from a ~0.06–0.07 AU patch (full-swarm.md) across the 0.09–0.41 pc gap. Steering at about the beam's own push (~7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²; Beam push, below), a raft moves ½at² ≈ 90–2,500 km in the 6–32-month round trip, against its own ~15 million km width; a 10 m/s jink would take hundreds of times that push. A spot covers about one drum's cell. At ~10⁹–10¹⁰ J/m², the patch that feeds a 0.1 AU raft (~1.2 × 10²³ W) kills it drum by drum in ~1–8 days. (Until 1 Nov 2026 the table said that even spread over everywhere a dodging raft could be, the beam was ~50× sunlight; that needed a spread ~380,000 km in radius, far more than any raft can move.)
- **Beam push:** ~1.95 kW of captured microwaves a person ÷ c, on ~9.9 t a person: ~7 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² (~1.1 × 10⁻⁹ at the common budget).
- **Kinetic:** circular speed at 0.824 pc around 4.35 × 10⁶ M☉. Free fall from the shell: (π/2)√(r³/2GM), × √2 at half lift. Damage: ~1 MJ wrecks a kg of collector (12.5 kg/m²), with 1–10% of the impact's 11 GJ/kg coupled in.
- **The stream:** payloads with periapsis 1 pc arrive at ~440 km/s in matter-delivery.md; the Shade's extra mass since 1 Nov 2026 deepens the well, adding ~6% (~470 km/s at 1 pc, ~480 at the Lantern). Hitting the Lantern takes 0.84× the angular momentum.
- **Frontier:** the nuclear stellar disk's 1.05 × 10⁹ M☉ (matter-delivery.md) at a mass-to-light ratio of 1.5–4 for an old population, and ~5–8.3 kW of light per person at the 2 kWe life (7.8–12.8 kW at the common budget, so the share of the core is unchanged).
- **Upkeep:** the Lantern's 5 × 10⁴ M☉ against the Shade's recycling of ~100 kg per person a year at the 2 kWe life (daily-life.md), ~10⁵ M☉ a year.
- **Delegation:** log₁₀₀₀(1.95 × 10³³) ≈ 11; log₁₀₀₀(6 × 10¹⁹) ≈ 6.6.
