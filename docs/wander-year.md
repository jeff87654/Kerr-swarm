# Wander year: thirteen cards from across Merrow

*Narrative sketch from a working session, Oct 2026. A year in the life of Marit Vell of Chervil, told in the cards she sent home while crossing Merrow on guest-right. Builds on open-drums.md (guest-right, the wander year, Chervil, Marit, Jory and Oona, Booking Day, the Long Flight), raft-transport.md (cyclers and capsules), daily-life.md (prices and the wallet), eating-out.md (the Wanderers' Galley, the ring counters, Lightfall's passing-raft menu), ashgrove.md (the Window, the River and the Shelf, back crews, spinball, the black thread), people.md (counted drums, the fitting, passing rafts) and raft-habitat.md (the Hollow, the Spindle and its flying halls). Numbers come from those docs. Marit, Jory, Oona, Chervil, the Galley and the ring counters are already in them; every other name is new, and the new places, customs and choices are proposals, listed under What the sketch assumes. Exploration page: [Marit's Wander Year](https://claude.ai/artifact/Lnb82ZHwuEfsqXrF6vrw2Y).*

## At a glance
- **Who:** Marit Vell, a cook from Chervil, an open drum on Herb Row (open-drums.md). She went at twenty-five, later than most, the year after her grandfather died; she holds his place now. She writes to Jory, her partner; Oona, one of her aunts and a lamp-painter; her table; and the Thursday crew at the Ring 2 hall.
- **What a wander year is:** most young people in open drums spend about a year crossing the raft, sleeping three nights at a time in drums that grant guest-right and riding slow cyclers between them (open-drums.md). The custom here is one card home from every row, sent by light, postmarked with the delay.
- **The trip:** twelve cycler legs in a loop, 30.7 million km, out from Herb Row to the far side of Merrow and back. ~183 nights aboard, 51 drums slept in, 13 cards.
- **What it cost:** 13.2 kWh of fares for the year, less than a pair of shoes (20). Her dearest purchase was a pair of remade wing-sleeves at 18 kWh. Almost all of the cost is time.
- **New here:** seven rows and their drums, a passing raft called Selkie, the Passing, the raft loaf, and a counted drum taking down its Count.

## The route
Merrow is ~300 billion nation drums 24.5 km apart, about 13.4 million km on a side. Herb Row sits near one edge. Every leg is a slow cycler at 2 km/s, boarded by capsule for ~1.1 kWh however far it goes (raft-transport.md); hops between neighbouring drums are free glides.

## The cards
Each card has a picture on the front. The postmark gives the day and the light delay home to Chervil.

### Day 1: Chervil, Station Ring 2
*To Oona. Front: the Ledger board at lamp-dawn, and the table's hand lamps. Light home: 0 s.*

Writing this on the platform, because the capsule's late and the whole table is still here pretending not to cry. You made me a dawn for leaving and I spent it in the queue at the Ring Counter. Sorry. I saw the gold part through the lift glass.

The Ledger had Grandad's name this morning with mine beside it. Jory says that means I'm allowed to go now.

I've packed one crock of Chervil sourdough (Lovage's, don't tell), my good knife, two shirts, a black thread in case, and a year. The first leg costs 1.1 kays. A pair of shoes costs twenty. I could go round the raft eighteen times for a pair of shoes.

A card from every row. I promise.

### Day 7: the slow line, leg 1
*To Jory. Front: the Wanderers' Galley and its shelf of starter crocks. Light home: 3.4 s.*

Nobody told me the slow line has no weight. It's obvious once you think about it: the raft doesn't spin, so neither does the train. Soup comes in bulbs, bread comes in a net, and you sleep in a bag clipped to the wall like a coat.

The stockpot is a sealed crock with a crank on the side, and it hasn't gone cold in living memory. Everyone adds something from the last drum they slept in. I put in our herb cuttings and three people asked what the green was. Coriander. They'd never had it fresh.

The riders are all twenty and I'm twenty-five, so I'm "aunt." Two of them have already asked me to settle an argument about broth.

They say do the bungee hour every day or your bones sulk. I'm doing it.

### Day 22: Sloe, Hedge Row
*To her table. A wood drum. Front: the dim wood. Light home: 8.7 s.*

Sloe is a wood drum. Its parks are one long dim wood instead of bright squares, and the whole drum is quieter than our hall at three in the morning.

In heliox a whisper carries, so in the wood nobody talks. People carry slates. A woodwarden, who is ninety and has kept the same yews for sixty years, wrote LISTEN on hers and walked off. So I did. Under everything you can hear the drum: fans, pumps, water, two hundred million people breathing. I've lived inside that sound my whole life and never once heard it.

Their hall serves the Mill table in silence, and it's the best jook I've ever had. I think it's because you actually taste it.

I already miss being loud.

### Day 60: Kittiwake, Gull Row
*To Jory. A maker drum. Front: crosswise lessons in the flying hall. Light home: 18.9 s.*

Kittiwake makes wing-sleeves, and the whole drum flies. Children here learn crosswise before they can read.

Flying crosswise is the hard way. Along the axis you fly straight. Across it, the drum turns under you, and at a good pace you drift twenty metres sideways in ten seconds. So you aim wide, like throwing anti at spinball. My teacher is nine. She calls me "big aunt" and laughs every time I hit the net.

On my fourth try I came out exactly at the hoop I'd aimed for, and the whole hall clapped. They clap for every grown-up, so it means nothing, and I'm still pleased.

I bought sleeves. Remade, not new: 300 grams of new cloth and three and a half hours of a maker's time, 18 kays. That's more than every fare I'll pay this year put together. We're flying the Long Flight in them.

### Day 96: Gloaming, Weather Row
*To Oona. A fair drum. Front: a painted dusk from the end-wall terraces. Light home: 28.0 s.*

Gloaming paints dusks, not dawns. It auctions the seats on the end-wall terraces and gives the rents back to everyone, which Lovage never would. Tonight's dusk was by a painter called Hesk, who is about thirty and very sure of himself.

He ended on green. Not a flash: forty minutes of it, the sunline going out through a cold sea-green while the far bank turned blue-black overhead. The terraces were silent and then they roared. I thought of you the whole time, because you'd hate it and then go home and try it.

Gloaming says dawns are easy, because everyone's grateful just to have light, and a dusk you have to earn. I told them about your River dawn and they've asked for the file. I said you'd want to fix the middle first.

### Day 103: Squall, Weather Row
*To Jory. A steady drum, one glide from Gloaming. Front: swell hour on the Hollow lake. Light home: 28.0 s.*

Squall has a swell. Paddles at one end of the Hollow lake make waves for three hours a day, and at half a g a wave comes in slow and tall: nearly half again as high as the same push would make on Earth, with five whole seconds between crests to think about what you're doing.

I stood up on the eleventh try. Then I fell off, very slowly, which at half a g leaves you time to wave. The paddles draw less than a few blocks' park lamps, and Squall votes every year to keep them, by a mile.

Their ring counter does a broth with sea lettuce from the strain library. I'm stealing it.

Booking Day is in two weeks and I'll be on the slow line for it. They say the Quiet in a tube is very quiet. I'll write you a long letter during it and send it after.

### Day 140: Carillon, Bell Row
*To the Thursday crew at the Ring 2 hall. A hall drum. Front: Selkie from Carillon's Window, black against the River. Light home: 32.6 s.*

I'm in Carillon for the Passing. Every few years another raft comes within a couple of AU of Merrow, and this time it's Selkie. From Carillon's Windows it's a black shape in the stars two fingers wide at arm's length, the biggest raft anyone here has seen.

Selkie is seventeen light-minutes away, so Carillon sings to it. Every evening the choirs send a piece, and thirty-three minutes later Selkie's answer starts: their voices, their heliox, a little different from ours. Last night they sent back a round we'd sent them, sung against itself. Two thousand people in the hall went quiet for the whole gap, and then cried.

The kitchens trade too. I sent our Thursday ramen. Selkie sent dumplings folded in a way none of us could work out until a ten-year-old did it. I'll teach you.

### Day 185: Dulse, Kelp Row
*To Jory. A hall drum on the far side. Front: noodles pulled on a third-g terrace. Light home: 36.9 s, the farthest of the year.*

The far side. You're 37 seconds away by light, the farthest you'll ever be from me, and I'm in a kitchen.

Dulse pulls its noodles on the end-wall terraces, at a third of a g, because there a strand can hang three times as long before its own weight tears it. The old cook here, Abelone, pulls one noodle per bowl. One. It goes round the bowl about forty times. She's seventy-eight and lets visiting cooks stay as long as they cook, so I've done nine days of breakfasts and every evening of stretch, fold and swing.

My strands break at about four metres. Hers don't break.

I think I know what I want now. A hall: a long table, the same faces every Thursday, and a pot that doesn't go cold. Tell Oona before she guesses.

P.S. The far side says "kays" like "keys." I'm not having it.

### Day 222: Madder, Pigment Row
*To her table. A counted drum, newly open. Front: the first Ledger going up at Station Ring 4, the old Count beside it. Light home: 33.1 s.*

I came to Madder for the colours (the whole row grinds pigments) and got here the week they took the Count down.

Madder counted for fourteen years after a vat fire. Its stop ran past par, the way stops do, and on Booking Day the assembly voted to keep the difference: 1.9%, about 3.8 million empty places. A Margin. Today they lifted the old Count board off the wall at Ring 4 and put up a Ledger, and this year, for the first time in fourteen, nobody fitted the fourteen-year-olds.

I didn't expect to cry at a board. Everyone did. The old man next to me said his granddaughter turns fourteen next month, and then he couldn't say anything else.

I told them about our Ledger hall, with our old Count hanging beside it. They want to hang theirs the same way. Someone from Rue should come and see this.

### Day 226: Ochre, Pigment Row
*To Oona. A steady drum, one glide from Madder. Front: the River from behind the radiators. Light home: 33.1 s.*

Ochre lets a guest ride one shift with a back crew, if you sign for the dose. So I spent eight hours on a sled behind the radiators, holding a pump while a woman called Tilde swore at it, with the whole sky over my shoulder.

I saw the River again, for the first time since I was eight. And I have to tell you: you're right. It is brown-violet. Not the stars: the dust lanes, at the edges, where the River frays into the Shelf. Tilde says the crews call that colour bruise. Your dawn has been right for more than seventy years and I'm the first person to check.

Pigment Row grinds colours for half the raft. I asked Ochre's grinder to match it. He did it in about ten minutes, which annoyed me. I'm bringing you a jar.

### Day 290: Spelt, Grain Row
*To Jory. A maker drum. Front: Pell's crock room and this year's raft loaf. Light home: 23.7 s.*

I've found the baker. Every wanderer on the far side told me to find Pell, and he's exactly what they said: enormous, floury, Low-deck born, seventy-one, and the man who makes the raft loaf.

Every spoon of starter a wanderer leaves in his crock room goes through Spelt's Gate and the strain library, gets registered, and goes into the year's mother. This year's has two hundred and twelve drums in it, and now ours. I gave him a spoon of Chervil sourdough and confessed it was Lovage's. He laughed for a full minute and said half the spoons are lies like that.

He gives every wanderer a heel of the raft loaf for the road. It tastes of everywhere. I'm going to keep a bit of it alive in a crock and never tell the Gate.

(I'm going to tell the Gate.)

### Day 344: the slow line, leg 11
*To Jory. Front: a noodle-pulling lesson in the Galley. Light home: 7.6 s.*

Do not pull noodles at zero g. They don't hang, they swim. The galley is full of loose noodle, the riders are catching it with chopsticks, and they've voted it the best night of the line.

I'm three weeks from Ring 2. At the last station I stood by the express ramp for a long time. 111 kays, about five days of my wallet, and I'd be home on Thursday. I didn't. I want to come in slow and watch the rows go by, and I want three weeks of you knowing I'm coming.

So far: 12.1 kays of fares out of 13.2 for the year, about a hundred and seventy nights aboard, forty-nine drums. You've had most of the cards.

Meet me at Ring 2. Bring the aunts.

### Day 365: Chervil, Station Ring 2
*To Pell, at Spelt. Front: an orchard apple cut crosswise on the platform. Light: 23.7 s back to Spelt.*

Home. My block met me at Ring 2 with hand lamps at lamp-dawn, my table at the front, and Oona with a jar of bruise she'd already ground herself. Jory had brought an apple. He cut it crosswise on the platform and held up the star, which is a wedding thing, and he knows it is, and he went very red. I'm going to ask him something on Booking Day.

The Ledger tonight: 194,052,000 people, 409,600 guests, and thirty-seven wanderers home.

I start at the Ring 2 hall next month: Thursdays, one long table, four hundred seats. We'll serve the Lovage loaf and say we make it. I'm sending you a spoon of the mother I've started. It has Sloe's quiet in it, and Dulse, and Selkie, and a bit of your heel.

If any of your wanderers come through Chervil, send them to my table. We'll know the same baker.

## What the sketch assumes
**Choices among options the docs leave open:**
- **Riding cyclers is weightless.** The raft is nearly weightless and doesn't spin (raft-transport.md), so a coasting train has no up. Soup comes in bulbs and riders exercise an hour a day.
- **Booking Day is raft-wide.** Merrow holds the beam contract, so it learns its beam on one day, and Marit is aboard leg 4 for it.
- **Guest-right is generous to workers.** Dulse lets visiting cooks stay past three nights as long as they cook.
- **Madder's Margin** came the way open-drums.md says most do: a stop that ran past par, kept by a vote on Booking Day.
- **Booking Day in Chervil** (open-drums.md) is the payoff: Marit's hall, the hall-fund vote, the Long Flight in her Kittiwake sleeves, the wanderers who "know the same baker" (Pell), and her asking to marry in the spring.

**New here:**
- **Rows and drums.** Hedge Row: Sloe, a wood drum of silent woods and slates. Gull Row: Kittiwake, a maker drum that makes wing-sleeves and teaches crosswise flying. Weather Row: Gloaming, a fair drum that paints dusks and returns its terrace rents, and Squall, a steady drum with a swell on its Hollow lake. Bell Row: Carillon, a hall drum of choirs. Kelp Row: Dulse, a hall drum on the far side that pulls noodles at a third of a g. Pigment Row: Madder, just gone from counted to open, and Ochre, a steady drum whose back crews take a guest for a shift. Grain Row: Spelt, a maker drum, home of the raft loaf. Thimble, Cooper, Kite and Quill Rows are passed through without a card.
- **People.** Hesk, a dusk-painter in Gloaming; Abelone, Dulse's old cook; Tilde, a back-crew hand in Ochre; Pell, the baker at Spelt.
- **Selkie,** a raft that passes Merrow at ~2 AU.
- **Customs.** One card home from every row, postmarked with the light delay. The Passing: choirs and kitchens trading with a raft on a close pass, the live version of Lightfall's passing-raft menu (eating-out.md). The raft loaf: a yearly mother starter built from wanderers' spoons and registered with the strain library. The bungee hour on the slow line. "Big aunt." "Bruise," the back crews' word for the brown-violet at the River's dusty edges, which Oona has been painting since she was eight (open-drums.md).

## Questions it raises
- **Weeks without weight.** A wander year spends about half its nights weightless, in legs of two to three weeks. Is an hour's exercise a day enough, or do sleeper cars on slow lines spin? It's the same question for anyone who lives aboard cyclers (raft-transport.md's cosmic-ray question is the other half).
- **Guests outside the jacket.** What dose does a guest take on one back-crew shift, and who signs for it?
- **How often rafts pass.** The Passing assumes a raft within ~2 AU about once in four years. If close passes are rarer or commoner, so is the festival, and so are the swaps people.md expects with passing rafts.
- **Starter traffic.** Wanderers carry living cultures through every Gate they pass. The Gates and strain libraries presumably make this routine, which is the raft loaf's premise.

## Method
- **Merrow:** 3 × 10¹¹ drums at 24.5 km spacing ≈ 5.5 × 10⁵ drums a side ≈ 13.4 million km. Herb Row at (1.6, 6.2) M km; stops placed by hand; the loop is 30.7 M km.
- **Legs:** distance ÷ 2 km/s (172,800 km a day) + 0.5 day boarding. Fare: 0.28 kWh × 2² ≈ 1.1 kWh a capsule (raft-transport.md); 12 legs ≈ 13.2 kWh. Stays of 6–30 days a row, three nights a drum on guest-right, give 51 drums and a 365-day year.
- **Light delays:** straight-line distance to Chervil ÷ c. The farthest stop, Kelp Row, is ~11 M km off: 36.9 s.
- **Wing-sleeves:** 0.3 kg new at ~25 kWh/kg (7.5) + 3.5 h at ~3 kWh (10.5) ≈ 18 kWh.
- **Crosswise flight:** in a nation drum ω = 0.0572 rad/s; flying across the axis at 4 m/s, the Coriolis push 2ωv ≈ 0.46 m/s² drifts you ½at² ≈ 23 m in 10 s.
- **Half-g waves:** a wave's energy per area is ½ρga², so for the same energy its amplitude goes as 1/√g: √2 taller at 0.5 g. Deep-water period √(2πλ/g): a 20 m wave takes ~5.1 s at 0.5 g against 3.6 s on Earth. A 1.5 m swell along a ~500 m paddle line carries ~0.5–1 MW, a few blocks' park light at ~160 kW a block (ashgrove.md).
- **Third-g noodles:** a hanging strand tears when its weight ρgAL exceeds its strength σA, so its longest length L = σ/ρg goes as 1/g: three times longer at a third of a g.
- **The Passing:** rafts ~13 AU apart (ashgrove.md) give a number density n ≈ 4.6 × 10⁻⁴ AU⁻³. At v ≈ 200 km/s ≈ 42 AU a year, passes within d come at nπd²v: ~0.24 a year for 2 AU (once in ~4 years), ~1 a year for 4 AU. At 2 AU a 0.1 AU raft is ~2.9° across, about two fingers at arm's length, and 16.6 light-minutes away, so a reply begins ~33 minutes after a song is sent. It stays within ~3 AU for about a month.
- **Madder:** 200 M places × 1.9% ≈ 3.8 M Margin.
- **Chervil at homecoming:** about four months before Booking Day, so between open-drums.md's figures: 194,052,000 people, a Margin of 5,948,000, ~410,000 guests.
- **The express:** 111 kWh by cycler at 20 km/s (daily-life.md), ~5 days of a 21.6 kWh wallet.
