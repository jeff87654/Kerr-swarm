# Matter delivery: metal for the full swarm

*Design proposal from a working session, Sept 2026. Scopes how the mature civilization (full-swarm.md: Sgr A* at 30% of Eddington, ~1.3 × 10³³ people) gets its heavy elements: how much, from where, how it travels, how it's caught, and where it's processed. The hole is held at 30% as it grows, so after the build-out the civilization keeps growing with it (full-swarm.md, Growth at 30%). Uses ~6.7 t of heavy elements per person (habitat-design.md) and the common budget (energy-budget.md); hydrogen and helium are free, as elsewhere. Corrects sgr-a-scope.md on white dwarfs, which can be mined, and answers energy-budget.md's open question on the price of growth. Revised Oct 2026 for the 3 km nation drum: lighter shields stretch the cluster's gas to ~820 Myr, and a lighter Shade (5.7 × 10⁶ M☉) slows arrivals by ~2%. Revised Oct 29, 2026 after a consistency audit.*

## Summary
- **~3.9 × 10⁶ M☉ of heavy elements must be imported.** The 30% civilization holds ~4.3 × 10⁶ M☉, and the nuclear star cluster supplied ~4.5 × 10⁵ for the 3% era. Hydrogen stays local at first: the cluster's stripped hydrogen and helium cover the shields and ~820 Myr of fuel at a held 30%.
- **Planning pace: 0.04 M☉/yr, building out from 3% to 30% of Eddington in ~100 Myr.**
  - An Earth mass every ~40 minutes, or a 1,000 km cube of rock every second.
  - ~2 M☉/yr of stars or gas processed: the Milky Way's star-formation rate, run in reverse.
  - About as much mass as the whole Lantern is always in transit.
- **Then the civilization grows with the hole.** Held at 30%, the hole doubles every ~0.5 Gyr once feeding has spun it up, and metal must keep pace: ~0.01–0.03 M☉/yr, rising with the hole. The nuclear stellar disk's living stars cover ~2.0 Gyr of that, with drums taking ~75% of their metal from white dwarfs (element-cycles.md); ~1.1 Gyr on solar-mix metal alone.
- **Lifting is the bill; the trip is cheap.** Per kg of heavy element: ~4,000–8,000 GJ to take apart the star it's mixed into, ~14 GJ to launch it inward, ~85 GJ released on arrival, ~0.1 GJ to fabricate it into habitat.
- **Old stars are too dim to lift themselves quickly.** On half their own light, a Sun-like star takes ~60 Myr and a red dwarf ~700 Myr. So the frontier runs on light beamed from the Lantern: ~0.2–0.4% of the mature civilization's electricity at the planning pace. The core pays in light; the frontier pays in metal. Outposts can still live on their stars' own light; only the mining needs the beam (governance.md).
- **Payloads fall for ~1–1.5 Myr** and reach the Shade at ~400–490 km/s, ~1 keV per nucleon, against orbits of ~160 km/s.
- **The catch is a momentum problem.** About 2 kg arriving prograde for each 1 kg retrograde keeps catchers on station, leaving ~85 GJ/kg to shed.
  - That's ~11,000× what it takes to vaporize iron.
  - So payloads either carry their own magnets for lossless braking, or are vaporized on purpose and caught as plasma, which sorts the elements on the way in.
- **Arrival energy is a windfall locally and a rounding error globally:** ~850× what fabrication needs, so catchers run the foundries, but the whole stream is ~4 × 10⁻⁵ of the Lantern.
- **Sort at the source, build at the core.**
  - Shipping bulk star material moves ~47× the mass and pours ~100× the hole's appetite of gas into the central parsecs.
  - Condensation strips out hydrogen, helium and neon almost for free.
  - Finished goods wouldn't survive the catch, and would arrive a million years out of date.
- **Stars may not be the best ore.**
  - The Central Molecular Zone's gas and the bar's inflow carry heavy elements at about the planning pace, with no star to take apart. Processing the gas costs only ~0.01–0.1 GJ per kg of heavy element; collecting it is the real bill, up to ~60 GJ/kg if it has to be matched in speed. Either way it's ~100–100,000× cheaper than stars.
  - White dwarfs are nearly pure carbon and oxygen. The nuclear stellar disk's hold 5–9× all the heavy elements in its living stars, at about a red dwarf's cost per kg.
- **Growth is ~10⁻⁹–10⁻⁸ per year,** so the population is effectively closed. Gathering one person's metal from stars costs ~25–55 PJ, ~50,000× building their habitat; from gas, as little as ~0.1 TJ.

## How much
| Pace | Heavy elements | Stars or gas processed | In transit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3% → 30% in 100 Myr (planning)** | **0.04 M☉/yr** | **~2 M☉/yr** | **~5 × 10⁴ M☉** |
| 3% → 30% in 10 Myr | 0.4 M☉/yr | ~20 M☉/yr | ~5 × 10⁵ M☉ |
| Then growing with the hole at 30% | ~0.01–0.03 M☉/yr, rising with the hole | ~0.4–1.5 M☉/yr | ~1–4 × 10⁴ M☉ |

- **Planning pace in familiar units:** 2.5 × 10²¹ kg/s, or ~13,000 Earth masses a year. At ~2.5 t/m³, a 1,000 km cube every second.
- **In transit** assumes ~1.2 Myr of flight. At the planning pace that's about the Lantern's mass.
- **Growing with the hole** takes metal about as fast as the hole gains mass, because the swarm's heavy elements weigh about as much as the hole (4.5 vs. 4.3 × 10⁶ M☉).
  - That's ~0.016 M☉/yr while feeding spins the hole up (~150–170 Myr), then ~0.009 M☉/yr, rising as the hole doubles every ~0.5 Gyr (full-swarm.md, Growth at 30%).
  - The nuclear stellar disk's living stars run out ~2.0 Gyr into the 30% era, with the hole ~19× its present mass, at the planning ~75% white-dwarf share (element-cycles.md). On solar-mix metal alone they'd run out at ~1.1 Gyr (hole ~5×).
  - The CMZ's gas and the bar's inflow (~1–6 × 10⁷ M☉ of heavy elements per Gyr) could more than double that. The disk's white dwarfs carry carbon and oxygen for ~2–2.5 Gyr of growth, if drums can lean on those two elements.
- **Hydrogen isn't imported at first.**
  - Stripped, the cluster's stars leave ~2.1 × 10⁷ M☉ of hydrogen and helium; its white dwarfs, ~10–15% of its mass, are kept as ore.
  - That covers the shields (2.2 t per person in 3 km drums, ~1.4 × 10⁶ M☉ today, growing with the population) and the hole's fuel for ~820 Myr, by which time the hole and the civilization have grown ~3.5×. (2 km drums' 3.3 t shields would have cut that to ~750 Myr.)
  - After that, fuel joins the stream at ~0.03 M☉/yr and rising.

## Where the metal comes from
| Ore | Heavy elements | GJ per kg of heavy element | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gas in the Central Molecular Zone (within ~250 pc) | ~0.4–1.3 × 10⁶ M☉ standing, in 2–6 × 10⁷ M☉ of gas | ~0.01–0.1 to process; up to ~60 to collect (rough) | No star to take apart; dust already holds about half the heavy elements |
| The bar's inflow into the CMZ | ~0.01–0.06 M☉/yr, in 0.4–2.7 M☉/yr of gas | Same as the CMZ's gas | About the planning pace by itself, while the bar is fed |
| Living stars, nuclear stellar disk (mostly 50–250 pc) | ~1.9 × 10⁷ M☉ | ~4,000 (red dwarfs) to ~8,000 (Sun-like), minimum | Every element; dim, so lifting needs beamed power |
| White dwarfs, nuclear stellar disk | ~1–1.6 × 10⁸ M☉, almost all carbon and oxygen | ~4,000, minimum | Dark: all power beamed |
| White dwarfs, nuclear star cluster (within ~10 pc) | ~2.5–4 × 10⁶ M☉, carbon and oxygen | ~4,000, minimum | Decades of light-delay, not centuries |
| Neutron stars, stellar black holes | — | ~1.5 × 10⁷ (a sixth of rest mass) | Not ore; herd them out |

**Gas.**
- **Processing is cheap.** With no star to take apart, the work is handling ~47 kg of gas per kg of heavy element: cooling it to freeze out the ices and filtering out the dust, very roughly 0.2–2 MJ per kg of gas, or ~0.01–0.1 GJ per kg of heavy element.
- **Collecting is the bill.** Gas moving 10–50 km/s relative to a scoop costs ~0.05–1.3 GJ per kg of gas to bring to rest: ~2–60 GJ per kg of heavy element. Catching opposing streams against each other, as the Shade's catchers balance prograde and retrograde payloads, would cost much less.
- The CMZ's standing gas plus ~140 Myr of the bar's inflow at 1 M☉/yr covers the whole import. The range of inflow estimates gives 50–360 Myr.
- Collecting diffuse gas is its own problem: scoops in the dust lanes, or whole clouds as they settle into the CMZ.

**Living stars.**
- Metals are mixed through the whole star, so the whole star comes apart: ~46 kg of hydrogen and helium per kg of heavy element. The disk is metal-rich (Z ≈ 0.021; about three in five of its stars are above solar metallicity).
- The minimum is the star's binding energy, half its gravitational energy: ~1.7 × 10¹¹ J per kg of star for the Sun, ~9 × 10¹⁰ for a red dwarf, which is less centrally concentrated. Real lifting will cost more; plan on ~2×.
- **Self-lifting is slow.** On half its own light, a Sun-like star comes apart in ~60 Myr, a K dwarf in ~180 Myr and a red dwarf in ~700 Myr. The disk formed mostly ~8 Gyr ago and is now mostly old dwarfs.
- **Red giants are the cheap exception.** Their swollen envelopes, which hold most of their metal, lift for a few hundred GJ per kg of heavy element. But they're a small share of any old population.

**White dwarfs.**
- An old population keeps ~10–15% of its mass as white dwarfs: roughly 2:3 carbon to oxygen by mass, with a little neon.
- In the nuclear stellar disk that's ~1–1.6 × 10⁸ M☉: 5–9× all the heavy elements in its living stars, and ~25–40× the whole 30% import.
- **Per kg of heavy element they cost about what red dwarfs do,** since no hydrogen comes along.
  - The first kg off the surface costs ~9,000 GJ (escape speed ~4,300 km/s).
  - The dwarf expands as it loses mass, so the average is ~3,900.
- They're dark (~10⁻³–10⁻⁴ L☉), so every joule is beamed in.
- **Carbon and oxygen only.** Iron, magnesium, silicon, nitrogen, phosphorus and the rest still come from gas or living stars.

**The ore picks where the frontier is.** Gas and the disk's stars and white dwarfs sit 50–250 pc out, centuries away by light. The old cluster's white dwarfs are within ~10 pc, a few decades away.

## Energy per kg of heavy element
| Step | GJ per kg of heavy element |
|---|---|
| Take apart a Sun-like star (minimum) | ~8,000 |
| Take apart a red dwarf or a white dwarf (minimum) | ~4,000 |
| Process gas: cool and filter (rough) | ~0.01–0.1 |
| Collect diffuse gas, if it has to be matched in speed (rough) | ~2–60 |
| Launch inward (cancel ~165 km/s of orbit) | ~14 |
| Shed on arrival at the Shade (balanced catch) | ~90, released |
| Fabricate into habitat (energy-budget.md) | ~0.1 |

- **Local energy propels it easily:** launching is ~0.2% of lifting. The discarded hydrogen and helium are free reaction mass, thrown the other way.
- **Powering the lifting:**
  - **The star's own light:** free but slow (above).
  - **Beamed from the Lantern:** distance is nearly free for beams (full-swarm.md).
    - At the planning pace, lifting from stars takes ~1–2 × 10³⁴ W at the minimum: ~0.2–0.4% of the mature civilization's electricity.
    - That's a few percent early in the build-out, and perhaps twice as much in practice.
    - Once growth tracks the hole, it's a few watts per person.
    - The frontier is then on the Lantern's beam like any raft.
  - **Fusing some of the lifted hydrogen,** if the setting has reactors. Burning ~0.1% of it pays for lifting everything, and the frontier needs no beam.
    - But the disk's ~6 × 10⁸ M☉ of hydrogen, fused over a Gyr, would give ~2 × 10³⁷ W, more than the hole at 30%.
    - That cuts against the energy-limited premise, so it's a choice to make deliberately.
- **Bulk shipping multiplies the trip by ~47:** ~660 GJ to launch and ~4,000 GJ to shed per kg of metal, rivaling the lifting.

## The drop
Payloads leave circular orbits by cancelling nearly all their orbital speed, then fall almost straight in.

| Launch radius | 100 pc | 150 pc | 200 pc |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orbital speed (≈ launch Δv) | ~150 km/s | ~165 km/s | ~170 km/s |
| Fall to the Shade | ~0.9 Myr | ~1.2 Myr | ~1.5 Myr |
| Arrival speed | ~415 km/s | ~435 km/s | ~455 km/s |
| Energy to shed, balanced catch | ~72 GJ/kg | ~83 GJ/kg | ~91 GJ/kg |

- Across the mass models: arrival at 400–490 km/s, falls of 0.75–1.65 Myr, and 70–105 GJ/kg to shed. Shade orbits run ~160 km/s at 1 pc.
- **Aiming.**
  - A few km/s of leftover sideways speed at launch decides which way a payload swings past its closest point. So the frontier chooses prograde or retrograde arrival almost for free.
  - Reaching a catcher after a ~1 Myr fall through a lumpy potential takes terminal guidance: a little hydrogen and a thruster, like everything else in the Shade.
  - The Lantern is downrange. Swinging past at the Shade's radius takes ~2–4.5 km/s of sideways speed at launch; ~0.4–0.7 km/s less sends a payload into the Lantern at ~450 km/s. So the frontier's aim is a matter of trust (governance.md).
- **Heating.** In the ember's light a bare payload warms to ~90 K at 10 pc, ~200 K at 2 pc and ~280 K at the Shade.
  - Water ice loses ~7 m a year at 200 K, so ices fly in reflective jackets (~110 K at 2 pc, ~160 K at the Shade).
  - Carbon flies reduced, as graphite or diamond.
- Routes skirt dense CMZ clouds, which would erode payloads at these speeds.
- As the hole and the Shade grow, the fall deepens a little and the catch moves outward with the Shade.

## The catch
**Momentum, not energy, is the design problem.** Each catch shoves the catcher. At its closest point a payload moves at ~440 km/s along or against the catcher's ~160 km/s orbit:

| Arrival | Relative speed | Energy to shed | Effect on the catcher |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prograde | ~280 km/s | ~39 GJ/kg | Pushed ahead; orbit raised |
| Retrograde | ~600 km/s | ~180 GJ/kg | Pushed back; orbit lowered |
| Radial (unaimed) | ~470 km/s | ~110 GJ/kg | Pushed inward and slowed |
| **~2.1 kg prograde per kg retrograde** | — | **~84 GJ/kg average** | **None** |

- Balanced, the catcher's orbit is untouched, and the energy shed is just the payload's excess orbital energy.
- **The payload can't keep any of it.** ~85 GJ/kg is ~1 keV per nucleon, ~11,000× what it takes to vaporize iron, so even 0.01% landing in the payload boils it. Induction brakes, which heat the payload in proportion to slip, are out.
- **Solid catch.** Payloads carry their own magnets, and a synchronous linear generator brakes them without slip.
  - That takes ~1,000 km of track and ~5 s at 10⁴ g (~10,000 km at 10³ g).
  - Most of the energy returns as electricity. The losses heat the track, which carries the radiators.
- **Plasma catch.** Vaporize the payload on purpose.
  - Singly ionizing it costs well under 1% of the arrival energy, and each atom already carries tens of keV, about what the calutrons used to separate uranium.
  - A magnetic mass filter sorts the ions by mass, and electrostatic decelerators recover their energy.
  - The catch becomes the refinery.
- **What the energy is worth:**
  - Locally, a windfall: ~850× the ~0.1 GJ/kg needed to turn the metal into habitat, so catchers run their own foundries and export the rest.
  - Globally, a rounding error: at the planning pace the stream carries ~2 × 10³² W, ~4 × 10⁻⁵ of the Lantern's output.

## Processing: sort there, build here
**Don't ship bulk.**
- Stars are ~98% hydrogen and helium, so bulk moves ~47× the mass, at the trip costs above.
- **The center can't take the gas.** At the planning pace, bulk would pour ~2 M☉/yr into the central parsecs, ~100× what the hole eats at 30%. In 100 Myr that's ~45 hole masses, ~10× the cluster's stored hydrogen and helium.
  - Gas beyond ~1,000 r_g fragments into stars (sgr-a-scope.md).
  - Dense ionized gas absorbs the Lantern's microwaves (full-swarm.md).
  - That much mass would run the Shade's dynamics.

**Sorting is nearly free.** As lifted gas cools, heavy elements condense out by temperature, the way dust forms in a red giant's wind:

| Stream | Share of heavy elements | Condenses at | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rock and metal | ~34% | Mostly ~1,300–1,700 K | Iron, magnesium, silicon, nickel, calcium, aluminium, sulfur (as sulfide, ~660 K), ~¼ of the oxygen |
| Ices | ~57% | ~20–180 K | The rest of the oxygen (as water), carbon, nitrogen, argon |
| Neon | ~9% | ~9 K | Left behind with the hydrogen and helium |

- By mass, a solar mix of heavy elements is oxygen 43%, carbon 18%, iron 10%, neon 9%, magnesium 5%, nitrogen 5%, silicon 5%, sulfur 2%, and everything else ~2.5%.
- Only ~17% are metals in the engineer's sense. Nearly half is oxygen and a sixth carbon: water, air and carbon structure, which drums want most.
- **Ship sorted slugs:** rock and metal; carbon as graphite or diamond; oxygen and nitrogen as jacketed ices or as ceramics (oxides, nitrides).

**Don't build there.**
- Anything designed at the frontier arrives a million years out of date, on specs already centuries stale by light.
- Only dense, simple slugs survive a ~1 keV-per-nucleon catch.
- Fabrication energy is trivial next to what the catch delivers at the core.

## Growth and the frontier
- **Growth is ~10⁻⁹–10⁻⁸ a year:** ~2 × 10⁻⁸ during the build-out, ~4 × 10⁻⁹ while the hole spins up, and ~1.4 × 10⁻⁹ after, as the hole doubles every ~0.5 Gyr. That's a part or two per million per lifetime, so the population is effectively closed.
  - 0.4%/yr, an early assumption in energy-budget.md, would need ~18,000 M☉/yr, ~450,000× the planning pace.
- **The price of growth** (energy-budget.md's open question).
  - A person's 6.7 t from stars costs ~25–55 PJ at the minimum, ~50,000× the ~0.75 TJ to build their habitat.
  - From gas, ~0.1 TJ to ~0.4 PJ, depending on how much collecting costs. At the low end that's less than building the habitat, so a gas-fed frontier is limited by how fast gas arrives, not by energy.
  - energy-budget.md's 0.1 kWe growth line now pays mostly for metal: about that much per person at the start of the build-out, and a few watts once growth tracks the hole.
- **Core and frontier depend on each other, but only slowly.** The core pays in light, the frontier in metal. Cut off from the beam, a frontier still lives on starlight: the nuclear stellar disk's stars could support ~10³¹ people, ~1–3% of the core, so a cut slows its mining but starves no one. With fusion it wouldn't need the beam even to mine. And ~1 Myr of metal is always falling, so an embargo wouldn't bite for a megayear. Neither side can coerce the other on any human timescale (governance.md).
- **Fuel after ~820 Myr.** When the cluster's hydrogen runs out, the hole has grown ~3.5× and eats ~0.03 M☉/yr. A simulation puts the Galaxy's own inflow from the CMZ to the central parsecs at about that today, so for a while the Galaxy feeds the hole by itself; as the hole keeps growing, the stream carries fuel too.

## Open questions
- **Element-by-element budget:** how far drums can lean on carbon and oxygen, and whether nitrogen, phosphorus or another element binds first. That decides how much white-dwarf ore is usable.
- **Taking a white dwarf apart:** beam-driven winds, or tidal shredding by one of the central parsec's stellar black holes.
- **Fusion:** whether the setting has reactors, and if so, why the frontier's hydrogen doesn't rival the hole.
- **Gas scoops:** collecting diffuse CMZ gas and the bar's inflow, and whether opposing streams can be caught against each other cheaply.
- **Solid or plasma catch,** and where catchers sit among the Shade's rafts and beams.
- **The stripped gas:** released metal-free, the leftover hydrogen and helium would cool poorly and likely form unusually massive stars, whose supernovae would make new metal.
- **Epoch:** whether the bar still feeds the CMZ in the setting's era. If not, stars and white dwarfs are the ore.
- **The long run:** how long the Galaxy's inflow and the frontier can feed a hole that keeps doubling.
- **Who runs the frontier:** outposts centuries of light-delay out, mining on the core's beam but able to live on their stars' light (governance.md).

## Method
- **Mass model:**
  - Sgr A* (4.3 × 10⁶ M☉).
  - The Shade (5.7 × 10⁶ M☉ with 3 km drums, a uniform shell at 0.9–1.25 pc). The first version used 6.7 × 10⁶, which put arrivals ~2% faster.
  - The nuclear star cluster's mass, kept as stored gas (2.5 × 10⁷ M☉, Plummer, 4 pc).
  - The nuclear stellar disk as a thin exponential disk (1.05 × 10⁹ M☉, scale length 88.6 pc), evaluated in its plane.
  - A Hernquist bulge (5 × 10⁹–1.5 × 10¹⁰ M☉, scale 0.6–1 kpc) bracketing the inner rotation curve (~140–200 km/s at 100–200 pc).
  - Dropping the cluster's mass lowers arrival speeds by ~15%.
- **Drops:** radial falls to 1 pc, from rest after cancelling the orbital speed.
- **Catch:** balanced energy = ½(v_arrival² − v_orbit²); geometries at 440 and 160 km/s.
- **Lifting minimum:** binding energy = |U|/2 (virial, γ = 5/3).
  - Sun: |U| ≈ 6.9 × 10⁴¹ J.
  - Red dwarf (0.3 M☉, 0.28 R☉) and white dwarf (0.6 M☉, 0.0126 R☉): n = 1.5 polytropes.
  - Heavy-element fraction 0.021 for the disk's stars and the gas; white dwarfs ~99%.
- **Self-lifting:** binding energy ÷ (½ × luminosity).
- **Composition:** Asplund et al. 2009 photospheric abundances; condensation temperatures after Lodders 2003; ~23% of the oxygen in rock.
- **White dwarfs:** ~10–15% of an old population's mass. After 13 Gyr about a quarter of the surviving stellar mass is remnants, ~73% of them white dwarfs (Conroy 2013). The disk formed mostly ~8 Gyr ago.
- **Heating:** a blackbody sphere in the ember's light (1.6 × 10³⁷ W); jackets with absorptivity 0.1 and emissivity 0.9; ice vapour pressure after Murphy & Koop 2005.
- **Gas (rough):** processing cools ~47 kg of molecular gas per kg of heavy element from ~50 K to ~20 K with cryocoolers, plus filtering out the dust. Collecting brings gas 10–50 km/s away to rest, ½Δv² per kg of gas.
- **Growth at 30%:** full-swarm.md's model (Bardeen spin-up from 0.9, capped at 0.998). The cluster's hydrogen and helium (~2.1 × 10⁷ M☉ after setting aside its white dwarfs) is shared between fuel and shields.
