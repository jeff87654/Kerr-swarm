# Open drums: the free and flourishing drums

*Design notes from a working session, Oct 2026. The bright counterpart to people.md's counted drums: nation drums that hold births to deaths without coercion, keep their rings open both ways, and live well on 2 kWe. What makes them work, what they're like, and how many there are. Builds on people.md (holdings, birth systems, counted drums, the right of the Glide), economy.md (the dividend, the price of an hour, places), daily-life.md (prices, light, travel), raft-habitat.md (the Hollow and the Spindle), cuisine.md (the calendar) and ashgrove.md (Herb Row). Jeff's decisions of 31 Oct 2026: open drums keep a Margin under par; about a quarter of drums are open at a quiet time; guest-right is a compact among open drums that most steady drums also join; Chervil on Herb Row is the example; the customs below are open-drum customs, not Shade-wide ones. The names (open drums, the Margin, the Ledger, guest-right, the wander year, tables and aunts, the Quiet, the Long Flight, Homecoming, light-painting, block courts) are proposals. Figures are for a 200 M nation drum with 120-year lives.*

## Summary
- **An open drum holds births to deaths without coercion and keeps its rings open both ways.** Anyone may leave; any traveller may stay three nights. It runs a little under par on purpose, posts its numbers in public, and lets its people make the big choices.
- **The physics makes the floor high and the good things cheap.** Nobody in any drum goes hungry or dark. The Glide, hot baths, slow travel, copies of anything, colour, song and conversation cost nearly nothing. What a drum can't buy more of is metal, light and in-hull heat, and joy doesn't need much of any.
- **Time is the one plentiful luxury.** Paying someone for their time moves claims, not energy (economy.md). A drum whose people spend more of their wallets on each other gets cooks, musicians, teachers and company at no extra cost to the beam.
- **Their secret is slack.** A drum a few percent under par has spare homes, a longer reserve and room to say yes: to guests, to refugees, to young couples. Most open drums got their Margin from a past Count that overshot, and chose to keep it.
- **About a quarter of drums, ~20 in every glide metro.** They need no special physics, only calm centuries to build trust and a Margin.
- **The example is Chervil,** across the rows from Sorrel on Herb Row, two glides from Rue.

## What joy costs at 2 kWe
A wallet is 21.6 kWh a day (daily-life.md). Almost everything people remember costs less than a cup of coffee.

| Pleasure | kWh | Why so cheap |
|---|---|---|
| Glide to a friend in the next drum | ~0 | The car falls free; the catch gets its energy back (raft-transport.md) |
| A long hot bath | ~0.05 | The heat is the food plant's waste; only pumping costs |
| An evening of choir, a string quartet, a play | ~0 | People's time costs claims, not energy; the hall's light is commons |
| Any song, book, film or design made in the Shade | ~0 | Copies are free; culture arrives from 10¹³ rafts by laser |
| A night flying the Spindle | ~0.01 | Under 3% g at the axis; a hand lamp of a watt or two |
| Re-dyeing a coat for a festival | ~0.1 | Colour weighs nothing; fashion lives in surfaces |
| A cycler anywhere on the raft at 2 km/s | ~1.1 | Trips cost speed squared, not distance: 0.1 AU takes ~87 days for the same 1.1 kWh as a short hop |
| A meal cooked by a person, in a hall of 400 | ~3.5 | A Vat-table dinner (2.5) plus a share of the cook's hours |
| A wander year across the raft, twelve legs | ~13 | Less than a new pair of shoes; the cost is a year of your time |
| *For comparison:* a new sofa | 1,000 | Mass is the dear thing: ~25 kWh a kilogram |

- **The dear things are metal, light and in-hull heat:** children, floor, new mass, bright gardens. They're fixed in every drum, open or not.
- **So flourishing is a matter of what a drum does with the cheap things,** and how fairly it shares the dear ones. That's what Mill hoped a stationary state would free people for: "as much room for improving the Art of Living, and much more likelihood of its being improved, when minds ceased to be engrossed by the art of getting on."

## What makes a drum open
### A Margin
- **An open drum runs 2–4% under par.** Chervil holds 3%: ~6 M places with nobody in them.
- **What slack buys.** Beam and metal follow places, so each resident gets ~2.06 kWe instead of 2.0, and the 55-day reserve stretches to ~57. More useful are the empty homes: guest flats, refuge for people fleeing dark drums, studios, and first homes lent to young couples.
- **The Margin is not for children.** That's the one hard norm. Births still follow deaths, and spending the Margin on births takes a supermajority, which a drum grants once after a disaster and then rebuilds over a generation. It's a granary, not a field.
- **Where it comes from.** Nearly every drum has counted at some point (people.md). A stop that runs a few years too long leaves a drum under par, and most refill the gap. Open drums are the ones that voted to keep it.
- **It's cheap to keep.** Once made, a Margin costs nothing a year: births equal deaths, as anywhere. One generation paid for it, long ago.

### Children without counting
- **Holdings, warmly run.** Places pass to heirs, as in most drums (people.md). A family can have a third child on thin shares and nobody fines it.
- **Marry young; have children when a place frees.** In most holdings drums people marry late because a household needs places. Open drums lend Margin flats to couples, so marriage isn't gated, only children are, and a couple waits for a death in the family with a home already made.
- **The gift of a place.** Someone with no children names an heir. In open drums the custom is to name a young couple outside one's own family, and say so in public. It's the grandest gift there is.
- **Tables and aunts.** A child has a table: parents plus three or four adults who stood at the naming, usually childless, called aunt whatever their sex. At 120-year lives about one person in seven is a child, so most adults aren't raising one, and the table spreads children across them. Okinawa's *moai*, lifelong circles of friends, are the nearest Earth echo.
- **Childless lives are full lives.** Aunts, crews, stewards, artists and hall cooks have standing, not consolation prizes. This is what people.md says custom needs in the Shade, and open drums are where it works.
- **Two names.** Children are named for the dead whose places they take (economy.md). Open drums read both names on the Ledger, and the dead's family brings a gift to the naming. Every birth closes someone's funeral.

### An economy of each other
- **Machines make everything; people pay for people.** A drum's energy is fixed, but the share of wallets spent on human time is a choice. Chervil spends ~40% against the planning case's 25% (economy.md). Money incomes rise from ~10,500 to ~13,200 kWh a year and an hour costs ~6 kWh, on exactly the same beam.
- **What that buys:** halls where a person cooks, schools where a person teaches, live music every night somewhere in every block, tailors, nurses who stay, and company for the old.
- **Cooked halls.** Most open drums run their Mill Halls with a paid cook and volunteers, one long table a block. Eating alone is possible and unusual.

### Voice at three scales
- **The block court.** A block of ~3,000 (Ashgrove's size) meets once a week in its atrium to settle noise, heat, rooms and Hollow swaps, out loud and in minutes. Valencia's Water Tribunal has met this way at the cathedral door every Thursday for about a thousand years.
- **The assembly by lot.** A drum of 200 M can't meet, so each year it draws ~1,000 residents by lot to set the commons. Athens chose most officials this way; Ireland's Citizens' Assembly (2016–18) did it with 99.
- **The Booking Day question.** When the beam is booked, every resident votes on one choice the assembly can't settle: another hour of sunline, a bigger hall fund, a brighter dim wood.
- **Ostrom's rules for a lasting commons fit a drum closely:** clear membership (places), local rules, rules chosen by their users, monitoring by peers (the meters and the Ledger), mild graduated sanctions, cheap courts, and nested layers (block, drum, raft).

### Open both ways
- **Out:** the right of the Glide (people.md). Anyone may leave, and a swap takes days.
- **In: guest-right.** Any traveller from a drum that grants the same may stay three nights in a Margin flat and eat at the halls. Open drums keep a compact of guest-right across the raft, and most steady drums join it. It nets to zero, since every drum is host and sender.
- **The wander year.** Guest-right plus cyclers at ~1 kWh a leg means a young person can cross a 0.1 AU raft and back, sleeping in a new drum each week, for ~13 kWh. Most young people in open drums go at about twenty. Coming home is a festival.
- **Refuge.** Open drums take people fleeing rungs 4–6 into Margin places (people.md's refuge swaps).

### Against Earth's happiest countries
The World Happiness Report explains most of the gap between countries with six factors. An open drum does well on all six, and every drum's floor is above Earth's on the first two.

| Factor | Every drum | What an open drum adds |
|---|---|---|
| Income | An equal dividend; nobody hungry or dark | The Margin's extra 3%; more paid human work |
| Healthy life expectancy | Proofread bodies, clinics, ~120-year lives | Company for the old |
| Someone to count on | Households, the block | Tables and aunts, cooked halls, block courts |
| Freedom to make life choices | The right of the Glide on most rafts | Marry young, childless with honour, guest-right, the wander year |
| Generosity | Gifts reckoned in kWh | Place gifts, refuge, ~40% of wallets spent on each other |
| Trust, low corruption | Meters and registers make cheating visible | The open Ledger, the assembly by lot, the block courts |

## Freedoms
- **To come and go.** Glide out any day; stay three nights anywhere that grants guest-right; cross the raft for a kWh.
- **To love whom you like and marry young.** Anyone in the Shade is a possible partner (people.md), and a lent flat waits.
- **To have children or not,** with standing either way, and a third on thin shares.
- **To refuse proofreading.** The clinic still sees the birth and registers the child; a wild-born child in an open drum is a child, not a case.
- **To work or not.** The dividend is never docked. Most people work anyway, for standing and for each other.
- **To go dark.** Anyone may switch off their worlds and be unreachable; the black thread means the same in every drum (ashgrove.md).
- **To be heard.** A block court every week, a chance of the lot every year, a vote every Booking Day.

## Culture
### Art lives where it's free
- **Light-painting.** Brightness costs energy, but colour and timing barely do. The high art of open drums is the Hollow's dawn and dusk: three-hour sequences of colour on the sunline and the land, composed by lamp-painters and played once. A great dawn costs no more than an ordinary one.
- **Heliox music.** Sound runs ~1.8× faster in heliox, so pipes are long (Wren's flute in ashgrove.md is 1.2 m), and voices keep their pitch but brighten as the throat's resonances rise. Every block has a choir.
- **Low-g dance and flight.** Terraces at a third of a g for leaping reels, the flying halls by day, the whole Spindle by night. Coriolis breaks every throw and leap by heading, so dance and sport are full of angles (spinball).
- **Visible mending.** Things are mended and inherited (daily-life.md). In open drums the mend is shown, as in Japan's *kintsugi*, and a coat's seams tell its owners' story.
- **The long letter.** A friend on a passing raft is hours away by light; one across the Shade is years. People write long, carefully, to be read once, and some drums hold readings when an old letter lands.

### The calendar
| Festival | What happens | What pays for it |
|---|---|---|
| The Quiet | The day before Booking Day the whole drum goes dark: no worlds, no messages. People sit in the halls and talk | Saves each person ~9.6 kWh of compute |
| Booking Day | The beam is booked; the Ledger is read; the assembly gives its split; one question is voted on. The Quiet's savings become a feast in every hall | ~9.6 kWh a head: a steak, a salad, half an orchard apple, cheese, wine |
| The Long Flight | That night the sunline stays dark and the Spindle opens end to end. Hundreds of thousands fly the axis with hand lamps, in shifts till lamp-dawn | A watt or two each |
| Knotnight | The ring era's winter in one evening: lamps down, the larder made together (ashgrove.md) | The larder is drill and gift at once |
| Homecoming | Wanderers back from their year are met at the station ring by their block and their table | Free |
| Namings | A child's table stands up for them; the dead's family brings a gift | Usually a remade heirloom |

- **The Quiet's heat is no problem.** Moving 9.6 kWh a head from data halls into the hull for a day overloads the hull's radiators by half for that day, but the drum's ~27 Mt of inside water soaks it up with a rise well under a tenth of a degree, and sheds it within days.

## Kinds of open drum
| Kind | Spends its choices on | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| Hall drums (Chervil) | Human time: cooks, music, teaching | Loud, crowded, fed by people |
| Wood drums | Dim woods instead of bright squares; long silences; many childless callings | A monastery the size of a nation |
| Fair drums | Light and spectacle, with the rents returned to everyone | Lovage, if Lovage shared |
| Maker drums | Craft schools, fabrication allowances, a famous thing (chairs, flutes, starters) | A workshop town people glide to for one thing |

## Chervil
- **Where.** Across the rows from Sorrel on Herb Row, Merrow (ashgrove.md), with Rue two glides away.
- **History.** A holdings drum that counted ~340 years ago after a radiator fault, stopped births for a decade too long, and came out 3% under par. Its assembly voted to keep the gap and called it the Margin. Its old Count board hangs in the Ledger hall beside the Ledger.
- **The Ledger** at each station ring shows places, people, the Margin, the year's births and deaths, guests that night, and the names: each newborn beside the dead whose place they took. On Booking Day it reads 200,000,000 places, 194,060,000 people, a Margin of 5,940,000, 1,615,000 born and 1,617,000 died, and ~410,000 guests.
- **How it differs from its neighbours.** Sorrel allots its fixed goods by age and runs a pool with giftable rights; Lovage auctions both. Chervil is a holdings drum that gives its Hollow hours by lottery and in festivals, lends Margin flats to the young, and spends ~40% of wallets on people.
- **Gin** is taxed lightly, with thiamine, and sold only in the halls after six, where someone is always cooking. Chervil's methanol deaths are among the lowest on Herb Row.
- **What Herb Row says.** Sorrel thinks Chervil is lovely and a little pleased with itself. Lovage thinks it dim and charming. Rue's councillors come to the Ledger every Booking Day and take notes. Rue is ~11 years from par, and some of them want a Margin.

### Booking Day in Chervil
*Marit Vell (26) is a hall cook, four months back from her wander year. Jory is her partner. Oona (81) is one of Marit's aunts and a lamp-painter.*

**06:00.** Yesterday was the Quiet, and Marit's worlds are still off. She takes the Spindle lift up to the Hollow's back terraces with Jory, because this morning's dawn is Oona's, a year in the making. It starts not white but the brown-violet of the River in a Window, which Oona saw once, at eight, and has tried to paint ever since. It warms through rose, and for twenty minutes the far bank of the Hollow, three kilometres overhead, is gold while the near side is still dark. Then it's an ordinary morning. The terraces clap. Oona says it was too slow in the middle.

**08:30.** The Ledger at Station Ring 2. The Margin line reads 5,940,000, down 20,000 for the year's refuge places, and people cheer it anyway. The names scroll past. Marit finds her grandfather's, from two years ago, and beside it her own, because she holds his place now. Two of Rue's councillors stand near the back with notebooks. Somebody brings them tea.

**10:00.** The booking arrives: Merrow's beam for the year, flat, as it nearly always is. The assembly reads its split and the question for the vote: an extra hour of full sun in the Hollow, or a larger hall fund. Marit votes hall fund, obviously. Jory votes sun, and won't say why.

**13:00.** Marit's hall seats four hundred. She has eleven volunteers, nine of them over seventy, and a menu built from the Quiet's savings: cultivated steak with herb butter, a salad cut generously for once, aged cheese, the Lovage sourdough everyone pretends Chervil makes, and an orchard apple cut crosswise for every two people, so everyone sees the star. Three guests are wanderers from the far side of Merrow, on guest-right. One is from a drum Marit slept in last year. They've never met; they know the same baker.

**17:00.** The vote comes in: hall fund, by four points. Jory admits he voted sun so the dawns would be longer for Oona's next one.

**22:00.** The sunline stays dark and the Spindle opens end to end for the Long Flight. Marit and Jory take wing-sleeves and hand lamps and go up with their block. At the axis you weigh nothing, and the line you'd have to keep away from by day is a dark cable down the middle of the air. As far as the end wall, almost four kilometres off, small lights drift along the axis like a slow river. Somewhere in it a choir is singing in heliox, high and strange. Marit tells Jory she'd like to marry in the spring. He says he's already asked for a Margin flat.

**02:00.** Oona didn't fly; she watched from the elders' terrace with a glass of gin. Marit asks whether the dawn was really too slow. "In the middle," Oona says. "Next year I'll fix it." She's eighty-one. In Chervil, that's a plan.

## How common
A drum becomes open if it goes ~500 years without a serious shock and chooses to build and keep a Margin. The shock rates are people.md's.

| | Few | **Canon** | Many |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serious overshoot or shock, per drum | 1 / 300 yr | 1 / 1,000 yr | 1 / 3,000 yr |
| Share of drums 500+ years past one | 19% | 61% | 85% |
| Of those, share that keep a Margin and open ways | 20% | 40% | 60% |
| Share of drums that are open | ~4% | **~25%** | ~50% |
| In a glide metro of ~80 drums | ~3 | ~20 | ~40 |

| At a quiet time | Share | Like |
|---|---|---|
| Open drums | ~25% | Chervil |
| Steady drums: decent, near par, no Margin | ~60% | Sorrel, Lovage, Tansy |
| Thin drums: overspent holdings, not counting | ~13% | Rue before its blight |
| Counted drums | ~1% | Rue now |

- **Everyone has open drums within a short glide,** about twenty of the eighty in their metro, to swap into, visit or marry into.
- **The Earth check.** In the 2024 World Happiness Report, 11 of 143 countries scored 7 or more out of 10. A quarter is higher, because every drum starts with a floor of food, health and shelter most of Earth never had, and drums can copy each other's institutions one glide away.
- **Waves run the other way too.** A raft-wide Dark spends many Margins at once and drops the share for a generation or two. Open drums recover first, since they had slack and trust going in.
- **A drum's history** is a few turns of this cycle per 3,000 years: counted drums become steady, steady ones open, and a Dark knocks some back.

## What open drums struggle with
- **Sorting.** Kind, sociable people swap in, and neighbours lose some of their best. Guest-right and the wander year spread the habits back out.
- **Smugness.** "Chervil manners" is Herb Row for a certain kind of pity aimed at Rue.
- **Raiding the Margin.** After a hard year there's always a faction for spending it on births. The supermajority holds most of the time.
- **The leisure problem.** Keynes thought that once the economic problem was solved, people's real problem would be how to live "wisely and agreeably and well." Open drums are the Shade's best answer, and not all of them get it right: gin, worlds that swallow whole decades, and the odd drum that confuses calm with standing still.

## Open questions
- Whether orbit families or whole rafts ever become open in the same sense, with a Margin of places held at raft scale.
- How a Margin is shared out after a raft-wide Dark: to the drum's own households, to refugees, or to neighbours.
- What the Lantern's beam-by-place rule makes of a drum's empty places over very long times.

## Method
- Chervil: 200 M places, 3% Margin → 194.06 M people. Beam per head 2.0 × 200 ÷ 194.06 ≈ 2.06 kWe. Deaths 194.06 M ÷ 120 yr ≈ 1.62 M a year.
- Time economy (economy.md): money income ≈ 7,900 kWh ÷ (1 − share): 25% → ~10,500; 40% → ~13,200. At 1,000 hours sold per adult, an hour ≈ 6.2 kWh at 40%.
- Cycler fare at 2 km/s ≈ 1.1 kWh a trip at any distance (daily-life.md). 0.1 AU ≈ 1.5 × 10⁷ km ÷ 2 km/s ≈ 87 days. Twelve legs ≈ 13 kWh.
- Guests: if each drum's ~1.6 M twenty-year-olds spend ~90 guest-nights on a wander year, each drum hosts ~1.4 × 10⁸ guest-nights a year, ~400,000 a night, against ~6 M Margin places.
- The Quiet: 9.6 kWh × 194 M ≈ 1.9 GWh ≈ 6.7 TJ moved into the hull for a day. Inside water ~0.14 t a head (~27 Mt; habitat-design.md): ΔT ≈ 6.7 × 10¹² ÷ (2.7 × 10¹⁰ kg × 4,186 J/kg·K) ≈ 0.06 K, before the hull's other mass.
- Long Flight: Spindle volume π × 75² × 3,750 m ≈ 6.6 × 10⁷ m³; at ~100 m³ a flyer, ~660,000 at once.
- Prevalence: share ≈ e^(−500 yr × shock rate) × share that keep a Margin. Canon: e^(−0.5) × 0.4 ≈ 0.24.
