# People: bodies and births

*Design notes from a working session, Oct 2026. What the Shade's people are like as a population: who breeds with whom, what the drums do to their bodies, how harmful mutations are cleared, and how drums hold births to deaths. Builds on raft-habitat.md (gravity bands, light), raft-transport.md (raft sizes, travel), economy.md (the place, the commons), governance.md (what drums decide) and full-swarm.md. Jeff's decisions of 30–31 Oct 2026: medical engineering is the norm but not a universal law; each drum gets a fixed allotment of beam and runs its own affairs; places mostly pass to heirs; a few people emigrate, but it's hard. Jeff's decisions of 31 Oct 2026 on coercive drums (counted drums): about 1% of drums at a quiet time, in waves after raft-wide shocks; most rafts guarantee the right of the Glide; the raft answers with sanctions and holds the beam back only for the worst; the darkest drums are rare and shunned; Rue on Herb Row is the example. Jeff's decisions of 1 Nov 2026: places are household land, like farms; children and young adults live in a parent's or grandparent's place, and most people get their own around 30; each person's wallet comes with a share, freed by a death in the family; a child born ahead of a death has no share until one frees. The names (proofreading, holdings, places and shares, the shareless, the wild-born, counted drums, the Count, the fitting, the right of the Glide) are proposals. Biology figures are approximate.*

## Summary
- **Chance does almost nothing; selection and medicine do everything.** At ~10²⁰ people a raft, a neutral variant would take ~10²⁰ generations to spread by luck. Rafts in the same conditions evolve almost identically.
- **Fixed families switch off most natural selection.** With births held to deaths, nearly every adult who reaches parenthood has about the same number of children. Unchecked, each child's ~1–2 new harmful mutations would pile up, and health would fall ~1% a generation.
- **Proofreading clears them.** The norm everywhere: every embryo is sequenced and its new mutations are put back to match its parents, along with known disease variants. It's a norm, not a law, and some drums refuse it.
- **So people look like all of humanity mixed together, for as long as the civilization lasts.** The average is medium brown, every old feature recombined, the range as wide as Earth's. Nobody tans, jaws are small, bones are light and nearly everyone wears corrected eyes. Any emigrant can marry anyone, anywhere in the Shade.
- **Places are household land; shares are people's.** A nation drum of 200 M has ~150 M places, like farms. Children and young adults live in a parent's or grandparent's place, and most people get their own around 30, usually a great-grandparent's. Each person's wallet comes with a share, freed by a death in the family and passed to a newborn. A child born ahead of a death has no share until one frees, so families that have extra children live thinner.
- **Drums choose their own birth systems.** Each gets a fixed allotment of beam, so a drum that overbreeds hurts only itself. Most run on inheritance and custom; some keep a pool with formal birth rights (Sorrel and Lovage in ashgrove.md); about 1% coerce.
- **Counted drums license births and enforce it.** Most fit a contraceptive implant at fourteen and stop births for a decade or two; the darkest are rare and shunned. Whether people can glide out decides how dark they get.
- **Open drums are the bright side.** About a quarter of drums hold births to deaths freely, keep a small Margin under par and open their rings both ways (open-drums.md).
- **The clinics see nearly every birth.** Children born outside them, the wild-born, are unproofread and often shareless.

## Who breeds with whom
| Level | People | Gene flow | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nation drum | ~2 × 10⁸ | Most partners come from inside the drum | Fully mixed within a few dozen generations |
| Raft | ~6 × 10¹⁹ (0.1 AU) to ~1.5 × 10²² (1.6 AU cap), ~600,000 to ~8 million drums across | Glides reach the next drums free; cyclers make far moves cheap but slow | Without proofreading, regional looks could last for millions of years if few people move far |
| Shade | ~10³³ in 10¹¹–10¹³ rafts | Almost none: rafts pass at 100–300 km/s, and emigrants come only as swaps | Each raft is a gene pool on its own. Medical standards travel by laser, like recipes |

- **Every possible single-letter mutation turns up in each drum about once a generation.** Nothing waits on luck; whatever is selected for spreads.
- **Rafts start alike.** A raft founded by whole drums carries almost all its parent's variation, so founder effects are negligible.

## What the drum does to a body
Most of what the drum changes is what it stops asking for.

| Factor | Within a lifetime | Over generations, without proofreading |
|---|---|---|
| Gravity: children raised at 0.85–0.98 g, most adults at 0.6–0.85 g (raft-habitat.md) | Lighter bones, less muscle, slightly longer spines | Tolerance of 1 g slowly erodes |
| Light: lamps with no UV, most hours indoors | Nobody tans; near-universal myopia from short sightlines | Skin colour is free of selection and follows taste |
| Air: heliox in warm rooms, oxygen ~16–17 kPa by dawn (cuisine.md) | A nightly touch of altitude | Weak selection for highland-style oxygen handling |
| Diet: complete, soft, synthetic (food-system.md) | Small jaws, crowded teeth | Unused: starvation resistance, bitter taste, digestive specialisation |
| Disease: dense drums, ~45% of residents glide out and back daily | One virus reaches 200 M people fast | Immune genes keep the strongest selection; defences against extinct Earth diseases fade |
| Radiation: ~20 mSv/yr (habitat-design.md) | More cancer late in life | ~0.6 Sv a generation could raise the inherited mutation rate by up to about half |

## Proofreading
- **What it is.** Every embryo, or the gametes before it, is sequenced and compared with both parents. Each new mutation (~70 a child) is put back, and known disease variants are repaired. Drums can also bank young gametes, so older parents pass on fewer new mutations.
- **What it does.** It clears mutation load completely without selection, and it stops new variation. The gene pool becomes a fixed, endlessly reshuffled sample of founding humanity, too big for chance to wear down. Mate choice and uneven family sizes can still shift common variants, but only within humanity's old range.
- **The line creeps.** What counts as disease is a choice made millions of times: myopia, crooked teeth, baldness, allergies, short stature. Over ~1 Myr most drums drift toward a wider definition, and people end up a little healthier-looking and a little more alike. Some drums hold the line firmly.
- **One species.** With nothing new added, every raft keeps the same genes, so the Shade stays one interfertile species for its whole ~2 Gyr.
- **Refusers.** Drums or families that refuse proofreading on principle accumulate load. They need some other way to clear it: choosing among embryos, letting thriving families have more children, or slow decline and growing dependence on medicine. Over ~100 Myr an isolated refuser raft would become a separate species.
- **Enhancers.** Drums that edit freely are possible but outside the norm. Their designs travel by laser like any information, so a fashion can cross the Shade in one lifetime. Bodies tuned to the 2 kWe life would be smaller, with eyes that see by a tenth the light, and the question of whether a smaller person counts as a whole share becomes constitutional.

## Births
### What binds
- **Each drum gets a fixed allotment of beam** from its raft and decides what to do with it (governance.md). Extra people don't take beam from neighbours; they thin their own drum's commons and their families' wallets.
- **Metal is fixed.** A drum gains one new share every ~3.6 years (and a new place every ~4.8) against ~1.7 M births a year at 120-year lives (economy.md), so births wait on deaths.
- **A drum with no limit hits the wall in about a century.** At three children a couple it is 10% over par in ~25 years, down to the lean 1 kWe a person in ~95, and at the bottom of the survival floor (~0.5 kWe, 25 m² of floor) in ~155. Growth starts slowly, at ~0.4% a year, because at first only the newest cohort is bigger. It reaches its long-run ~1.2% a year after about a century. Every lasting drum has some way of holding births to deaths, formal or not.

### Places and shares
The common system, and closest to how most land-limited societies on Earth worked: they limited who could set up a household rather than who could give birth. A drum keeps two ledgers (economy.md).

- **A place is household land, like a farm.** A nation drum of 200 M people is divided into ~150 M places, each ~47 m² of home, about 1⅓ people's share. Children and young adults live in a parent's or grandparent's place. At death a place passes to an heir, so nobody is turned out when the householders die.
- **Most people get their own place around 30.** A place frees when its holder dies at ~120 and lands about three generations down. With children around 30, four generations are alive, and the usual place is a great-grandparent's. A couple sets up its own home when it holds places, so most marry around 30. A family-tree model of a drum with 150 M places puts first places at ~30 on average: a quarter by 25, a fifth after 35. Younger adults live in the family's place or lodge in others': the drum's version of Europe's servants in husbandry.
- **A share is a person's wallet.** Every person at par holds one: the dividend (21.6 kWh a day) on top of the commons that everyone gets, shared or not (economy.md). A death frees a share. It goes first to the family's longest-waiting shareless member, if there is one, and otherwise to the next child born in the family, who is often named for the dead.
- **Most children start shareless.** About three babies in five are born before the death in the family that frees their share. They wait for it ~4 years on average, and one in ten waits 8 years or more. So at par ~2% of people are shareless, nearly all of them small children on their families' wallets. Shareless adults are rare in a drum at par.
- **One death, two inheritances.** A couple's 16 great-grandparents die around the couple's late 20s and 30s, and on average they free a place for each partner and a share for each of two children. A death at 120 gives a home to a great-grandchild and a wallet to a great-great-grandchild. Births follow deaths inside each family.
- **A family can overspend.** A child born ahead of a death has no share until one frees in the family, so the household divides its wallets: a third child takes a family of four from 21.6 kWh a head to ~17. The child still eats at the Mill table, because the commons is per head. So an extra child's wallet half (0.9 kW) falls on the family and its commons half (1.1 kW) on the whole drum, which is why drums still need norms. Overspent households have less floor and fewer kWh each, and suffer first in a Dark, like Ireland's subdivided plots.
- **The shareless work.** An adult without a share gets the commons but no dividend, and lives on family wallets and work: at ~3 kWh an hour, full-time work earns ~17 kWh a day, ~80% of a wallet (economy.md). A share is a cushion and the freedom not to work, not survival. Most of the shareless get one in time, as deaths in the family free shares.
- **Childlessness is rare,** about one adult in twenty, so two children is normal and a third is notable. At ~5% childless, ~12% of families have a third child, which replaces the childless and the few who die before 35. Each further point of childlessness makes room for about two more families in a hundred to have a third, so a third in 15% of families needs ~6% childless. Any more thirds than the childless make room for, and the drum runs over par.
- **Places and shares drift to careful families.** A childless person's place goes to a niece, a cousin or an adopted heir, and their share to the next in line in that branch of the family. Over generations, small families grow rich in places and prolific ones grow thin. Adopting an adult heir, as Japanese families adopted sons-in-law, is a normal way to keep a holding, and the only way to give a share outside the family.
- **An elder can hand over a place early.** Central European farmers retired on a maintenance contract, the *Altenteil*: the heir took the farm and the old couple kept a room and their keep. In a drum it moves the wait from the young to the old.
- **Drums with fewer, larger places run on three generations.** A drum divided into ~137 M places settles at places around 38 and first children around 41.
- **The drum keeps a small pool** for places and shares with no heir, orphans, and the few new ones metal brings.

### Other systems
| | Holdings (common) | Pool with birth rights | Custom and orders | Coercion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A child's share comes from | A death in the family | The drum's pool, spending a parent's right (economy.md) | The pool, by norm | The drum, by order |
| Big families are | Possible but poorer | Rich, connected or lucky | Disapproved of | Punished |
| Who stays childless | ~5%: callings, choice, a few shareless younger siblings | Sellers of rights, where sales are allowed | Orders and the honoured | Whoever the drum says |
| How it fails | Overspent households break in a Dark | Shareless children; the poor selling their own child's right | Norms weaken and the drum overshoots | Revolt, or other drums turning on it |
| Earth echo | Northwest Europe, Japan's *ie* | China's licences, Boulding's proposal | Tikopia, monastic Tibet | Tokugawa famines, China's worst years |

- **Pool drums** (Sorrel, Lovage) are reformers' drums: shares return to a pool at death and places are allotted by age or auction. They're equal between families, and they need a registry and an answer for the shareless child.
- **Unspent rights go back to the pool (proposal).** A right whose holder dies before spending it returns to the pool and is reissued, by lottery to couples who have spent theirs. Without that rule a pool drum loses the rights of everyone who dies before having children, ~0.7% a generation at 120-year lives, and it shrinks; after a plague it would never refill. With it, the pool keeps births equal to deaths, and the lottery is where a pool drum's third children come from.
- **Open drums** (open-drums.md) are holdings drums where custom does work: a Margin of unused shares and drum-held places, places lent to couples until they inherit, the gift of a share by adoption, and tables of aunts around every child, most of them adults whose own children are grown.
- **Custom is weak here** because the Shade lacks the outlet most Earth societies leaned on: there's nowhere to emigrate. Swaps between rafts free no shares, and new shares come one at a time. What remains are honours for the childless (Hollow hours, a high-deck flat), and childless callings with real standing.
- **Coercive drums** (counted drums) appear where a drum has overshot and nobody will bail it out. See Counted drums below.

### Counted drums
A counted drum decides who may have a child, and enforces it. People call them that for the Count, the board at every station ring that shows people against shares.

**Why drums count.**
- **Deaths set the births.** A nation drum loses ~1.7 M people a year, so that's how many children it can have.

| Children per couple | Long-run growth | 10% over par in | Lean 1 kWe life in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.25 | 0.34%/yr | ~64 yr | ~250 yr |
| 2.5 | 0.64%/yr | ~41 yr | ~150 yr |
| 3.0 | 1.16%/yr | ~25 yr | ~95 yr |

- **Overshoot is slow to show.** The growth rates are long-run. A drum at par is three or four generations deep, and when couples start having more children only the newest cohort is bigger, so the first decades grow at about a third of the long-run rate. The Count barely moves while the bigger cohorts are being born, and by the time it does, they're already here.

- **Overshoot is survivable for a long time.** At 10% over, each person has ~1.8 kWe and the 55-day reserve covers ~50 days; real danger starts near 1.5–2× par. So counting starts over the trend, not hunger, and every counted drum overstates its emergency.
- **The raft bails out disasters, not overbreeding.** A 0.1 AU raft gains ~8 × 10¹⁰ shares a year, so a drum that loses 10 M shares' worth of habitat to an accident can be rehoused from about an hour of the raft's growth, spread across ~4 × 10⁷ drums by tube. Nobody hands shares to a drum that bred past its own, or every drum would.
- **Holdings drums hide overshoot** in thin households until a blight or a Dark hits them all at once, like Ireland in 1845. Most counted drums start as overspent holdings drums after a bad year.
- **Raft-wide shocks** (a Dark, a raft-wide blight) leave nobody with slack, so many drums tip into counting together.
- **Some count one group.** On Earth most coercive sterilisation targeted minorities and the poor. The Shade's obvious targets are refuser families and their wild-born, and a drum at par can still count them.

**The ladder.** Drums usually climb one rung at a time. Above rung 4, neighbours stop calling it a drum's own business.

| Rung | What the drum does | Earth echo | How others see it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Price | Proofreading free for licensed births, charged for others | Singapore's "Stop at Two" | Policy |
| 2 Penalty | Unlicensed parents lose wallet, Hollow hours, deck seniority, posts | China's "social maintenance fees" | Harsh but legitimate |
| 3 The fitting | A reversible implant for everyone at fourteen, removed at the clinic on licence | China's mandatory IUDs | Contested; many call it reasonable in a crisis |
| 4 Forced procedures | Unlicensed pregnancies ended; sterilisation after a quota | China 1983 (~21 M sterilisations), India 1976–77 (~8 M), Peru 1996–2000 (~270,000) | Criminal to most drums |
| 5 Unpersoning | Wild-born get no share, register entry, school or commons, or are taken and fostered | China's *heihaizi*; Romania's orphanages | Atrocity; neighbours act |
| 6 Removing the living | Expulsion onto neighbours' lanes; a fixed age of death; death lotteries | Tikopia's sea voyages; Tokugawa *mabiki*; Trollope's *The Fixed Period* | A small Burn: every drum's enemy |

- **Rung 3 is the typical counted drum:** licences by lottery, the fitting, and a birth stop of one to two decades. It's what a reform council, not a tyrant, ends up with.
- **Long lives tempt rung 6.** A full stop clears 10% overshoot in ~12 years at 120-year lives, ~40 at 400. A fixed age of 100 in a 120-year drum would remove a sixth of it at once.
- **Expulsion barely works.** A share doesn't travel, so neighbours gain mouths and no metal, and they can refuse to catch the cars. Dumping people on the Glide is an attack on the next drum.
- **Control is easy in a drum.** The clinics see nearly every pregnancy, the commons meters show a household eating for five on four shares within weeks, the register records every share and heir, and nine station rings are the only doors. Against it: 200 M people can hide a lot, home births need no machines, and every drum hears how others live.
- **The quiet nightmare** is a counted drum that edits embryos toward wanting fewer children, and stops needing implants.

**Life in one.** Rue, across the rows from Sorrel on Herb Row (ashgrove.md), is nine years into a stop.
- It was a holdings drum where three children had come back into fashion. At ~14% over, a *Fusarium* blight took two vat lines, its thin households went short for a season, and the council that came out of that year posted the first Count and cut births to a quarter of deaths.
- Its Count now reads ~217 M people on 200 M shares (and 150 M places), ~8.5% over, 450,000 licences against ~1.66 M deaths a year: par in ~14 years. People bet on the date. Deaths are fewer than 217 M ÷ 120 would suggest, because the extra people are young, and the bettors who know it win.
- At fourteen every child goes to the clinic for the fitting, now a rite with a family dinner. Licences go by lottery to couples who both hold places, first children first, and can't be sold.
- Low-deck schools stand half-empty; in fifteen years Rue will have a hollow in its workforce.
- The well-off have Lovage births: they buy a birth right in Lovage and place the child there with relatives. Poorer couples hide pregnancies, and Rue's wild-born are mostly theirs.
- Rue's extra people are mostly shareless children, so its shares still carry full wallets, and the childfree swap in while would-be parents swap out. Each swap makes its rule a little more chosen.

**Legitimacy.** Counting holds when the Count is public and falling, the burden falls equally by lottery, it follows a shock people remember, it has an end date, and people can leave. It fails when the powerful are exempt, it outlives the shock as a bureaucracy's income, it targets one group, or the rings are shut. China's one-child rule lasted 35 years, past its own rationale; India's sterilisation drive cost the government the 1977 election.

**The right of the Glide.** Most rafts guarantee that anyone may glide out of any drum. That keeps most counted drums at rungs 2–3, since the people most likely to resist leave. But a share doesn't travel, so leaving without a swap makes someone a shareless lodger elsewhere: exit works for those who can find a swap, and fails the poor, who are counted hardest. A raft without the right, where drums can shut their rings, is where the dark drums are.

**What neighbours and the raft do.**

| Tool | Held by | Used against |
|---|---|---|
| Refuge swaps: shares from the pool for people fleeing | Neighbour drums | Rungs 4–6 |
| Placing wild-born children, or buying them rights | Neighbours, charities | Rung 5 |
| Gate embargo on produce, strains and parts | Neighbours | Rungs 3+ |
| Clinicians refusing licence checks, by Shade-wide norm | The medical guild | Rungs 3–4 |
| Cutting the Glide and tubes | The raft | Rungs 5–6 |
| Holding back the beam share | The raft, which holds the contract | Rung 6 only |

- **The raft sanctions; it doesn't dock beam** for anything less than rung 6, since that would undo the fixed allotments this section rests on.
- **Neighbours are tempted to pay for coercion.** A counted drum at rung 3 keeps its people home; one that collapses sends them onto the Glide. Drums beside a counted one often quietly prefer it to count. Herb Row talks about Rue that way.

**How common.** Share counting ≈ how often a drum hits a serious overshoot or shock × the chance it answers by coercion × how long that lasts.

| | Rare | Canon | Common |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serious overshoot or shock, per drum | 1 / 3,000 yr | 1 / 1,000 yr | 1 / 300 yr |
| Answered by coercion | 10% | 30% | 50% |
| How long it lasts | 20 yr | 40 yr | 80 yr |
| Share of drums counting | ~0.07% | **~1%** | ~13% |
| In a glide metro of ~80 drums | 1 in ~20 metros | ~1 | ~10 |
| At rungs 5–6 | 1 in ~14,000 drums | 1 in ~800 | 1 in ~80 |

- **About one in every glide metro,** so nearly everyone has a counted drum a glide or two away, and about one drum in 800 is at rung 5–6. Earth's 1950–2020 rate was ~0.6% of state-years.
- **Waves.** After a Dark or a raft-wide blight the share on that raft can reach 10–30% for a generation. A story set just after a Dark has counted drums on every row.
- **More common with age.** Drums drift toward people who want children badly (What births do to the gene pool), and long lives slow every repair. An old Shade likely has more counted drums than a young one.
- **Every drum has counted at some point:** at the canon rate, ~40 years in every 3,000. Most keep an old Count board in a museum.

### The clinics and the wild-born
- **Proofreading makes nearly every birth visible.** In a pool drum the clinic is where the right is checked; in a holdings drum it records which household the child belongs to and whose share, if any, they carry.
- **The wild-born** are children conceived and carried outside the clinics: unproofread, unregistered, often shareless. Since proofreading is a norm and not a law, some are the children of refusers, not lawbreakers. People confuse the two.
- **A drum that wants control** can make proofreading free for licensed births and charge for the rest, without punishing anyone outright.

### Leaving
Emigration is no outlet: a few go, and it's hard. Someone short of floor and food can't afford the trip, and a drum won't let metal leave.

| Trip | Cost per person | Time | What else it takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| To a passing raft | A 100–300 km/s capsule throw, ~10–90 GJ (raft-transport.md): ~2,800–25,000 kWh, 4 months to 3 years of a wallet | Days, but only to rafts passing within a few AU | A catch ramp on the other raft, and a share there, so in practice a swap |
| To a chosen raft across the Shade | The same throw | Thousands of years at ~300 km/s across ~2 pc | A habitat for the trip: not a capsule ride |
| To the frontier, in person | Climbing back out to 100–200 pc against the falling stream: ~80 GJ/kg, ~1.6 × 10⁸ kWh for ~7 t of person, shield and life support (~20,000 years of a wallet) | ~1 Myr, the payloads' fall run backwards (matter-delivery.md) | A self-supporting habitat, so a whole drum leaves together, taking its metal |
| To the frontier, as a genome | Nearly free: a genome is information | Centuries by laser | An outpost willing to grow and raise the child |

- **Most emigrants are swaps with passing rafts,** arranged across light-minutes to light-days for the few days a raft is near.
- **A drum leaving whole** for the frontier is a once-in-an-era event, and its metal walks out with it, so the raft has a say.
- **The cheap way to the frontier is to send a child, not go.** A would-be emigrant can sign over a genome; an outpost grows and raises the child. Whether outposts want Shade children, and whether this counts as a birth anywhere, is open.

### What births do to the gene pool
- **Selection needs uneven family sizes.** Strict two-each drums barely evolve. Holdings and tradable rights let some families have more, so over ~10,000 years most drums drift toward people who want children badly, which tightens the pressure on whatever system they use.
- **Celibate callings select against whatever draws people to them.**

## Open questions
- How often people move far across a raft, which sets whether a raft has regional looks among refusers.
- Whether frontier outposts take Shade children as genomes, and on what terms.
- Whether the residential dose should come down from ~20 mSv/yr.
- How the Shade's age at story time shapes things: 10,000 years, 1 Myr and 100 Myr look different among refusers.

## Method
- Generation ~35 years: 100 Myr ≈ 3 × 10⁶ generations.
- New mutations ~60–80 per child, ~2 more per year of the father's age; ~1–2 harmful. A drum makes ~2 × 10⁸ children a generation, ~1.4 × 10¹⁰ mutations, against ~9 × 10⁹ possible single-letter changes.
- Neutral fixation ~4N generations. Divergence between drums by chance F_ST ≈ 1/(1 + 4Nm), ~10⁻⁸ for N = 2 × 10⁸, m = 0.1.
- An advantage s spreads across a grid at ~σ√(2s) per generation: ~0.14 drums a generation at σ = 1 drum, s = 0.01, ~4 × 10⁶ generations across 600,000 drums. Well mixed: ~(2/s) ln 2N ≈ 9,000 generations.
- An unselected gene is knocked out by mutation pressure in ~10⁵–10⁶ generations.
- Unchecked growth: three children a couple, ~1.5× a generation, ~1.2% a year in the long run. From a drum at par, a cohort model (Gompertz mortality fitted to 120-year lives, births at 35 ± 6) gives ~0.4% a year for the first 20 years, 10% over in ~25 years and doubling in ~95.
- Places: with lives of L ≈ 120 years and a place of one's own at age A, the share of people without a place is A ÷ L and people per place L ÷ (L − A). At A = 30: 25% and 1.33, so a drum of 200 M has 150 M places of 35 × 1.33 ≈ 47 m² of home. A family-tree model (~15,000 people over 640 years: marriage at ~28, children 3 and 6 years later, places to the eldest placeless descendant of the dead) gives first places at 30.4 (10–90%: 21–40) and 1.34 people a place; 71% of places come from a great-grandparent, 16% from a grandparent, 12% from an aunt's or cousin's line.
- Generations: a place freed at L lands k generations down. With first children ~3 years after a place, A ≈ (L − 3k) ÷ (k + 1): k = 3 gives places at ~28 and children at ~31, four generations alive and 1.30 people a place (~154 M places); k = 2 gives places at ~38 and children at ~41, 1.46 people a place (~137 M places). The rhythm is a rule of thumb; the place count sets the age, and 150 M places give ~30.
- Shares: in the same family-tree model, with ~5% childless and thirds set to hold par, ~60% of babies are born before the death that frees their share and wait ~3.6–3.9 years (90th percentile 7–8). About 1.5% of people are shareless at exact par, and ~2.3% at 0.8% over, almost all children. About half of shares come from a great-great-grandparent, ~12% from a great-grandparent and most of the rest from an aunt's or cousin's line.
- Families: replacement is 2 ÷ (survival to 35) ≈ 2 ÷ 0.993 ≈ 2.01 births a couple. With a share c of adults childless, parents average 2.01 ÷ (1 − c): at c = 5%, 2.12, so ~12% of families have a third child if none stop at one. A third in 15% of families needs c ≈ 6%.
- Pool drums: a right is lost if its holder dies before spending it; survival to 35 is 0.993, so ~0.7% of rights a generation.
- Inheritance: each person has 8 great-grandparents, and each great-grandparent 8 great-grandchildren and 16 great-great-grandchildren, so on average each death frees a place for a great-grandchild and a share for a great-great-grandchild.
- Overspending: four wallets of 21.6 kWh across five people ≈ 17.3 kWh a head. Work: 3.1 kWh an hour × 2,000 hours ÷ 365 ≈ 17 kWh a day.
- Leaving: wallet ~0.9 kW ≈ 7,900 kWh a year. Climbing out to the frontier ≈ ½(v_arrival² − v_orbit²) ≈ ½(440² − 160²) km²/s² ≈ 84 GJ/kg, the catch figure run backwards; × 7 t ≈ 5.9 × 10¹⁴ J ≈ 1.6 × 10⁸ kWh. Crossing ~2 pc at 300 km/s ≈ 6,500 years.
- Germline doubling dose ~1 Gy; 20 mSv/yr × 30 years ≈ 0.6 Sv.
- Counted drums: growth (children ÷ 2)^(1/35 yr) − 1; 10% over in ln 1.1 ÷ rate; 1 kWe in ln 2 ÷ rate. Rue: three children a couple takes a drum at par to 228 M (14% over) in ~35 years (~50 at 2.5); births at a quarter of deaths for 9 years ≈ −1.2 M a year → ~217 M. The extra people are young, so deaths stay near ~1.66 M a year, not 217 M ÷ 120 ≈ 1.8 M; at 450,000 licences the drum falls ~1.2 M a year and reaches par in ~14 years. All from the cohort model above. Raft growth 1.4 × 10⁻⁹ a year × 6 × 10¹⁹ ≈ 8.4 × 10¹⁰ shares a year; 10 M shares ≈ 1 hour of it, or a year's growth of ~3.6 × 10⁷ drums (a patch ~150,000 km across). Prevalence = rate × probability × duration; Earth ≈ 75 coercive state-years ÷ (~190 states × 70 yr).
