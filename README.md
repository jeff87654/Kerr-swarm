# Kerr swarm

Worldbuilding for a far-future human civilization living around a Kerr black hole. The baseline is Sgr A\* fed at 30% of its Eddington luminosity and held there as it grows: the hole's glowing envelope (the Lantern) beams power to rafts of nation drums in the Shade outside it. Population is very high (~2 × 10³³ people) and resources per person are tight: about 2 kWe of electricity, ~6.7 t of heavy elements and ~3 m² of raft area each.

This repository is the source of truth for the design docs. The older Google Drive mirror is retired.

The docs are also published as a browsable world bible at **https://jeff87654.github.io/Kerr-swarm/**, rebuilt from `docs/` on every push to `main` (see [`site/`](site/README.md)). The markdown stays the only source: edit the docs, not the site.

## Canon decisions

When docs disagree, `full-swarm.md` (Oct 2026 revisions) is the baseline for the mature civilization, and the newer detailed docs (`element-cycles`, `raft-habitat`, `drum-transport`, `governance`, `economy`) beat older ones. Ring-era docs stay valid for ring-era numbers. Decisions from the consistency audit and the stress test of 29 Oct 2026:

- **Population** (1 Nov 2026, physics audit; 6 Nov 2026, world audit): ~1.87 × 10³³ people, set by the 2 kWe life at 30% of Eddington with everything beamed (~8.7 kW of the disk's light each). The Shade weighs ~2.2× the hole, counting the front caps' extra hydrogen (Radiation). Older figures: ~1.95 × 10³³ while the Lantern ran the batch compute unbeamed (1–6 Nov 2026); ~1.27 × 10³³ for the 3.1 kWe common budget.
- **Raft area:** 3 m² per person, sized for the 2 kWe life. A raft at the full common budget would need ~3.5–4.3 m² for its radiators (~3.7 with its hot loads at ~500 K). Rafts near the Shade's inner edge take ~3.3–3.8 m², since reflected Lantern glow lands on their 300 K radiators.
- **Growth horizon:** ~1–2 Gyr (hole grows ~19×): ~2 Gyr if the hole spins up to 0.998, ~0.9–1.6 Gyr if magnetized flows hold it at ~0.93–0.99. Drums take ~75% of their metal from white dwarfs (`element-cycles.md`); 1.1 Gyr is only the all-solar-mix case at full spin. As the hole grows, rafts must climb: a free orbit shrinks while the Lantern widens.
- **Metal:** the 30% civilization holds ~6.3 × 10⁶ M☉ of heavy elements (6.7 t a person). The harvest reaches the inner bulge, out to ~0.4 kpc, as well as the nuclear stellar disk (`matter-delivery.md`).
- **Travel shields:** sealed water only. Polyethylene is banned because it leaks hydrogen.
- **Per-person power:** the Lantern generates ~2.75 kWe per person for the 2 kWe life, all of it beamed (4.24 kWe for the common budget, which `hole-choice.md`'s older fed-hole rows use). Cryocoolers pause only while boil-off is used or stored.
- **Hydrogen store:** the cluster's stripped hydrogen and helium is kept as ~4 × 10⁸ brown dwarfs at ~10–30 pc, not as gas or ice. It covers the feed and the shields for ~675 Myr (~750 before the front caps thickened). The feed travels as Jupiter-mass bodies, ~19 a year, which survive the fall through the Lantern and boil off at periapsis; each is a ~10³⁸ J projectile that must be aimed through the Shade (6 Nov 2026, proposal; `full-swarm.md` § Growth at 30%).
- **Lantern mirrors:** ~3–5° grazing, since a spun-up disk's light is mostly above ~100 eV.
- **The Burn:** the beam can focus to destroy a raft. It's possible but held to be a crime against the whole Shade, which unites the other rafts against whoever orders it. A patch built as independent sub-arrays, each locked to a beacon on the raft, can't focus tighter than the raft's beacons; whether the builders chose that is history (6 Nov 2026, `governance.md` § Checks built into the plumbing).
- **Rules of the road:** orbits kept round (free eccentricity under ~0.003; the Lantern's latitude-dependent glow forces another ~0.001–0.003 on inclined orbits, which the rule doesn't count), and every raft climbs with a steady prograde push as the hole grows; one collision is a 10³³–10³⁶ J blast that can cascade.
- **Electronics** (6 Nov 2026, world audit): data halls and power electronics sit in water-walled vaults of ~4.5 t/m², thick enough for ordinary chips even under the corona's MeV tail; rectennas use radiation-hard diodes. Nothing computes on the Lantern: its electronics take ~8–20 MGy a year, shielding them would outweigh the collectors, and shuttling replacements across the 145–215 km/s gap would cost a fifth of the civilization's output, so all compute, batch work included, runs in the rafts' vaults on beamed power, and the Lantern remakes its own transmitters and controls on site from its own stock.
- **Disk weather:** dips of tens of percent are ridden out on methanol (~95 kg a person for a month at 20%, in fuel cells at ~48%), then rationed. The tank farm banks the matching oxygen as liquid (~145 kg a person) and keeps the CO₂ (~130 kg) for re-synthesis, since nothing vents. Refilling costs ~780 kWh a person.
- **Genome drift:** accepted. Huge rafts drift little. Radiation adds little: at ~1 mSv/yr a generation gets ~3% more new mutations, and proofreading clears them.
- **Radiation** (2 Nov 2026, `habitat-design.md`, `raft-transport.md`, `full-swarm.md`): the hole is quiet, as a stated premise: no jets, a corona that makes few neutrons, and a wind the Lantern absorbs rather than shocks. So cosmic rays in the Shade stay ~1–4× the Galaxy's; hedging against a proton-accelerating corona would cost ~4–5.5 t more hydrogen a person and cut the fuel horizon to ~400–470 Myr. The corona's leak is planned with an MeV tail of ~10%, so each drum's front cap carries ~100 m more hydrogen (shields ~3.2 t a person), glide cars carry ~6.2 t/m² of water facing the Lantern, tubes ~6.9 and cycler trunks ~6 all round (~7.5 facing the Lantern). Residents take ~1 mSv/yr, least on the Middle decks. Nobody works behind a raft in a suit: back crews ride closed sleds of ~4 t/m², careers are capped at ~1 Sv, and the Windows are glazed with ~1.5 m of water.
- **The great year:** a calendar unit of 39,000 years, about one orbit. Nothing in the sky marks it.
- **Interest** (6 Nov 2026): zero growth doesn't make interest zero; what keeps fortunes from compounding is each drum's rule. The two poles are a usury rule (Sorrel) and rentier wallets (`economy.md` § Saving and interest).
- **Core and frontier** (6 Nov 2026, proposal): the core wants metal for new rafts, its one narrow outlet for the placeless (~400 new drums a year on a 0.1 AU raft); the frontier, capped by starlight, wants beam to grow on. The contracts are beam for metal (`economy.md` § The frontier).
- **The settlement** (6 Nov 2026, provisional, Jeff's sketch): why minds stay biological. A singleton divided the universe long ago and this galaxy fell to biological life under terms; the hole need then not be Sgr A* itself but a nucleus like it, with the Milky Way's inner structure as the model (`governance.md` § Minds, machines and the settlement).
- **Bodies and births** (31 Oct 2026, `people.md`): proofreading of every embryo is the norm, not a law, and clears mutation load. Each drum gets a fixed allotment of beam and runs its own birth system. Places and shares mostly pass in the family; pool drums with birth rights are the minority.
- **Places and shares** (1 Nov 2026; stem places 2 Nov 2026, `people.md`, `economy.md`): a place is household land, like a farm, and passes whole to one heir, so it is one household's home and a nation drum of 200 M has ~90 M places of ~78 m². Children and young adults live in a parent's or grandparent's place; an heir usually inherits a great-grandparent's place in their late 20s, and a spouse marries in. A place goes down its own line, never up to an older relative who has waited longer. Children belong to a place: couples have them once they have one, so a drum's extra children become the placeless rather than more households, and a drum that has more of them levels off instead of compounding. Each person's wallet comes with a share, one per person at par, freed by a death in the family and passed to the family's longest-waiting shareless member, or otherwise to a newborn. A child born ahead of a death has no share until one frees, and lives on the commons, family wallets and work. Beam follows shares.
- **The placeless** (2 Nov 2026, `people.md`): adults who never head a household can marry, since birth control separates partnership from children. They live in houses (homes held by bodies that don't die, entered by election, with no children raised in them) or Margin flats lent for life, foster and teach, and hold offices of trust. They can still marry an heir or be adopted into a heirless place. In a steady drum they are about one person in forty, nearly all of them placeless-born (9 Nov 2026; Born to a place, below).
- **Births and the posted price** (6 Nov 2026; revised 9 Nov 2026, `people.md`, `economy.md`, `open-drums.md`, `glossary.md`): nobody waits on a death to have a child, since no society on Earth ever did; births follow places, wallets follow deaths, and a household's wallets thin while a child waits for its share. A child's keep is the commons', so a third costs its family only a thinner wallet, and under stem places with no line drawn at birth every child's chance of a household is the same, so a three-child family out-bred a two-child one three to two and the wish climbed (`models/ratchet.py`, `models/share_market.py`, `models/ratchet.md`). A shareless sibling is no worse a match, so the marriage market never tuned the wish, and no price did either. The posted price is what most steady drums charge a household for the keep of a child born outside its allowance: it keeps the cost off the neighbours and holds nothing. Open drums post none. Rungs 1–2 of the counted-drum ladder are not counting.
- **Families** (6 Nov 2026; revised 9 Nov 2026, `families.md`): drums differ in how a household passes (whole to one heir, as canon; with portions for the others; split among all the children; kept by the siblings for life), in how leave for a third is given (the draw, gift, sale, a block's say) and in where they draw the line. They differ on children born in rented rooms: some ban them with varying severity, some frown on them and the children grow up poorer, some don't mind, and those run well above par. Since about one couple in twenty is childless, the families with a third always replace them and gain by at least the room they leave, so no rule a drum could live with held the wish for children still (`models/regimes.py`, `models/ratchet.py`).
- **Born to a place** (9 Nov 2026, second world audit; Jeff's decision, details proposals; `families.md`, `people.md`): the Shade is far older than the few thousand years the wish takes to saturate, and every new drum is founded by an old one's people, so most couples in every drum want a third. What holds a drum's numbers is standing. A household has two children by right and a third by leave, issued only as fast as the childless and the early dead leave room: about one birth in eighteen, a third for one family in eight. A child born outside that allowance is placeless-born: registered, proofread, fed and schooled, but passed over by the line rule unless it is placed, by adoption into an heirless place, a marriage the drum allows or long service, and every placing spends room. A household that waits keeps its chance of leave and one with a child outside is out of the draw, so the rule holds wherever the door is narrower than the draw's odds. A steady drum sits ~2.7% over par, with about one person in forty placeless-born. The placeless-born have nothing to lose, and where they become a people apart their line grows; drums keep them mixed in, place their children, charge a keep or fit them. A drum that votes its line away has three children a couple within a generation, and a drum that must shrink can't do it by standing, which is when it counts (`models/placing.py`).
- **Homes and rent** (1 Nov 2026, `people.md`, `open-drums.md`, `economy.md`): couples bridge the years before a place of their own in many ways at once: a parent's place, rented rooms, lodging, an elder's early hand-down, a swap, and in open drums a Margin place lent for up to five years, by lottery for the best flats (and for the loans themselves when the Margin runs short). Rent floats with a drum's slack: about 0.1 kWh per m² a day at par, about half that in an open drum, two to four times it in a thin one. Chervil's halls hire their own people first, and Sorrel counts back-crew years toward going up.
- **Open drums** (31 Oct 2026, `open-drums.md`): about a quarter of drums hold births to deaths without coercion, keep a Margin of 2–4% under par, and grant guest-right as well as the right of the Glide. Their blocks give leave for third children (9 Nov 2026). Chervil on Herb Row is the example.
- **Eating out** (31 Oct 2026, `eating-out.md`): restaurants are rated in apple-seed stars; waste-heat (65 °C) cooking and low-g cooking are recognised styles; animals are a rare luxury (one henhouse on Herb Row); osmotic produce ("orchard pearls") exists as a recent Lovage invention; Tansy, Yarrow and Borage are on Herb Row.

## Games and atlases

- [The Sorrel Atlas](atlas/sorrel/): an interactive cutaway of one drum, Sorrel, where every point opens a place: 213 hand-written landmarks from the quay to the axle, and all 69,426 neighbourhoods, generated from their addresses. [Explore it on the site](https://jeff87654.github.io/Kerr-swarm/sorrel-atlas/). Its landmarks are written up in `docs/sorrel.md`; its generated neighbourhoods are not canon.
- [Three Nights Anywhere](games/three-nights/): a browser game of a wander year across Merrow, three nights a drum on guest-right. [Play it on the site](https://jeff87654.github.io/Kerr-swarm/three-nights/). Its generated drums and customs are not canon; its 16 hand-written drums follow the docs.
- [Round the Clock](games/round-the-clock/): twelve small games, one for each Hour of Sorrel, played with the drum's own physics from a full g at the rim to none on the axis (a stair race up through falling weight, the morning tide, spinball, night flying across the Spindle), and then the Round. [Play it on the site](https://jeff87654.github.io/Kerr-swarm/round-the-clock/). The Hours, clubs and places follow the atlas and `docs/sorrel.md`; going round the clock, the threads and each Hour's game are the game's own inventions, not canon.
- [Guest-Right](games/guest-right/): a first-person game with no violence in it, set in Chervil, the open hall drum of `docs/open-drums.md`. Three nights on guest-right as a wanderer from Spelt: a station under a vault, a block of three thousand with its court, streets, shops, school, baths and flats, one table a hundred and twenty metres long, the whole Hollow to walk with its far bank overhead, and on Booking night the Long Flight down the axis. [Play it on the site](https://jeff87654.github.io/Kerr-swarm/guest-right/). Chervil, its customs, Marit, Jory, Oona and Booking Day follow the docs; the block Candlewick, its shops and most of its people are the game's own inventions, listed in its README and in `games/guest-right/content/not-canon.md`, and are not canon.

## Models

- [models/stem_places.py](models/stem_places.py): the family-tree model behind `people.md`'s figures for stem places, the placeless and shares. Pure Python; each run takes a few minutes.
- [models/ratchet.py](models/ratchet.py): the cohort model of the wish for children under each way of holding births, behind `people.md`'s § What births do to the gene pool and `families.md`; the three models are written up in [models/ratchet.md](models/ratchet.md). Needs numpy and scipy; a few seconds.
- [models/share_market.py](models/share_market.py): `stem_places.py`'s family tree with a raised wish for children and four marriage markets, measuring each child's chance of a household and what a third costs its siblings (outputs in `models/out/`). About two minutes a run.
- [models/regimes.py](models/regimes.py): a generation model of a drum under each inheritance and birth rule in `families.md`, with an evolving wish for a third; `python3 regimes.py table` prints each rule's advantage against the floor. Pure Python; a few minutes.
- [models/placing.py](models/placing.py): a drum with the wish saturated, held by who is born to a place: the room for thirds, what a drum carries by the leak and the door, whether a child outside the allowance pays, and when the line outside takes off (output in `models/out/placing.txt`). Pure Python; a second or two.
