# Canon brief: the Shade, Merrow and its drums

This is the reference for writing content for **Three Nights Anywhere**, an exploration game set in a hard-SF world bible (the "Kerr swarm"). The player is a young traveller on their *wander year*, gliding from drum to drum across a raft called Merrow, staying three nights at a time on guest-right, and sending cards home. Every one of Merrow's ~300 billion drums is generated, so the game needs large pools of short, specific, warm, physically correct writing. Everything you write must fit the facts below. When in doubt, be smaller and more ordinary rather than grand.

## The setting in one paragraph

Far in the future, ~10³³ people live around the Milky Way's central black hole. A shell of collectors called **the Lantern** catches the hole's light and beams power by microwave to **rafts**: flat platforms of spinning habitats in **the Shade** outside it. Nobody ever sees the hole or the Lantern. **Merrow** is one raft: about 0.1 AU (13.4 million km) on a side, ~6 × 10¹⁹ people in ~300 billion **nation drums**, standing 24.5 km apart on a square grid. Each drum is a nation of ~200 million people with its own rules. Power is steady; there is no day, night or season outside; every light is a lamp. Life is comfortable, crowded, thrifty and very old: drums have stood for ages, and most things people own are older than they are.

## The nation drum (the habitat)

- A squat cylinder 3 km in radius and 3.75 km long, spinning once every 110 seconds. **Out is down**: gravity is 1 g at the rim and falls toward the axis.
- ~375 **decks**, 4 m apart (ceilings ~3 m). Most of the drum is an indoor city of arcades, streets, lift lobbies and flats, lamp-lit like a big building. Avenues run *spinward*, streets run along the axis. Almost nothing has windows.
- **Bands by gravity:**
  - **The Keel** (decks 1–15, ~1 g): no homes. The food plant (the Mill, the Vats, the Garden racks), water works, methanol tank farm, survival reserve, recyclers, workshops, and the **station rings** where people leave the drum.
  - **Low decks** (16–113, 0.98–0.85 g): families, schools, sport, inns for visitors. Children are raised near full weight "because bones grow best there", or so parents believe.
  - **Middle decks** (114–301, 0.85–0.6 g): most homes and work.
  - **High decks** (302–375, 0.6–0.5 g): elders' flats and prestige addresses, lowest radiation dose, nearest the Hollow. "Money lives up; the way out is down."
- **Neighbourhoods** are blocks of ~3,000 people, a few decks deep, sometimes around an **atrium** cut through several decks (Ashgrove's "Court" is 6 decks tall with pear trees espaliered on its brightest wall).
- **The Hollow**: the open core, 3 km across and 3.75 km long. Its floor is the innermost deck at **0.5 g**: lawns, orchards, a lake, pavilions, woods or bright squares. The far bank hangs **3 km overhead, upside down**, perfectly sharp because filtered heliox carries no dust. A **sunline** along the axis lights it: a 3-hour dawn, ~6 hours of full brightness (100,000 lux, a clear noon), a 3-hour dusk, then lamp-night. Some drums buy more (Lovage runs 12 full hours). The sunline stops ~450 m short of each **end wall**. End walls are **terraced**, climbing toward the axis and getting lighter with every tier; top terraces are gardens where a child can jump the height of a house. The Hollow is scarce (0.18 m² per person) so time in it is rationed, sold or rotated.
- **The Spindle**: the axis. Within ~75 m of it you weigh under 3% of a g: flying country. People fly in **wing-sleeves** (fabric fins wrist to hip). By day only in the netted **flying halls** at each end; in the lamp-night the whole Spindle opens. Flying *along* the axis is easy; flying *across* it, Coriolis bends your path ("crosswise" is the hard way).
- **Behind the drum**: the radiator layer. **Back crews** keep up the radiator panels on sleds in suits, and are among the few people who see stars. Some drums keep **Windows**: shielded galleries behind the radiators where children are taken once, at about eight, to see the sky.
- **Coriolis rules everyday life.** "Axis flies true, spin sinks, anti floats." Moving spinward makes you heavier, antispinward lighter. A dropped cup lands ~2 cm *antispinward* of where it was aimed. Children say "anti" when something falls, as though the drum did it. Leaps at a third of a g come down a hand's width spinward. Cabs lean.
- **The air is heliox**: 21 kPa oxygen in ~80 kPa helium. Sound runs ~1.8× faster, so voices are bright and thin-edged, rooms ring (good rooms hang felt), wind instruments are long (a flute is ~1.2 m), and old recordings sound "spoken through a blanket". Helium chills skin, so rooms run warm, hot baths are the cheapest luxury, and food is served **under lids** (an uncovered dish says something about your upbringing).
- **Getting around inside**: the **Lattice** of six-seat **cabs** with gimballed cabins runs in lift cores and on line decks, door to door; any trip is ~6 minutes on average. Rim to the innermost deck is ~1.5 minutes. Stairs exist but walking costs food.

## Between drums

- **The Glide**: drums share tangent lanes; a car let go from one drum's rim **falls free in a straight line for 140 seconds at 174 m/s** and is caught by the next drum's rim at exactly its speed. It is **free** and runs even in a beam cut. Weightless for the whole glide. The catch brings a full g back over a second or two: "a young people's ride"; some elders won't glide.
- Glide cars are squat and heavily shielded with **sealed water** (the hall outside is flooded with the Lantern's hard X-rays, lethal in minutes). **Windows face aft**, through a metre of clear water. A glide is the only view of outside most people ever get: a dark hall 3.75 km deep between the rectenna layer and the radiator layer, drums standing in it like squat pillars, and every drum wearing **rings of quay lights round its waist**, rank behind rank to a vanishing point.
- Each drum has ~9 **station rings** on its rim deck; around them the **Quaysides**: markets, freight, and **the Gate**, quarantine for anything biological (in a drum fed by monoculture vats, a stray phage is a famine). Living cultures (starters, yogurts, seeds) are sequenced and registered with the **strain library**.
- **Every station ring keeps a hatch, the ring counter**, serving a cup of Mill broth and a sticky rice ball for free. Each drum's broth is different (Sorrel's peppery, Lovage's sweet, Chervil's with a sprig of whatever the hall cut that day). Arguing which is best is one of the oldest sports.
- **Cyclers**: sleeper trains that never stop, looping on trunk tubes across the raft. You board and leave by capsule. **Price is speed squared, not distance**: a slow cycler at 2 km/s costs ~1.1 kWh for any distance (a million km takes ~6 days); the **express** at 20 km/s costs ~111 kWh (five days of a wallet) and does a million km in ~16 hours. Slow lines are **weightless** (the raft doesn't spin): soup in bulbs, bread in nets, sleeping bags clipped to the wall, an hour of bungee exercise a day ("or your bones sulk"). **The Wanderers' Galley** on slow lines has a stockpot that hasn't gone cold in living memory; every rider adds something from the last drum they slept in. A shelf of starter crocks: take a spoon, leave a spoon.
- Light delay across Merrow: up to ~45 seconds corner to corner. People across the raft talk with a few seconds' wait; between rafts, minutes to years. **Slow worlds** are games built to tolerate lag. **Long letters** are written carefully, to be read once.

## Money, prices and what's scarce

- **Money is the kWh**, called **"kays"** in speech. One currency on every raft. Everyone draws an equal **dividend of 21.6 kWh a day** on top of **the commons** (air, a free Mill-table food credit, public light, schools, clinics, cabs and the Glide).
- Paying someone for their time moves claims, not energy. An hour of anyone's time costs ~3 kWh in most drums (~6 in open drums that spend more on each other).
- **The dear things are mass, light and speed.** New things cost ~25 kWh per kg, so things are mended, altered, remade and inherited. Fashion lives in linings, collars, cuffs and dye ("this season's hard algae blue"). Light is the luxury good: a bright home is the plainest sign of wealth. Express travel is the signature splurge.
- **What energy can't buy:** a child or a bigger flat. Both are metal, which arrives only as fast as the hole grows. **Births match deaths.**
- **Prices (kWh):** a glide 0; herbs from the pot 0.03; boiling a litre 0.045; cup of coffee 0.15; a gin 0.18; a pint of beer 0.38; a tomato 0.5; a side salad 0.59; bowl of noodles 0.96; slow cycler leg 1.1; ramen 1.77; Vat-table dinner 2.5; a cultivated steak 2.9; an hour of someone's time ~3; an orchard apple 4.4; a new shirt 5; a day of personal compute 9.6; a new pair of shoes 20; remade wing-sleeves ~18; express cycler leg 111; a new chair 125; a new sofa 1,000.
- **Going dark**: switching off your personal compute (worlds, messages) saves 9.6 kWh a day. It's a ritual fast, signed by a **black thread round the left wrist**: don't message me, I won't see it.

## Food

- **The Mill** (abiotic chemistry) makes starch, sugar, fat and amino acids: about two-thirds of calories. **The Mill table** is free from the commons: jook in the morning, glass noodles and starch jellies at noon, stew with a starch swallow at night, Mill lager, and **the Nine** (a seasoning of the nine essential amino acids, masked with salt and glutamate; "have some Nine" is said to anyone who looks peaky).
- **The Vats** (microbes, fungi, cell culture) make protein, dairy, egg, meat, texture and flavour: cheese, mycelium steak, cultivated meat, vat egg, sourdough. **The Vat table** costs a little more than the Mill table.
- **The Garden** (LED-lit plants) gives crisp produce and herbs: ~2% of calories but dear. **Inverted scarcity: pastry is cheap and salad is a treat.** Tomatoes are sold like jewellery. An **orchard apple** (4.4 kWh) is a gift; at weddings one is **cut crosswise to show the five-pointed star of seeds** (for most people, the only star they'll ever see). Every flat has a **herb shelf**: the first thing anyone plants, the last thing that goes dark. Herbs are the classic host gift.
- **Waste-heat cooking**: the food plant's free 65 °C loop is a low oven: onsen custards, amazake, black garlic, broths held below a simmer. Searing and frying draw power.
- **Blights** are this world's famines: a phage takes out one vat line, the Mill carries the day, and the drum eats a **blight menu** until the line regrows from library stock. Switching organism families changes the taste, so blight years become **vintages** (the "orange-steak year" when Sorrel ate Neurospora).
- **The gin problem**: alcohol is nearly free (a bottle of gin < an apple). Drums that tax it get bootleggers using the food plant's methanol, which kills. Drums choose a point on that curve. Sorrel sells gin near cost with thiamine in it.
- **Animals are a rare luxury.** Tansy's forty hens (the only shell eggs on Herb Row) are famous. No pets to speak of, **no bees** (children pollinate the pears with brushes), no wild birds.

## People and society

- People look like all of humanity mixed together: medium brown on average, every old feature recombined; small jaws, light bones, corrected eyes; nobody tans. Names come from every Earth language, worn smooth over ages (canon names: Anselm, Teodora/Teo, Wren, Ossian, Hedda, Ilka, Juno, Mira, Tobiah, Bram, Marit, Jory, Oona, Hesk, Abelone, Tilde, Pell). Lives run ~120 years. Speech is "given in translation": plain, modern, unslangy.
- **Places**: each person holds one place (a share of habitat, beam and raft). It can't be sold. Most drums pass places to heirs (**holdings**); some pool them and issue **birth rights** (Sorrel lets rights be given, not sold; Lovage lets them be sold). Children are often **named for the dead whose places they take**, and families send a note to the dead's family each year.
- Drum types (at a quiet time):
  - **Open drums (~25%)**: hold births to deaths without coercion, keep a **Margin** of 2–4% of places empty (guest flats, refuge, first homes lent to young couples), post a **Ledger** at every station ring (places, people, Margin, births, deaths, guests, and each newborn's name beside the dead whose place they took), grant **guest-right** (any traveller may stay three nights in a Margin flat and eat at the halls), spend more of their wallets on each other's time. Kinds: **hall drums** (cooked halls, music, teaching; loud and crowded), **wood drums** (dim woods instead of bright squares, long silences; people carry slates), **fair drums** (light and spectacle, with the rents returned to everyone), **maker drums** (craft schools and one famous thing: chairs, flutes, wing-sleeves, starters).
  - **Steady drums (~60%)**: decent, near par, no Margin. Most join guest-right.
  - **Thin drums (~13%)**: overspent holdings, crowded, not counting.
  - **Counted drums (~1%)**: license births; fit a contraceptive implant at fourteen ("the fitting") during a birth stop; post **the Count** (people against places) at every platform. The darkest are rare and shunned. The **right of the Glide** (anyone may glide out of any drum) keeps most counted drums mild.
- Open-drum customs: **tables and aunts** (a child's table is parents plus three or four adults, all called aunt whatever their sex), **cooked halls** (one long table a block), **block courts** (a block of ~3,000 meets weekly in its atrium), **the assembly by lot** (~1,000 drawn each year), **visible mending**, **the long letter**, **light-painting** (lamp-painters compose the Hollow's dawns and dusks; a great dawn costs no more than an ordinary one), **heliox choirs**, **low-g dance** (leaping reels on third-g terraces).
- **Calendar** (chosen, since nothing outside makes a day or season; weekdays exist; a Thursday is a Thursday): **Booking Day** (the raft learns its beam for the year ahead: the harvest), **the Quiet** (the day before: the whole drum goes dark and sits in the halls and talks), **the Long Flight** (Booking night: the sunline stays dark and the Spindle opens end to end; hundreds of thousands fly the axis with hand lamps), **Knotnight** (the old ring-era winter turned into one evening: lamps down, the larder made together), **Homecoming** (wanderers met at the station ring by their block and table), **namings**.
- **The lamp clock**: lamps ramp up at about 06:30 in homes; lamp-night belongs to the machines (laundry at 1 a.m., when kWh are cheapest; batch work and the Yards run harder).
- **History**: **the Eleven Days**, a beam gap within living memory in Merrow (74 years ago): drums dropped to survival load, people ate their larders, the Glide kept running, and afterwards Merrow ran its cyclers fast as a store of energy. Every household keeps a **larder** as a point of pride. **The Dark** is a beam cut; **the Burn** (focusing the beam to destroy a raft) is the one unthinkable crime.
- **The Passing**: every few years another raft comes within a couple of AU. Choirs and kitchens trade songs and recipes with it across the light delay (33 minutes round trip at 2 AU). In canon a raft called **Selkie** passed during one wander year.
- **The sky** (only from Windows or the back of the raft): no near stars. **The River**, a broad band of pale-gold stars (the nuclear stellar disk, 100–200 parsecs out, centuries away by light), frayed at the edges with dark dust rifts whose brown-violet edges back crews call **"bruise"**; above and below it a fainter glow, **the Shelf**. Here and there, black shapes where nothing shines: **other rafts**, about a sixth of the sky. All the metal in everything, chairs and bodies, "fell out of the River": it's mined there and falls inward for a million years.

## Places already in canon (Merrow)

- **Herb Row**: a stretch of drums founded together and named for herbs. **Sorrel** (steady; allots high decks and Hollow hours by age; birth rights giftable not sold; gin near cost; the Daces live in Ashgrove on decks 73–78), **Lovage** (steady; auctions high decks and Hollow hours, spends rents on a 12-hour noon; rights can be sold; spirits taxed hard; brighter, newer clothes; "Lovage finds Sorrel dim and a little drunk"), **Tansy** (steady; strawberries, the Row's longest strain library, the only henhouse), **Chervil** (open hall drum, 3% Margin since a stop ~340 years ago; Marit Vell's hall at Station Ring 2; "lovely and a little pleased with itself"), **Rue** (counted drum, nine years into a birth stop after a blight hit its overspent households; ~11 years from par; its Blight Kitchen), **Yarrow** (maker drum; Nine, a Mill-only tasting menu), **Borage** (fair drum; Lightfall, which cooks recipes received by laser from other rafts).
- **Hedge Row**: **Sloe** (wood drum of silent woods; people carry slates; a woodwarden wrote LISTEN). **Gull Row**: **Kittiwake** (maker drum; wing-sleeves; teaches crosswise flying; children learn before they can read). **Weather Row**: **Gloaming** (fair drum; paints dusks; returns its terrace rents), **Squall** (steady; a wave-making swell on its Hollow lake; waves at half a g come in slow and tall). **Bell Row**: **Carillon** (hall drum of choirs; sang to Selkie). **Kelp Row** (the far side of Merrow): **Dulse** (hall drum; pulls noodles on third-g terraces; the old cook Abelone pulls one noodle per bowl). **Pigment Row** (grinds colours for half the raft): **Madder** (counted for 14 years after a vat fire, just took the Count down and kept a Margin), **Ochre** (steady; back crews take a guest for a shift if you sign for the dose). **Grain Row**: **Spelt** (maker drum; Pell the baker makes the **raft loaf**, a yearly mother starter built from spoons wanderers leave). Thimble, Cooper, Kite and Quill Rows also exist.
- The **Crosswise Guide** rates restaurants in **apple-seed stars**: one is worth the glide, two worth a cycler, three worth going dark for. The **Ladle** marks the best free commons food.

## Voice

- Plain, concrete, specific, warm, quietly funny. Short sentences. Physics as texture, never lecture. One exact detail beats three adjectives.
- **British spelling** (neighbour, colour, metre, grey, theatre, organise, licence as noun).
- People speak plainly, in translation: no modern Earth slang, no "folks", no "awesome", no "y'all", no corporate talk.
- Numbers are small and lived: "about a fifth of a kWh", "two glides over", "deck 286". Never invent a precise statistic that contradicts the figures above.
- **No em dashes** anywhere. Use commas, colons, full stops, or parentheses.
- Avoid clichés of AI prose: no "testament to", "tapestry", "bustling", "nestled", "whispers of", "a symphony of", "vibrant", "hum of life", "in a world where".
- Affectionate rivalry between neighbouring drums is the default social texture. Nobody is a villain. Problems are small and human (a missing crock, a noisy flute, a rota, a feud over the Court's light).
- Humour comes from the physics and the thrift: a coat with four owners' mends, a noodle that swims at zero g, a vote won "mostly because of the pears".

## Canon constraints (never break these)

- No sun, sky, weather, rain, wind or seasons inside a drum (Squall's swell is paddle-made; Gloaming's dusks are painted; "Weather Row" is just a name). No sunlight; say lamp-dawn, lamp-noon, lamp-night, the sunline.
- No stars visible from inside a drum. Only Windows, back crews, and the apple's seeds.
- No open fire, candles or wood stoves (cooking is electric; hand lamps, not candles). No smoking.
- No animals except very rare luxury ones (a henhouse is famous). No pets, bees, birds, fish ponds, horses. Animal words survive only as names.
- No ships, planets, moons, beaches with tides, mountains, forests in the Earth sense. Woods exist inside Hollows (yews, birches, pears, apples, bamboo, etc.), tended by woodwardens.
- No currency except kWh ("kays"). No buying land or places. No large families as a casual thing (two children is normal; a third is notable).
- Nobody lives on the Lantern; nobody sees it. Minds are biological. No robots as characters (robots exist as appliances: cleaning robots, cooking robots in halls).
- Nothing travels between rafts except light (messages, recipes, genomes); emigration is a rare swap during a Passing.
- Distances: neighbouring drums 24.5 km and 140 s by glide; across Merrow is weeks by slow cycler.
- Keep invented numbers modest and consistent with the price list.
