# Guest-Right

A first-person game set in **Chervil**, the open hall drum across the rows from Sorrel on Herb Row (`docs/open-drums.md`). There is no violence in it. You are a wanderer of twenty from the maker drum Spelt, eleven legs into your wander year, with three nights in Chervil on guest-right: the Thursday you arrive, the Quiet, and Booking Day. You carry a spoon of this year's mother from Pell the baker for Marit Vell, who cooks the Ring 2 hall. Everything else is the drum itself: a station under a vault, lamplit streets that curve up ahead of you, one table a hundred and twenty metres long, shops, a school, baths, flats, the Hollow with its far bank hanging three kilometres overhead, and on the last night the Long Flight down the axis in the dark.

The three days happen in one block, Candlewick. The rest of Chervil is there too. All 65,807 blocks of its indoor city can be walked into or reached by cab, each with its own name, streets, court, hall and cook; and the drum's great places are built by hand, from the vat halls of the Keel and a glide car out to Sorrel to the top of the end walls, both axles, and a Window on the stars.

Play it on the world-bible site at **https://jeff87654.github.io/Kerr-swarm/guest-right/**. A desktop browser with a real graphics card is best; it needs WebGL 2.

## Playing

| | |
|---|---|
| Walk, run, jump | W A S D, Shift, Space (a gamepad's sticks and A also work) |
| Look | the mouse (click the picture to take the pointer; where a browser refuses, drag instead) |
| Use, talk | E |
| Throw the spinball, once you have borrowed one | F or the right mouse button |
| Journal, map, photo | J, M, P (in the map, V changes the view: here, round the drum, the drum in section) |
| Cabs | E at a berth in any lift lobby. The list has Candlewick, the landmarks by part of the drum, and "Another block of Chervil" for any address |
| Menu | Esc |
| In the Spindle | W and S row you along your line of sight, Space and C lift and sink, E goes back to the gate |

The clock runs: a day takes forty-eight minutes unless you change it in the settings. Sleep in your flat or sit a while on a bench to pass time. The title screen's Chapters start you at the moments of the three days; the last of them, "The rest of the drum", starts you at Candlewick's cab berths with the errands done and everywhere open.

Money is energy: your wallet is 21.6 kWh a day, and the prices are the docs'. The physics is the drum's: you weigh 0.98 of a g in Candlewick and half that in the Hollow; running spinward makes you heavier; a thrown ball sinks spinward, floats anti and flies true along the axis; in the Spindle there is almost no weight at all and every crossing bends.

## What is in it

- **Station Ring 2** (the Keel): the glide platform and its armoured cars, the Ledger hall with the Ledger and the old Count board, the guest desk, the ring counter, a Quaysides lane, the Gate behind glass.
- **Candlewick**, a block of about three thousand on decks 16 to 21: Wick Court (a six-deck atrium with planted terraces), Ladle Row and Thread Street, Marit Vell's Hall and its kitchen, a bakery, a greengrocer, a larder shop, a herb shop, a mender, a dyer and tailor, a chair remaker, a flute maker, Oona's lamp-painting studio, a book room, a toy library, a clinic, a block office, a choir room, a laundry, a fernery, the baths, the school and its spinball hall, two lift lobbies, and five flats (yours among them).
- **The Hollow**: the whole floor, 9.4 km round and 3.75 km long, can be walked: a lake, orchards, lawns, dim woods at the ends, a lift pavilion, a festival green and bandstand, a jetty and robe house, the orchard's working corner, a kite field. The far bank you see overhead is the same land, drawn from the same map.
- **The back wall**: the dawn terrace and the Dawn Stand, the Third (a third of a g, for reels), the flying hall's gate and the sleeve hire.
- **The Spindle**: the netted flying hall by day, and the whole axis on Booking night.
- **The rest of the indoor city**, generated from each block's address: 58 layers of decks from deck 16 to deck 375, 45 to 88 blocks round each and 17 along, 65,807 blocks in all, at weights from 0.98 of a g down to a half. Walk out of Candlewick under the arch at either end of Ladle Row and you are in Ropewalk or Pennyfold; keep going spinward for eighteen and a half kilometres and you come back to where you started. Every block has an avenue and a street that cross in a court two to six decks tall, a hall with one long table where a guest has a seat, a lift core with cabs and a stair to the blocks above and below, a dozen shops by trade, the rooms a block keeps for itself, and one or two larger places: a school, a bath house, a play hall, an inn, a market, workshops by the hour, a theatre, reading rooms, light studios, a tea dance hall, a cradle arcade, naming rooms, a dim wood, a bright square, a still room, a lamp row, a garden. From the top decks the last stair comes up through the floor of the Hollow, where a lift pavilion stands at every crossing of the paths.
- **Landmarks**, built by hand and loaded when a cab takes you there (or, in the Hollow, when you walk near):
  - *The Keel:* the Flour Hall (vats at sixty to seventy degrees, and the Kettle, where their heat joins the loop); the Garden (six double rows of racks 320 m long behind glass, a tomato house, tea rows, mushroom rooms); the Loop Hall (plate exchangers the size of houses, a gin kitchen and salt pans on the same heat); the strain library (a cold room five decks tall worked by stacker cranes, and a wall of a thousand cooks' doors).
  - *The stations:* Station Ring 5 (the crossing, under a board of every drum on Herb Row, with the rim trains beneath); Station Ring 9 (the late station and its noodle counter); and the Glide, a car to Sorrel and back, weightless for the crossing, with one port that looks aft between the drums.
  - *The line decks:* the line works (where cabs are mended and lost things wait a year and a day); the Press and the Soufflé (rides at 1.2 g and 0.3 g).
  - *The decks:* the Spice Rack and the Spouts (a climbing shaft eight decks tall, where a dropped chalk bag lands 2.75 m anti, and three slides timed against each other); the Measure (a mile to run heavy or light against the clock, and the Tin for spinball); the Bellows (an organ whose bottom pipe is 17.4 m long, which a guest may play, with a concert hall hung in felt and a sound archive); the lot hall (the assembly's bowl of 956 chairs); the Simmer (the slow-water pool on deck 340, among the elders).
  - *The Hollow:* Lid Island (a rope ferry, a bench for anyone a hundred or over, a high board) and the Cold Rows (apple trees wintering under tents); the Breadboard (a 400 m track with a heavy bend and a light one, and spinball pitches); the front wood and the Long Lamps.
  - *The end walls:* the Warming Shelf and the front Third on the front wall (the dusk terrace and the wedding floor); the Quarter, the Tenth and the Twentieth on the back wall, where a standing jump goes fourteen metres.
  - *The axles:* the back axle (the heat hall, the slow ring, the still side and the Window, where the shutter rises on the River); the front axle (the Cold Room, the Lap, the Slow Pour).
- People: about two thousand in the places of the three days, and more in every block and landmark as you reach it, with lives that follow the hour and the day (meals fill the hall, the Quiet empties the screens, Booking Day sends everyone to the dawn, the Ledger, the feast and the Spindle); fifty-seven of them named, with something to say that changes by the day.
- Things to do: twenty-four threads in a journal, a hundred and forty-two things to find, some thousands of things to read, sit on, knock at, buy, borrow or try, rides to take (a slide, a loop at 1.2 g, a glide car, a rope ferry, a dive, a mile against the clock), a wallet of 21.6 kWh a day, a card home to write on the fourth morning, and two endings. About eleven hundred lamps and a thousand painted or chalked signs in Candlewick alone.

## Canon status

The setting follows the docs: `docs/open-drums.md` (Chervil, its customs, and the Booking Day scene the third day stages), `docs/wander-year.md` (Marit, Pell, Spelt, the cards), `docs/eating-out.md`, `docs/cuisine.md`, `docs/daily-life.md`, `docs/raft-habitat.md`, `docs/drum-transport.md`, `docs/raft-transport.md`, `docs/ashgrove.md`, `docs/sorrel.md`, `docs/families.md`, `docs/people.md`, `docs/economy.md`. Marit Vell, Jory, Oona, Pell, the Ledger and its figures, the hall, the Dawn Stand, the ring counter, the Quiet, Booking Day, the vote and the Long Flight are theirs.

**Introduced here, and not canon** (ideas worth keeping should be written into `docs/` on purpose):

- The block **Candlewick** and everything in it: Wick Court and its lamp-painters' ceiling, Ladle Row, Thread Street, every shop and its name, the fernery, the block office, the choir room, the laundry, the plan of the hall (one room 124 m long) and of the flats.
- The staging of the three days round the player: arriving on the Thursday, the draw of a terrace flat, the errands, the block court's sitting on the evening of the Quiet, the hours given to each moment of Booking Day.
- Fifty-three of the fifty-seven named people (all but Marit, Jory, Oona and Pell), their work, and everything they say.
- The plan of Chervil's Hollow (where its lake, orchards, lawns and woods lie), the layout of Station Ring 2, the dawn terrace, the Third and the flying hall's gate as built.
- The colours and timing of Oona's dawn beyond what `open-drums.md` gives (the brown-violet of the River, rose, the far bank gold while the near side is dark).
- The whole of the generated city: the grid of 65,807 blocks (the method is the Sorrel Atlas's, itself not canon) and everything made from a block's address: its name, streets, cook, hall, dish, shops, rooms and people.
- Every landmark's name, plan, people and customs, and where it stands: the docs say what a drum has (vat halls, garden racks, a strain library, nine station rings, line decks, a slow-water pool for the old, climbing shafts, miles, an organ in heliox, an assembly drawn by lot, end walls that climb to the axis) and are silent on where Chervil keeps its own. Among the larger liberties: Chervil having its own Window behind the back axle; the tiers of the end walls fifty metres apart; the Soufflé's own tube, faster than the Lattice allows; a glide car that brings you back without setting you down in Sorrel.
- The lift pavilions of the Hollow and the last stair up to them; the stair in every lift core.
- Small customs and details invented by the builders and writers: these are listed in `content/not-canon.md`, which has a part for the three days and a part for the rest of the drum.

## Layout

- `src/main.js` starts the game; `src/dev.js` is the development entry (it loads each place on its own, so one broken file does not stop the rest).
- `src/core/`: the drum's geometry and physics (`drum.js`), input, settings, saving, the clock.
- `src/render/`: the renderer. A deferred pipeline on three.js r170 (vendored in `vendor/`): G-buffer, hundreds of clustered lamps, overhead soft shadows, ambient occlusion, screen-space reflections, temporal anti-aliasing, bloom, automatic exposure, a filmic grade. `texgen.js` makes every surface texture on the GPU at start-up; `atlas.js` holds every painted or chalked word; `shell.js` draws the Hollow's far bank, end walls and sunline by ray.
- `src/city/`: the rest of Chervil. `plan.js` lays a block out from its address, `streets.js`, `court.js` and `lots.js` build it, `streamer.js` keeps the blocks round the player built a few milliseconds a frame and lets go of the rest, `pavilions.js` does the same for the Hollow's lift pavilions, `picker.js` is the cab's list of addresses. `src/core/layout.js` is the grid.
- `src/world/`: the building kit (`kit.js`, `kit2.js`, `props.js`, `signs.js`, `doors.js`, `meshbuilder.js`), the world container (`world.js`) and the Hollow's map (`land.js`).
- `src/places/`: one module per place, listed in `src/places.json`. `lots_*.js` are the kinds of room a generated block can have (shops by trade, halls, rooms, schools, baths and the rest); `lm_*.js` are the landmarks, each a region that `src/game/regions.js` builds when somebody goes there and takes down afterwards.
- `src/sim/`: the player, collision, and the people.
- `src/game/`: using things, zones, the wallet, the journal and conversations, cabs, the flow of the game (title, chapters, saving, the ending), and the music director that chooses which tune is playing.
- `src/ui/`, `src/audio/`: the interface and the sound (all synthesised).
- `src/content/`: the words: cast, places, signs, menus, items, discoveries, overheard talk, tips, customs, the Ledger, conversations and threads.
- `content/brief.md`, `content/place-brief.md`, `content/expansion-brief.md`: the briefs the game was built from. `content/drum-digest.md`: what the docs say about the inside of a drum, with sources. `ARCHITECTURE.md`: the contract between the parts, and the plan of the world.

No assets are loaded: geometry, textures, text, people, sound and music are all made in code. The only binary files are four open-licensed font families in `assets/fonts/`.

## Building

```
python tools/build.py                    # dist/guest-right.html (the artifact page) and dist/index.html (standalone)
python tools/build.py --standalone DIR   # also DIR/index.html, for the world-bible site
python tools/build.py --check            # the module rules and the writing rules
```

The build has no dependencies: it bundles the ES modules itself (see "Rules for every source file" in `ARCHITECTURE.md`), wraps three.js and inlines the fonts into one HTML file of about nine megabytes. The world-bible site build (`site/build.py`) runs the standalone build into `_site/guest-right/` and links it from the home page and the sidebar.

## Developing and testing

```
python tools/serve.py                    # http://localhost:5190/ : the game straight from src/, never cached
npm install                              # once, for the screenshot tools (puppeteer-core; Chrome or Edge must be installed)
node tools/shot.mjs --q "at=court&t=13" --shot out/a.png
node tools/shot.mjs --q "only=test&at=test" --shot "out/b.png|1|game.player.yaw = 1.2"
```

`tools/shot.mjs` loads the game in headless Chrome on the real GPU, runs JavaScript, takes pictures and prints frames per second, draw calls, triangles, lights and console errors. `tools/play.mjs` plays the game through from the title with real key presses and a picture at every step: `node tools/play.mjs opening evening quiet booking ending credits screens shops fullflight phone` covers the three days, the Long Flight (landed early and flown the whole way), the last scene and the credits, every screen, saving and continuing, every shopkeeper at their counter, and a phone held both ways; `node tools/play.mjs tour` photographs every named place by day and by night, and `python tools/sheet.py out/play/tour out/sheets` lays the pictures out as contact sheets (it needs Pillow). Add `--page dist/index.html` to play the built file instead of the source. For the rest of the drum: `node tools/play.mjs drum` walks out of Candlewick into the next block, eats at its hall, takes a cab to the High decks, climbs the stairs and the last stair into the Hollow, rides to a landmark, saves and continues; `node tools/play.mjs rides` starts every set piece from its own prompt (a slide, the Press and the Soufflé, the Glide out and back, the rope ferry and the high board, the wall lift, the Lake Stair, the Window, the last strides of a mile) and checks that each hands the player back; `node tools/tour.mjs` rides a cab to every landmark stop in turn and reports what it found there; `node tools/lots.mjs` builds one of every kind of place a block can hold and stands outside and inside it; `node tools/walk.mjs` holds a key down and reports any fall or stop; `node tools/prof.mjs` takes a CPU profile. Useful URL parameters: `only=id,id` (build only those places), `at=<spawn>`, `pos=x,y,z`, `t=<hour>`, `day=1|2|3`, `freeze`, `q=low|medium|high|ultra`, `debug=1..6` (G-buffer views), `cell=L:i:j&lot=<kind>` (start in a generated block, in front of one of its lots), `citylog` (print what each block cost to build), `nocity`. `tools/ui-dev.html` and `tools/audio-dev.html` exercise the interface and the sound on their own; `tools/check-content.mjs` and `tools/check-story.mjs` check the words; `tools/check-found.mjs`, `tools/check-threads.mjs` and `tools/check-flags.mjs` check that every thing to find can be found, every step of every thread can be done, and no conversation waits on a flag that nothing sets; `tools/audio-check.mjs` renders every sound and tune and checks its level.
