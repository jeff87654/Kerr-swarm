# Drum digest: the inside of a nation drum, and Chervil

A builders' reference for making the whole of Chervil playable. Every fact carries its source. "(Sorrel)" marks facts the docs give for Sorrel, the steady drum next door, which shares Chervil's physics, size, structure and station rings but not its customs. Where a number below is worked out from a doc's formula rather than quoted, it says "derived". Where the docs say nothing, this file says "the docs are silent".

Compass words used throughout (sorrel.md, Addresses): "Up is in, down is out." Up is toward the axis and lighter; down is toward the rim. "Spinward" and "antispinward" ("anti") are the two ways round; "front" and "back" the two ways along. The front is the power end, the back the heat end (sorrel.md, The nine Rings).

## 1. Geometry and numbers of a nation drum

### The drum
- Rim radius 3,000 m at 1 g; length 3,750 m; one turn every 110 s, ω = 0.0572 rad/s; 0.55 rpm; rim speed 172 m/s (habitat-design.md, Sizes; drum-transport.md, Method).
- Squat cylinder, length about 1.25 R, "passively spin-stable" (habitat-design.md, Geometry).
- About 200 M people. Chervil: 194,060,000 on Booking Day (open-drums.md, Chervil).
- 376 decks every 4 m from the rim (deck 1) to the Hollow floor at 1,500 m (0.5 g) (drum-transport.md, Setting). Deck n is at r = 3,000 - 4(n - 1) m and weighs r ÷ 3,000 of rim weight (sorrel.md, Method).
- Decks move at 86 to 172 m/s (drum-transport.md, Setting).
- 100 m² of floor a person: "~35 m² of home, 15 of workplace, 15 of schools, clinics, shops and halls, 25 of streets and lift lobbies ... 5 of parks and 5 of Keel" (raft-habitat.md, A deck up close).
- Sorrel's 376 decks hold "~19,900 km² of floor, about the area of Wales, in ~69,400 neighbourhoods of ~3,000 people each. Visiting one a day would take 190 years" (sorrel.md, Summary).
- Each deck is "a city of 350,000 to 700,000 people, 9 to 19 km around and 3.75 km wide" (raft-habitat.md, Summary). Deck 201 is 13.8 km around with ~520,000 people; the rim deck 18.8 km around, the innermost 9.4 km (raft-habitat.md, A deck up close).
- Air is heliox: 21 kPa oxygen in ~80 kPa helium, ~101 kPa, 0.40 kg/m³ (habitat-design.md, Assumptions; drum-transport.md, Setting). Pressure rises ~4.4 kPa from the Hollow floor to the rim (drum-transport.md, Setting). Rooms run at ~295 K (raft-habitat.md, The hull's two loops).
- Sound runs ~1.8× faster; voices and wind instruments ~10 semitones sharp; helium chills skin, "so habitats run warm" (habitat-design.md, Life-inside numbers).

### Structure, from outside in
- **The jacket:** 50 m of liquid hydrogen at 20 K, ~450 Mt, over hull and caps, with ~100 m more over the front cap (raft-habitat.md, How a drum sits in its cell; habitat-design.md, Radiation environment). Sorrel's Cold Plant keeps it cold, "spending forty-seven watts to pump each stray watt of heat back out" (sorrel.md, The Keel).
- **The guard vacuum:** "the narrow gap of nothing between Sorrel's hull and its jacket, where cryopanels freeze every atom the hull leaks" (sorrel.md, Outside the hull). Frost crews warm the panels a bank at a time, "the most dangerous chore in the drum" (element-cycles.md, Culture and story hooks).
- **The moat:** ~0.5 m of the drum's own water, ~51 g/cm², outside the jacket over the sides and front cap: ~53 Mt of the drum's ~80 Mt of water. It is the X-ray shield and the water supply, "piped across the jacket to the Keel" (habitat-design.md, The moat). The other ~27 Mt is inside: the lake, trim tanks and a reserve (habitat-design.md, The moat).
- **The quay:** a band round the waist at ~3,050 m, outside jacket and moat, moving at 174.4 m/s at ~1.02 g, with cradles on a track (raft-transport.md, The Glide).
- **Three ports:** "power comes in at the front cap, heat leaves at the back cap, and people and goods leave at the rim" (raft-habitat.md, Summary). ~160 GW comes in through the front axle's rotary joint; ~160 GW of heat leaves through the back axle's as two-phase ammonia, "a river's worth" (raft-habitat.md, How a drum sits in its cell).
- **Radiators** are not on the drum: fixed panels across the raft's back, ~390 km² for one drum's hull (raft-habitat.md, How a drum sits in its cell).
- Structure ~100 kg a person; contents 50 kg/m² (habitat-design.md, Assumptions; Sizes).

### The Hollow, the sunline and the Spindle
- **The Hollow:** r < 1,500 m, 3 km across, 3.75 km long, a quarter of the drum's volume. Floor ~35 km² at 0.5 g, 0.18 m² a person (raft-habitat.md, Districts). Floor 9.4 km around (ashgrove.md, Day two).
- **The sunline:** a lamp on a tensioned spar "from axle to axle"; the lit part stops ~450 m short of each end wall, so it is ~2.85 km long (raft-habitat.md, The sunline and the Spindle).
- **Schedule:** full sun (100,000 lux on the floor, ~330 W/m²) for ~6 hours around midday, ramping over 3 hours either side (raft-habitat.md, Districts; daily-life.md, Light). Ashgrove's day gives the times: "a three-hour dawn. By nine it's too bright to look at ... for six hours; then come three hours of dusk", and "At three the sunline begins to ease" (ashgrove.md, Day two). So dawn 06:00 to 09:00, full sun 09:00 to 15:00, dusk 15:00 to 18:00 (open-drums.md's 06:00 dawn agrees).
- **Power:** ~12 GW at full sun; ~2.4 GW of it is heat carried down the spar to the back axle (raft-habitat.md, The sunline and the Spindle). Light near the line: ~1.0 kW/m² at 500 m, ~1.7 at 300 m, ~6.7 at 75 m, "Painful within seconds; burns within a minute" (raft-habitat.md, table).
- **Light on the end walls:** the floor at the foot of an end wall gets ~38% of mid-floor; terraces 750 m from the axis ~0.14 kW/m²; top terraces 150 m from the axis ~25 W/m² direct "plus the Hollow's glow" (raft-habitat.md, table). The Hollow's own glow is ~50 to 80 W/m², 15,000 to 25,000 lux, "a bright overcast day" (raft-habitat.md, The sunline and the Spindle).
- **The Spindle:** the axis; within ~75 m you weigh under 3% of a g (raft-habitat.md, Districts). Its middle opens when the line is under ~14% of full: "the last ~25 minutes of dusk, the lamp-night and the first ~25 minutes of dawn, about 13 hours a day" (raft-habitat.md, The sunline and the Spindle). Volume ~6.6 × 10⁷ m³; ~660,000 flyers at once at ~100 m³ each (open-drums.md, Method).
- **The axles:** "plant: power in at the front, coolant out at the back" (raft-habitat.md, Districts).

## 2. How the indoor city is laid out

### Streets and blocks
- "Avenues run spinward and streets along the axis, crossing them. Both are for walking and cycling" (raft-habitat.md, A deck up close). Walking spinward you are heavier, anti lighter; along the axis, weight never changes: Pear Street is "where your weight stays the same whichever way you walk" (sorrel.md, Low decks).
- Ceilings: "Decks are 4 m apart, so ceilings are ~3 m. Most of the drum is an indoor city of streets and arcades. The Hollow and the atria, parks cut through several decks, are the only ceilings higher than a room" (raft-habitat.md, A deck up close).
- **A block (Sorrel, Ashgrove):** ~3,000 people on six decks (73 to 78), "a block roughly 230 m on a side, served by five lift cores"; "an indoor town of arcades, lamp-lit about as brightly as a living room" (ashgrove.md, Ashgrove).
- **The atrium (Sorrel, the Court):** "cut up through all six decks. Its floor ... about 90 by 60 m, and its ceiling is the underside of deck 79, 24 m up. Each deck above opens onto it as a ring of planted terraces" (ashgrove.md, Ashgrove). Lit to 10,000 lux for twelve hours, terraces "about a quarter of that" (~2,700 lux) (ashgrove.md, Ashgrove; Method).
- **A block's street plan (Sorrel):** the Long Arcade runs spinward across Ashgrove on deck 75 "with a slow trolley down the middle"; Pear Street runs along the axis and crosses it at the Court (ashgrove.md, Ashgrove).
- **The atlas grid (Sorrel, explicitly not canon):** residential decks cut into 58 layers of four to eight decks, each into 18 blocks along the axis and ~45 to 88 round it: 69,426 blocks of ~2,000 to 4,000 people, ~210 m on a side. "None of the generated places is canon" (sorrel.md, What the atlas generates).
- **Chervil's block:** ~3,000 people, "Ashgrove's size", with an atrium where the block court meets (open-drums.md, Voice at three scales). The docs name no Chervil block, street or atrium.

### Addresses
- **Sorrel:** deck, Ring, Hour, said in that order: "Seventy-seven, Three, Bee" (sorrel.md, Summary). Nine Rings are 417 m slices along the axis, Keel to Hollow, named for the station ring beneath; twelve Hours are 30° wedges counted spinward, named for animals (sorrel.md, The nine Rings; The twelve Hours). Counting starts at "a worn bronze plate on Station Ring One's platform wall" (sorrel.md, Addresses).
- The Lattice numbers its ~5,200 lift cores, "but nobody says those aloud" (sorrel.md, Addresses). A drum's formal address on the raft is "a pair of grid numbers that nobody says aloud" (ashgrove.md, Merrow and Herb Row).
- **Chervil:** the docs are silent on how Chervil addresses a block. They use "Station Ring 2" and "the Ring 2 hall" (open-drums.md; wander-year.md). Sorrel's Hours are a Sorrel custom ("named by the founders").

### Light
- Homes ~200 lux; lamps "ramp up at half past six, a long even day at about 200 lux, a fade toward eleven" (ashgrove.md, The flat; daily-life.md, Light). They come up "over twenty minutes, warm and then white" (ashgrove.md, Day one).
- A morning light panel: twenty minutes at 2,500 lux, ~0.01 kWh (ashgrove.md, Day one).
- Streets and corridors are commons light, part of ~45 W a head for interiors (daily-life.md, Light).
- Parks: ~53 W a head; "A neighbourhood of 1,000 people can have one bright square of ~2,600 m² or a dim wood of ~2.6 ha. Which one is a civic choice" (daily-life.md, Light).
- A private 10 m² garden at overcast brightness costs 4.8 kWh a day, ~22% of a wallet. "A bright home is the plainest sign of wealth" (daily-life.md, Light).

### Windows
- Flats have none: "Almost nothing in Sorrel does, apart from the flats on the Court's terraces and the terraces of the Hollow's end walls" (ashgrove.md, The flat). The end-wall riser flats and atrium-terrace flats "are the only homes in Sorrel with windows" (sorrel.md, The Hollow's plan).

### Homes and how they are held
- **A place:** "one household's home, ~78 m², about 2¼ people's share"; ~90 M in a drum; passes "whole to one heir", usually a great-grandchild in their late 20s; can't be sold or pledged; "Its rooms can be let" (economy.md, The place).
- **A typical flat (Sorrel):** the Daces' on deck 77, "about 125 m², four rooms and a kitchen: the ~78 m² of their place, and two rooms they rent from the old couple next door. It has no windows" (ashgrove.md, The flat). Contents: an inherited table, four chairs that don't match, a starter crock, a metre-long herb shelf under a grow lamp, a larder cupboard (ashgrove.md, The flat).
- **An elder's flat (Sorrel):** Hedda's on deck 361 is "small, and it's the brightest place the children know": a 4 m² garden lit like an overcast day, ~1.9 kWh a day, with a tomato vine (ashgrove.md, Day two).
- **Rent:** in a steady drum ~0.1 kWh per m² a day, a whole place ~7.8 kWh a day; in an open drum "rents there run about half that" (economy.md, Homes).
- **Chervil's Margin flats:** ~2,673,000 places held by the drum, for "guest flats, refuge for people fleeing dark drums, studios, first homes lent to young couples for up to five years, and homes for life lent to placeless couples" (open-drums.md, A Margin). About 1 M are on loan to couples at once; "its lottery only decides who gets which flat: the terraces and the Hollow's edge go by draw, the dim decks to whoever's left" (open-drums.md, Children without counting). The docs do not define "the terraces", "the Hollow's edge" or "the dim decks" further.
- **Guests:** in Chervil, a Margin flat for three nights (open-drums.md, Open both ways). Sorrel "keeps no Margin, so guests sleep in inns the drum keeps near the station rings" (sorrel.md, Government).

### Civic buildings per block
- Ashgrove (Sorrel) has, on its arcade and street: a Mill Hall (three free meals a day), baths, a greengrocer, a mending shop "between a clinic and a stair", a school on deck 75 that "takes the children of Ashgrove and the blocks around it", and a gin cellar (ashgrove.md, Ashgrove; eating-out.md).
- Chervil: "one long table a block" with a paid cook and volunteers (open-drums.md, An economy of each other); "live music every night somewhere in every block"; "Every block has a choir" (open-drums.md, An economy of each other; Culture).
- The docs give no count of schools, clinics, baths or inns per so many people beyond the floor split (15 m² a person for "schools, clinics, shops and halls").

## 3. Getting about inside a drum

### The Lattice
- **Cabs:** 6 seats or a wheelchair, pram or cart; ~300 kg, ~3.5 m long, 2 m wide, 2.1 m tall; linear motors, magnetic guidance, "no ropes, wheels or engines on board" (drum-transport.md, Cabs).
- **One party per cab:** "You call a cab at your lobby, and it takes you and whoever you're with, nobody else" (drum-transport.md, Cabs). A cab usually waits in the berth; the wait is ~15 s or less.
- **The gimbal:** the cabin tilts against Coriolis "about three-quarters of the way", leaving ~0.06 g sideways. At 20 m/s a full tilt would be 13° at the Keel, 17° at mid-drum, 25° near the Hollow, ~31° at the worst moment; riders 1.03 to 1.10× their weight (drum-transport.md, Cabs; Lift cores).
- **Sealed:** cabs carry their own heliox; "A full cab's air lasts about an hour"; scrubbers and oxygen hoods (drum-transport.md, Cabs).
- **Lift cores:** "a core every ~100 m, running through all 376 decks; ~5,200 cores of ~38,000 people each"; ten channels a core (two express and three collectors each way) (drum-transport.md, Lift cores). Each lobby has four berths and serves ~100 to 115 people; ~320 m² per core per deck including "a waiting area and stairs" (drum-transport.md, Capacity).
- **Speeds:** radial 20 m/s at 1.5 m/s²; tubes 30 m/s on cross-lines; loop limits depend on direction and depth, "Spinward runs high, antispinward runs low" (drum-transport.md, Line decks). Loop limits: line deck 13 spinward 17.5 m/s (1.20 g), anti 30 (0.67 g); line deck 363 spinward 30 (0.93 g), anti 21 (0.30 g) (drum-transport.md, Speed limits).
- **Line decks:** tubes ~3 × 5 m; loops round the drum (74 a line deck), cross-lines along the axis (141 columns); junctions turn at ~10 m/s on 70 m curves, ~20 s a turn. Tubes take a third of a line deck; "The rest is cab depots, workshops and freight sorting" (drum-transport.md, Line decks).
- **Helium in the tubes:** "nothing can burn in them"; "Crews wear masks" (drum-transport.md, Keeping drag down).
- **How it feels:** "The cab is a room. Most people know the Lattice as a small private room that hums, leans a little and opens somewhere else. Nobody sees the tubes" (drum-transport.md, Culture). Loop signs post two limits; "Some people choose their route for the feeling" (drum-transport.md, Culture). Teo's ride: "drops her 76 decks in about thirty seconds, its cabin leaning a little against the Coriolis push and her weight creeping up from 0.9 g to a full g, then sidesteps along the Keel" (ashgrove.md, Day one).

### Walking, cycling, stairs, carts
- Walking speed falls with weight: ~1.35 m/s on the Low decks, ~1.15 mid-drum, ~1.0 on the High decks (drum-transport.md, Streets).
- Cycling is allowed on avenues and streets; "some decks may keep slow trolleys for short hops" (drum-transport.md, Streets). The docs name no bicycle anywhere.
- "Every core has" stairs. The Keel to mid-drum is "a 660 m climb in Earth terms (~540 kcal)" (drum-transport.md, Side effects). "Walking costs food. A long walk is a small indulgence" (drum-transport.md, Culture).
- No vehicles run on the Hollow floor (drum-transport.md, The Hollow and the Spindle).
- **Freight** rides the same cabs off-peak: "food and goods up from the Keel and the Quaysides, waste back down", a few kg a person a day (drum-transport.md, Side effects). Sorrel's Night Sort packs "the day's goods ... into carts by lobby and sent up in idle cabs overnight" (sorrel.md, Line decks).
- Any cab can be an ambulance, "a stretcher lies level on the gimballed floor" (drum-transport.md, Side effects).

### Into the Hollow and up to the axis
- "One core in four rises through the Hollow's floor as a pavilion, ~1,300 in all" (drum-transport.md, The Hollow and the Spindle); "one every 170 m" (sorrel.md, The Hollow).
- **Spindle lifts:** 1.5 km "in a little over 2 minutes at 12 m/s", arriving in the flying halls. "In the last ~400 m the Coriolis push outweighs what's left of the spin's pull, so the cabin swings until 'down' points sideways. In the last ~50 m, braking takes over and 'down' swings toward the axis, then fades to nothing" (drum-transport.md, The Hollow and the Spindle). By the top Ossian is "holding her sleeve so as not to drift away" (ashgrove.md, Day four).

## 4. The Keel and the works

The docs describe the Keel's contents for any nation drum and in detail for Sorrel. For Chervil they say only that the Ledger stands at every station ring.

### Food plant
- **The Mill:** abiotic chemistry, about two-thirds of calories: electrolysis, methanol synthesis, enzyme cascades for starch and sugar, chain growth for fat, amino acids (food-system.md, Tier 1). Methanol is "the main food feedstock", ~0.8 kg a person a day, a dozen lethal doses (food-system.md, Feedstocks).
- **The Vats:** "microbes, fungi and cell culture fed by the Mill": protein flour, precision casein, whey, egg, gelatin and gluten, mycelium steaks, oils, algae, plant cells (coffee, cocoa), cultivated meat, brewing (food-system.md, Tier 2). Thermophilic strains run at 60 to 70 °C, and "Their waste heat also supplies the kitchens' hot water" (food-system.md, Tier 2).
- **The Garden:** LED hydroponics for herbs, greens, tomatoes, cucumbers, strawberries, alliums, tea; tree fruit "is a delicacy" (food-system.md, Tier 3). About 1 m² of racks a person.
- Floor: the food system takes ~0.5 m² a person "on the rim decks" (food-system.md, Mass and floor space).
- **The air plant is the food plant:** electrolysers make ~1.2 kg of oxygen a person a day (food-system.md, Summary).
- **What it looks like (Sorrel):** the Air Halls (electrolysers, decks 5 to 11); Methanol Row ("a street of synthesis reactors"); the Cascade ("the quietest hall in the food plant ... enzymes in tall columns"); the Long Vats ("most of a kilometre of thermophilic vat halls"); the Steak Halls; the Dairy (yeast vats, "line numbers that skip a four"); the Blue Hall (algae); the Red Racks ("five decks of Garden racks under red-weighted lamps ... in air too thin in oxygen for the crews to breathe"); the Tea Racks; the Mushroom Rooms ("cool, wet, dim"); Nine Hall; the Still; the Board ("a wall of lights shows every line") (sorrel.md, The Keel).
- **Blights** are phages; each line has a Mill "blight menu" while it regrows from library stock (~3 days for protein flour, a week for yeast and fungi, a month or more for animal cells) (food-system.md, Risks).

### Water, heat and the tide
- Water comes in from the moat "in vacuum-sleeved pipes, is cleaned, and goes up to every tap" (sorrel.md, The Crossings).
- **The 65 °C loop:** the Vats' waste heat, made into "the 65 °C loop that warms every bath and kitchen tap" at Sorrel's Sixty-Five exchanger hall (sorrel.md, The Keel). Boiling a litre from the 65 °C tap costs 0.045 kWh (daily-life.md, Prices).
- Heat leaves in two loops: rooms' ~0.45 kW a person at ~283 K, the food plant's ~0.35 kW at ~330 K (raft-habitat.md, The hull's two loops).
- **The tide:** a drum whose commuters glide out each morning sends ~3 Mt out through its rim and slows ~0.1%, so ~3 Mt of moat water is pumped to tanks at mid-drum, and "the water goes back out at night through turbines". "A drum with as many visitors as commuters doesn't need to" (drum-transport.md, Side effects). In Sorrel: ~120 tanks, "from about 07:00, fullest about 10:00, and back out ... after 21:00"; couples sit on the Tide Stair to hear it (sorrel.md, Customs). Whether Chervil runs a tide the docs are silent.

### Reserves, waste and salvage
- Methanol tank farm ~19 Mt with ~29 Mt of liquid oxygen; survival reserve ~12 Mt of banked oxygen and fuel cells; together the drum survives a Dark ~55 days, ~90 with a full farm (raft-habitat.md, table; glossary.md, Survival reserve).
- Waste goes through supercritical-water oxidation "to water, gas and salt" (sorrel.md, The Oxidisers); salt is crystallised "on warm pans and fortified with thiamine and iodine" (sorrel.md, The Salt House).
- "The dead come home": bodies go back into the loop, and "funerals are about what's kept: a name, a voice, a pinch of ash in glass" (element-cycles.md, Rules; Culture and story hooks). Sorrel has nine Down Halls in the Keel, one per Ring (sorrel.md, Customs).
- Recyclers check every gram of scarce metal "against its passport" (sorrel.md, The Take-Apart). The Yards (industry) are outside, in the mid-raft (raft-habitat.md).
- Cellars: the Booking Day feast's "wine and cheese in the cellars" are made in the weeks before (open-drums.md, The calendar). Where the cellars are, the docs are silent.

### The Quaysides, the Gate and the strain library
- "The Quaysides, the blocks around the station rings, are the busiest in the drum: markets, freight, and quarantine for anything biological, since here a phage is a famine" (raft-habitat.md, Districts).
- **The Gate (Sorrel):** "a long room off the inbound platform, with clean benches, a sequencer bay and a cold room". Crates arrive "sealed and certified"; one in fifty is spot-checked (ashgrove.md, Day one).
- **The strain library (Sorrel):** "every vat culture and registered household starter, kept as a frozen vial and a genome, with an isolation vault you can visit" (sorrel.md, The Keel).

### The station rings
- Nine, at z = (2i + 1) L ÷ 18 (drum-transport.md, Method), so 417 m apart. "Each ring is a station four decks deep round the whole drum: glide trains along the rim deck, cab berths on island platforms on the three decks above" (drum-transport.md, Station rings).
- Per ring at the peak: ~1.85 M riders an hour each way; ~23,000 cab berths, "~100 km of kerb, six faces round the 18.8 km ring"; 1,000-seat trains (100 m long) "one every ~1.9 s each way"; ~50 trains at platforms; "~60 locks" through the jacket (drum-transport.md, Station rings; Method).
- "Each ring passes as many people every hour as Shinjuku ... does in a day" (drum-transport.md, Station rings).
- Cabs reach a ring by running down a core to the rim deck and sidestepping along the axis, at most ~210 m (drum-transport.md, The Keel).
- **Which ring goes where:** "Every drum in a row shares its lane levels, so Sorrel's are the same as Lovage's and Chervil's" (sorrel.md, The nine Rings). In Sorrel, Rings Two, Four, Six and Eight "train to Chervil and Rue", Rings One, Three, Seven and Nine to Lovage and Tansy, and Ring Five is "the tubes: cyclers and anywhere further" (sorrel.md, The nine Rings). Where Chervil's own nine rings lead, the docs are silent.
- **What a ring is like (Sorrel):** a bronze plate at Ring One; a booking hall "under a clock with three faces" at Ring Six; a refuge desk "its kettle on whenever the trains run" at Ring Eight; noodles till the last train at Ring Nine; "The Sorting" sends commuters "home three to a cab" (sorrel.md, Landmarks).
- **In Chervil:** the Ledger "at every station ring"; the Ledger hall with the old Count board (open-drums.md, Chervil); a ring counter with "a sprig of whatever the hall cut that day" (eating-out.md, The Ring Counters).
- **Night service (Sorrel):** last glide train 01:00, first 05:00, freight between (sorrel.md, Customs).

### The Glide
- Free. "A car let go at one touch point coasts in a straight line to the next and arrives at exactly the speed of the rim it meets" (raft-transport.md, How it works). 174.4 m/s, 24.5 km, 140 s (raft-transport.md, Numbers).
- **Leaving:** you board at the rim deck at 1 g; "Gravity switches off in about a second." **Arriving:** "the cradle takes the car on a compliant, regenerative arm and brings gravity back over a second or two" (raft-transport.md, The quay). "The catch is a young people's ride," says Hedda (ashgrove.md, Day three).
- **Cars:** "squat and heavily armoured"; ~13 t of sealed water a seat; "their windows face the one safe way, aft", ~1.3 m of clear water (ashgrove.md, Day three; raft-transport.md). Trains wait in vacuum berths; passengers board "through docking hatches" (element-cycles.md, The ledger).
- **The view:** "a hall nearly four kilometres deep, between the rectennas in front and the radiators behind, with the radiator layer running away like a ceiling over the world ... Every drum wears a ring of quay lights round its waist ... rank behind rank to a vanishing point" (ashgrove.md, Day three).
- Getting out to the quay: Sorrel's Minutes are "Sixty short dark tunnels through the hydrogen jacket" (sorrel.md, Outside the hull).

### Who works in the Keel, and when
- Teo Dace works the Gate by day, and the Gate has a night crew that goes off shift in the morning (ashgrove.md, Day one; Day three). Seconds is "the Keel's shift canteen, open at all hours" (sorrel.md, The Keel). The Reserve's crews "drill for one every month" (sorrel.md, The Keel). The lamp-night "belongs to the machines": batch work, fabrication and slow vat lines (daily-life.md, Rhythm). Shift hours are not given.

## 5. The Hollow

- **Size:** ~35 km² (35.3), 9.4 km round, 3.75 km long, at 0.5 g (raft-habitat.md; ashgrove.md, Method).
- **Arriving:** "You come up into the Hollow through the ground, out of a cab pavilion among trees, and the first thing everyone does is look up and keep looking. The land rises on both sides of you and keeps rising until it's overhead. The far bank hangs three kilometres above you, upside down, and ... it's perfectly sharp: you can see people on it, and a child over there flying a kite back at you" (ashgrove.md, Day two). Why: "filtered heliox carries no dust and scatters very little" (raft-habitat.md, Districts).
- **Land (Sorrel's plan, sorrel.md, The Hollow's plan):**
  - The lake: ~3 km², 2 m deep, ~6 Mt, Rings Four to Six; Bench Island rises through the lake bed with a jetty and the elders' bench. "Curving up at both ends until the far shore stands on its side" (sorrel.md, The Hollow). At half a g "the waves come in slow and tall, and a dive off the jetty takes half again as long to fall" (ashgrove.md, Day two). The lake "is kept starved and clear" (element-cycles.md, The inside ledger).
  - The orchards: nearly 5 km², apples mostly; winter tents at four degrees for six weeks in turn, "the only winter in Sorrel you can walk into"; pollinated "by hand, from a step-ladder, with a soft brush" (sorrel.md, The Hollow).
  - The lawns: nearly 7 km², with the kite field, where "kites are flown by walking" (sorrel.md, The Hollow).
  - The grounds: half-g track, twelve spinball pitches, the bandstand (sorrel.md, The Hollow's plan).
  - The Dim Ends: "the floor within ~750 m of each end wall, where the sunline's light falls away to ~38% at the wall's foot: woods, fern gardens and the Lamp Walk" (sorrel.md, The Hollow's plan).
- **Soil:** none. "Parks root in inert media on recycled nutrient solution" (element-cycles.md, The inside ledger).
- **Apples:** a Hollow apple costs 4.4 kWh, "the same ... as a lamp-grown one; the saving on light goes to the commons" (sorrel.md, The Hollow's plan).
- **Sharing it:** it holds ~1.4 M at a crowded beach's ~25 m² each, and is open 16 hours a day, giving everyone "about three-quarters of an hour a week" (economy.md, The Hollow and the high decks). Sorrel gives each deck "a six-hour turn about every eight weeks" and its elders the lake 06:00 to 09:00 (ashgrove.md, Day two; sorrel.md). Chervil "gives its Hollow hours by lottery and in festivals" (open-drums.md, How it differs); block courts settle "Hollow swaps", and a block that gives too many thirds can lose "weight in the Hollow draw" (open-drums.md). The Lattice fills the Hollow easily: ~780 cabs an hour per pavilion core for a full crowd (drum-transport.md, Running it).
- **Sound:** at Sorrel's bandstand "the band waits for its last chord to come back off the far bank" (sorrel.md, The Hollow). A shout down a bare 640 m arcade comes back "in two seconds" (sorrel.md, The Ringing Arcade).
- **Front and back (Sorrel):** the back wall is "the heat end and the busier wall"; the front wall "the quieter" (sorrel.md, The end walls). Sorrel proposes in the Back Wood and promenades on the Lamp Walk (sorrel.md, The Hollow). Chervil's dawn is watched from "the Hollow's back terraces" (open-drums.md, Booking Day in Chervil).
- **Festivals there (Chervil):** light-painted dawns and dusks, "three-hour sequences of colour on the sunline and the land, composed by lamp-painters and played once" (open-drums.md, Culture); the Long Flight above it. A dawn can make "the far bank of the Hollow, three kilometres overhead ... gold while the near side is still dark" (open-drums.md, Booking Day).
- **Chervil's Hollow layout** (where its lake, orchards and lawns lie): the docs are silent.

## 6. The end walls, the tiers and the Spindle

- **Tiers:** thirty, "lighter with every tier" (sorrel.md). Named tiers and uses:
  - The Third (a third of a g): leaping reels; "every leap comes down about a hand's width spinward" (ashgrove.md, Day three). Sorrel marries on the front wall's Third at dusk and dances the Long Reel on the back wall's, which "drifts round the wall a hand's width a leap, starting each year from a brass stud" (sorrel.md). Lovage's weddings and its restaurant Noon are on its back wall's Third (ashgrove.md; eating-out.md). Dulse pulls noodles there, since "a strand can hang three times as long" (wander-year.md).
  - The Quarter: "where Low-deck children learn to be light, starting with how to stop" (Sorrel front); the Slow Fountain, "Twelve jets ... that climb eight metres, hang for five seconds and come down three metres spinward" (Sorrel back) (sorrel.md, The end walls).
  - The Tenth: named only (sorrel.md).
  - The Twentieth: the top gardens, "where a child can jump the height of a house and comes down in somebody's beans" (Sorrel back); the Tea Steps, "lit mostly by the Hollow's own glow" (Sorrel front) (sorrel.md).
- **Terrace flats:** "The flats in their risers go to its very oldest" in Sorrel; the Sill's "youngest resident on this tier is a hundred and fourteen" (sorrel.md). In Chervil, terraces go "by draw" in the Margin lottery (open-drums.md). Chervil's elders' terrace exists: Oona watches the Long Flight "from the elders' terrace with a glass of gin" (open-drums.md, Booking Day).
- **Guests at dusk (Sorrel):** the Guest Tiers, the back wall's lower tiers, "where guests from other drums may sit at dusk without a turn" (sorrel.md).
- **The flying halls:** "within 75 m of the axis, from each end wall out to ~300 m, netted off ~150 m short of the line's end" (raft-habitat.md). Inside "a soft half-light around the axis, inside a long sock of netting" (ashgrove.md, Day two). Sorrel's back hall is "a netted sock of air 300 m long ... where Sorrel learns to fly lengthwise"; its front hall is "the quiet end: no axle roar, a cool spar, the old flying in the mornings and a crosswise club in the afternoons" (sorrel.md, The Spindle).
- **Wing-sleeves:** "fabric fins from wrist to hip, hired by the turn, with your shoes kept as the deposit" (sorrel.md); Marit's remade pair cost 18 kWh (wander-year.md).
- **Flying:** "Flying along the axis is easy. Flying across it is a lesson: Coriolis bends your path" (ashgrove.md, Day two); at 4 m/s you drift ~23 m in 10 s (wander-year.md, Method). Nothing boils at the axis; tea is brewed lower and "sent up in lidded bulbs" (eating-out.md, Axis Tea Room).
- **Wardens:** Sorrel's night flying "has wardens with red lamps at the Spindle's 75 m edge, since a flyer who drifts off the axis falls for minutes and lands at ~85 m/s" (sorrel.md, Open questions).
- **The Long Flight (Chervil):** "the sunline stays dark and the Spindle opens end to end. Hundreds of thousands fly the axis with hand lamps, in shifts till lamp-dawn" (open-drums.md, The calendar). "At the axis you weigh nothing, and the line you'd have to keep away from by day is a dark cable down the middle of the air. As far as the end wall, almost four kilometres off, small lights drift along the axis like a slow river. Somewhere in it a choir is singing in heliox, high and strange" (open-drums.md, Booking Day). People go "up with their block" (same). Sorrel's own flight is the Length, front gate to back wall at 22:00 "behind the oldest flyer's lamp" (sorrel.md, Customs).
- **The axles:** the front axle hall takes ~160 GW "and you would never know it by ear"; the back axle hall is "The loudest place in Sorrel", pipes "as wide as rooms" (sorrel.md, The axles; ashgrove.md, Day four).
- **Front against back:** power and quiet at the front; heat, noise, the busier wall, the despun chamber, the Windows and the back crews' dock at the back (sorrel.md; ashgrove.md). Back-wall terraces take the highest dose, ~2 to 10 mSv a year (habitat-design.md).

## 7. Outside the drum but reachable

- **The despun chamber:** "a chamber that turns with Sorrel, and slowly it stops turning, or rather the world around it does: for a little under two minutes Ossian watches the whole hall wheel round him once" (ashgrove.md, Day four).
- **The Window (Sorrel):** a dozen long low galleries behind the radiators, one per Hour, "each a long low gallery seating about a hundred and fifty", glazed with ~1.5 m of water; reached through the back axle and "a short armoured car" on a shielded track (ashgrove.md, Day four; sorrel.md). "Every child in Sorrel goes to the Window once, at about eight, with one adult of their choosing" (ashgrove.md). Visitors wait "twenty minutes in the red light"; an 80-minute visit gives ~12 µSv (ashgrove.md; raft-transport.md, Cyclers).
- **What is seen:** "There's no near star at all ... What fills the Window instead is what the back crews call the River: a broad band of stars right across the sky, more than anyone could count, pale gold, the brightest of them orange, thick and bright in the middle and frayed at the edges, with dark rifts of dust lying across it. Above and below it hangs a fainter glow the crews call the Shelf" (ashgrove.md, Day four). "Here and there ... places where nothing shines at all: black shapes ... 'Those are the holes,' Hedda says. 'Rafts.'" About a sixth of the sky is rafts (same). The dusty edges are brown-violet; the crews call that colour "bruise" (wander-year.md, Day 226).
- **Chervil and the Window:** Oona saw the River "once, at eight", and Marit "for the first time since I was eight" (open-drums.md; wander-year.md). Whether Chervil has its own galleries the docs are silent. Lovage "has no Windows" (eating-out.md).
- **Back crews:** they keep the radiator panels "in closed sleds behind the raft", under a ~1 Sv career cap, and "see the stars on ordinary working days" (sorrel.md; raft-transport.md). Nobody works there in a suit (raft-transport.md, Cyclers). Ochre lets a guest ride one shift (wander-year.md).
- **The raft (Merrow):** a 0.1 AU raft of ~6 × 10¹⁹ people in ~300 billion nation drums 24.5 km apart, mid-Shade, orbit ~36,000 years (ashgrove.md, Merrow and Herb Row). Layers: rectennas in front, radiators behind, 3.75 km of mid-raft between, "flooded with the Lantern's hard X-rays" (raft-habitat.md).
- **Slow lines and cyclers:** sleeper trains that never stop, boarded by capsule; the slow line at 2 km/s crosses a million km in ~6 days for ~1.1 kWh; it is weightless aboard (raft-transport.md; wander-year.md). Sorrel's Ring Five is the tube level (sorrel.md).
- **The Lantern:** the shell that makes the power, ~0.82 pc from the hole; "nobody lives on it" and "Nobody sees the Lantern" (glossary.md; raft-habitat.md). **The Shade:** the inhabited zone outside it, ~1.87 × 10³³ people (glossary.md). **The River:** the crews' name for the band of stars (ashgrove.md).
- **Herb Row** (ashgrove.md, Merrow and Herb Row; eating-out.md; open-drums.md):
  - **Sorrel:** steady pool drum; fixed goods by age; "gin is sold almost at cost"; plain dawns; one glide from Chervil (its Ring Two is "one glide from Chervil"). Sorrelers come over "for ramen at a long table" on Thursday evenings, and Ring Six commuters work in Chervil "where an hour pays twice" (sorrel.md).
  - **Lovage:** auctions its high decks and Hollow hours, a sunline at full for twelve hours, spirits taxed hard; "dazzling and a little vulgar" to Sorrel; home of the Crock, whose sourdough Chervil serves "and pretend[s] they make" (eating-out.md). Distance from Chervil: the docs are silent.
  - **Tansy:** steady drum "proud of its vat lines and its strawberries; keeps the longest strain library on the Row", and the Row's only henhouse (eating-out.md). Distance from Chervil: the docs are silent.
  - **Rue:** a counted drum after a blight, implants at fourteen, "its Count is posted on every platform"; "~14 years from par"; "two glides from Chervil" (ashgrove.md; eating-out.md; open-drums.md).
  - **Yarrow** (maker drum) and **Borage** (fair drum): "Further along Herb Row" (eating-out.md).
- **In an afternoon:** Sorrel is ~8½ minutes door to door and free (drum-transport.md); Rue is one glide further (eating-out.md). A Window visit is a full hour plus twenty minutes in the dark and the ride through the back axle (ashgrove.md); whether a guest could go is not stated. A cycler leg is days.

## 8. Chervil specifically

- **What it is:** an open drum and a hall drum, "Loud, crowded, fed by people" (open-drums.md, Kinds of open drum). Across the rows from Sorrel on Herb Row, Merrow, "with Rue two glides away" (open-drums.md, Chervil).
- **History:** "A holdings drum that counted ~340 years ago after a radiator fault, stopped births three or four years too long, and came out 3% under par. Its assembly voted to keep the gap and called it the Margin. Its old Count board hangs in the Ledger hall beside the Ledger" (open-drums.md, Chervil).
- **The Margin:** ~6 M unused shares and ~2.7 M places the drum holds. Each resident gets ~2.06 kWe instead of 2.0; the 55-day reserve stretches to ~57 (open-drums.md, A Margin). "It's a granary, not a field." Spending it on births takes a supermajority (same).
- **The Ledger** at each station ring shows "shares, people, the Margin, places, the year's births and deaths, guests that night, and the names: each newborn beside the dead whose share they carry, and each new householder beside the dead whose place they took." On Booking Day: 200,000,000 shares; 194,060,000 people; Margin 5,940,000; 90,000,000 places (2,673,000 held by the drum); 1,626,000 born; 1,617,000 died; ~410,000 guests (open-drums.md, Chervil). At Marit's homecoming, four months earlier: "194,052,000 people, 409,600 guests, and thirty-seven wanderers home" (wander-year.md, Day 365).
- **Guest-right:** "Any traveller from a drum that grants the same may stay three nights in a Margin flat and eat at the halls" (open-drums.md, Open both ways). A hall meal is "free on guest-right" (eating-out.md).
- **Money and time:** ~40% of wallets go on each other's time; an hour costs ~6 kWh; money incomes ~13,200 kWh a year (open-drums.md, An economy of each other). Chervil's halls, schools and blocks "hire their own people first" (same). A hall meal ~2.7 kWh (open-drums.md, What joy costs). Rents run "about half" a steady drum's (economy.md, Homes).
- **Children:** no posted price for a child; two by right, a third by the block's leave, granted "out loud" by the block court; "about one birth in eighteen, which for a block of ~3,000 is a third child a year or so" (open-drums.md, Children without counting). The third-child line "has crept from one family in nine to one in seven since the Count" (open-drums.md, How it differs). Two names: the dead's family "brings a gift to the naming. Every birth closes someone's funeral" (open-drums.md).
- **Marriage:** couples marry young, "mostly at about twenty-five", and borrow a Margin flat for five years or until one inherits (open-drums.md, Children without counting).
- **Tables and aunts:** "A child has a table: parents plus three or four people who stood at the naming, mostly adults whose own children are grown and the placeless, called aunt whatever their sex" (open-drums.md).
- **Block courts:** a block of ~3,000 "meets once a week in its atrium to settle noise, heat, rooms and Hollow swaps, out loud and in minutes" (open-drums.md, Voice at three scales). Sanctions: "the block's weight in the Hollow draw, its couples' turn in the flat lottery, its place on the Long Flight" (open-drums.md, Children without counting).
- **The assembly by lot:** ~1,000 residents drawn each year to set the commons (open-drums.md). One question goes to every resident on Booking Day (same).
- **Cooked halls:** "one long table a block", "a paid cook and volunteers. Eating alone is possible and unusual" (open-drums.md).
- **Gin:** "taxed lightly, with thiamine, and sold only in the halls after six, where someone is always cooking. Chervil's methanol deaths are among the lowest on Herb Row" (open-drums.md, Chervil). 0.18 kWh a glass (eating-out.md).
- **Light-painting, visible mending, the long letter, heliox choirs, low-g dance:** open-drum culture, all in open-drums.md, Culture.
- **What Herb Row says:** "Sorrel thinks Chervil is lovely and a little pleased with itself. Lovage thinks it dim and charming. Rue's councillors come to the Ledger every Booking Day and take notes" (open-drums.md, Chervil). "'Chervil manners' is Herb Row for a certain kind of pity aimed at Rue" (open-drums.md, What open drums struggle with).
- **What it argues about:** spending the Margin; whether to post a price for a third or draw leave instead; every few Booking Days a motion to do so "loses" (open-drums.md, How it differs).
- **Named places:** Station Ring 2; the Ledger hall; Marit Vell's Hall, "the Ring 2 hall", "near Station Ring 2", four hundred seats at one long table (eating-out.md; wander-year.md); the Dawn Stand, a coffee cart on the back terraces "set up wherever the morning's painted dawn looks best" (eating-out.md); the ring counter; the elders' terrace; the Hollow's back terraces (open-drums.md).
- **Named people:** Marit Vell (26), hall cook, holds her great-grandfather's place; Jory, her partner; Oona (81), aunt and lamp-painter (open-drums.md). Her hall has "eleven volunteers, nine of them over seventy" (open-drums.md).
- **Prices:** Thursday ramen 1.77; "whatever the aunts made" ~0.5; gin 0.18; Dawn Stand coffee 0.15 and honeycomb steamed cake 0.30; ring counter free (0.30 to make) (eating-out.md).
- **The Booking Day scene** (open-drums.md, Booking Day in Chervil), key lines:
  - 06:00: Marit "takes the Spindle lift up to the Hollow's back terraces"; the dawn "starts not white but the brown-violet of the River in a Window ... It warms through rose, and for twenty minutes the far bank of the Hollow, three kilometres overhead, is gold while the near side is still dark ... The terraces clap. Oona says it was too slow in the middle."
  - 08:30: "The Ledger at Station Ring 2. The Margin line reads 5,940,000, down 29,000 on the year, 20,000 of it refuge, and people cheer it anyway." Rue's councillors stand at the back; "Somebody brings them tea."
  - 10:00: "Merrow's beam for the year, flat, as it nearly always is." The question: "an extra hour of full sun in the Hollow, or a larger hall fund."
  - 13:00: "cultivated steak with herb butter, a salad cut generously for once, aged cheese, the Lovage sourdough everyone pretends Chervil makes, and an orchard apple cut crosswise for every two people, so everyone sees the star."
  - 17:00: "hall fund, by four points."
  - 22:00: the Long Flight. 02:00: Oona with gin on the elders' terrace. "She's eighty-one. In Chervil, that's a plan."

## 10. Customs, games, festivals and daily rhythms

### The day
- 06:30 lamps ramp over twenty minutes; light panel; 07:00 breakfast under lids; 07:20 to work; school break at 10:30 is spinball; lunch at the Mill table; 15:30 after school, rehearsals, baths; 18:30 dinner; 20:00 shared worlds, tutors, reading, practice; 23:00 lamps fade; laundry set for 01:00 "when kWh are cheapest" (ashgrove.md, Day one).
- "The lamp-night belongs to the machines" (daily-life.md, Rhythm). kWh cost "most at lamp-noon and least in the lamp-night" (economy.md, Money).
- ~45% of residents glide out and back each day (drum-transport.md). "The staggered day": a drum's working hours "are part of its character, and of its rents" (drum-transport.md, Culture).
- Named weekdays exist: Thursday ramen in Chervil (eating-out.md); Saturday at the Thiamine; second Tuesday at the Larder (eating-out.md); Monday at the Hundredth Breakfast; Thursday tea dance (sorrel.md).

### Physics games
- **Spinball:** "Every child in Sorrel learns its rule before they can read: *axis flies true, spin sinks, anti floats.* A ball thrown along the axis goes straight. Thrown spinward ... it gains about a sixth of its weight and dives; thrown antispinward it loses as much and sails. Every throw breaks according to its heading, so the game is all angles" (ashgrove.md, Day one). Ossian "is good at it, because he's small and throws anti" (same). The docs give no pitch size, team size or scoring.
- **Children race antispinward and lose fights spinward**; "A dropped cup lands about two centimetres antispinward" and children say "anti" (ashgrove.md).
- **Reels at a third of a g:** "Dancing at a third of a g is mostly leaping, and every leap comes down about a hand's width spinward of where it began, so Lovage's young people have a reel that works its way round the terrace a hand's width at a time" (ashgrove.md, Day three).
- **Flying:** halls by day, the whole Spindle by night; lengthwise is easy, crosswise taught (ashgrove.md; wander-year.md).
- **Kites** flown by walking; **the Round**, the Heavy and Light Round on deck 201 (Sorrel); stair races (Hare) (sorrel.md).
- **Low-g cooking:** soufflés rise taller on third-g terraces; tea poured inward "bends spinward" (eating-out.md).

### Festivals (Chervil's calendar, open-drums.md, The calendar)
- **The Quiet:** "the whole drum goes dark: no worlds, no messages. People sit in the halls and talk". Saves ~9.6 kWh a head.
- **Booking Day:** "The beam is booked; the Ledger is read; the assembly gives its split; one question is voted on. The Quiet's savings become a feast in every hall." The feast is "grown and brewed in the weeks before". Booking Day is "the nearest thing to a harvest festival" (economy.md, Culture).
- **The Long Flight:** that night.
- **Knotnight:** "The layer era's long dark in one evening: lamps down, the larder made together". In Ashgrove "The Court's lamps go down, and the neighbourhood makes the year's larder together" (ashgrove.md).
- **Homecoming:** "Wanderers back from their year are met at the station ring by their block and their table". Marit's block met her "with hand lamps at lamp-dawn" (wander-year.md).
- **Namings:** "A child's table stands up for them; the dead's family brings a gift", usually "a remade heirloom".
- **Weddings and the apple:** an orchard apple cut crosswise shows "the five-pointed star of seeds"; "An apple is never for one" (ashgrove.md; eating-out.md). Jory's apple on the platform "is a wedding thing" (wander-year.md). Marit means to marry "after Knotnight" (open-drums.md).
- Sorrel-only: Summer Day (cold food), the Length, the Round, the refuge cake (sorrel.md). Steady drums: Draw Day (families.md, What it feels like). Raft-wide: the Passing (wander-year.md).

### Other customs
- **Going dark:** "a black thread round the left wrist, meaning *don't message me, I won't see it*" (ashgrove.md, Day one).
- **The wander year:** at about twenty; twelve legs ~13 kWh; "one card home from every row, sent by light, postmarked with the delay" (wander-year.md).
- **Lids:** "Sorrelers think an uncovered dish says something about your upbringing" (ashgrove.md). "Lift the lid when it reaches you" (eating-out.md).
- **Host gifts:** "Bring herbs, not gin" (eating-out.md). A Hollow hour "is the classic gift" (economy.md).
- **"Have some Nine"** to anyone peaky (ashgrove.md). "Kays" for kWh (ashgrove.md).
- **Clothes:** children's seams carry "a hand's width folded into every seam"; "fashion in Sorrel lives in linings, collars, cuffs and alterations" (ashgrove.md).

## 11. Hard limits

- **No animals except forty hens in Tansy.** "Animals are a rare luxury (one henhouse on the Row)" (eating-out.md). Orchards are hand-pollinated (sorrel.md). Sorrel's Hours are "named for animals nobody in Sorrel has seen" (sorrel.md). The brief rules out all others.
- **No natural light, sky, weather or season.** "No natural light anywhere ... Every garden runs on lamps" (food-system.md, Tier 3). "No night, no season" (daily-life.md, Rhythm). The only "winters" are chilled tents (sorrel.md). A theatre programme "explains what rain was" (sorrel.md). Sorrel's one season was a fault (sorrel.md, History).
- **No stars from inside.** "There are no stars; the raft's layers hide the sky. Only crews on the back of the raft see stars ... Nobody sees the Lantern" (raft-habitat.md). Children see them once at a Window (Sorrel); Lovage's people mostly never (eating-out.md).
- **Fire and smoke.** Nothing can burn in the helium tubes; fire dumps go "to a tank inside the cold"; nothing vents (drum-transport.md; element-cycles.md). The docs cook with electric power and the 65 °C loop ("searing and frying draw power"). They do not state an outright ban on candles; the game brief does.
- **No soil.** "No soil" (element-cycles.md, Rules); parks root in inert media.
- **Money is kWh only.** "One currency, no exchange rates" (economy.md). Kays.
- **New mass is dear:** ~25 kWh a kilogram; a chair 125, a sofa 1,000 (daily-life.md). So things are old, mended and inherited.
- **What cannot be made cheaply or at all:** "juicy crunch", "bone-in, skin-on or marbled whole cuts, and whole fish", "natural kernels (brown rice, beans, nuts, popcorn)", "shell eggs with a separate yolk" (food-system.md, What the kitchens can make). Grains, pulses and nuts "are reserved for ritual" (food-system.md, Tier 3).
- **Robots are appliances in the docs:** cleaning robots, robots that cook at the Mill Hall, maintenance (ashgrove.md; eating-out.md; daily-life.md). Minds on the raft's compute "may be persons" and are deliberately left undescribed (governance.md, Minds, machines and the settlement). Assistants and tutors exist ("Ossian does half an hour with his tutor, who is mostly patient") (ashgrove.md).
- **No homes in the Keel or on line decks** (raft-habitat.md; drum-transport.md). **No vehicles on the Hollow floor** (drum-transport.md).
- **No flying in the Spindle's middle by day:** within 75 m of the lit line is burning light (raft-habitat.md).
- **Glide cars stay on the rim;** nobody changes vehicles inside a drum (drum-transport.md).
- **Fixed prices** (kWh): herbs 0.03; coffee 0.15; gin 0.18; cheese slice 0.33; beer 0.38; tomato 0.50; side salad 0.59; mycelium steak 0.84; noodles 0.96; ramen 1.77; Vat-table dinner 2.48; cultivated steak 2.91; orchard apple 4.4; bottle of gin 2.8; a shirt 5; shoes 20; chair 125; sofa 1,000; an hour ~3 in Sorrel, ~6 in Chervil; wallet 21.6 a day; personal compute 9.6 a day (eating-out.md; daily-life.md).
- **Numbers not to contradict:** R 3,000 m, L 3,750 m, 110 s a turn; 376 decks at 4 m; Keel 1 to 15, Low 16 to 113, Middle 114 to 301, High 302 to 375; Hollow floor 0.5 g, ~35 km², far bank 3 km; sunline stops ~450 m short; Spindle edge 75 m, under 3% g; thirty tiers; cores every ~100 m; cabs 20 m/s radial, 30 m/s on tubes; glide 140 s; Chervil's Ledger figures (sections 1, 3, 8).

## 12. Open questions and gaps

1. **Chervil's addresses.** Sorrel's Rings and Hours are Sorrel's; the docs are silent on how Chervil names its wedges, blocks and decks.
2. **Where Chervil's nine station rings lead.** Sorrel's even rings train to Chervil and Rue, but no doc says which of Chervil's rings go where, which is its tube ring, or what each ring's character is.
3. **Station halls.** The Ledger stands "at every station ring", but the docs describe one Ledger hall (with the Count board) and do not say whether each ring has its own hall, guest desk or ring counter, or where Chervil's guests sign in.
4. **Chervil's Hollow plan.** Where its lake, orchards, lawns, woods and grounds lie, how big they are, and whether it has a Bench Island, robe house or bandstand: silent.
5. **Chervil's Hollow rota.** "By lottery and in festivals" is all; turn length, frequency and who has the lake at dawn are not given.
6. **The front wall in Chervil.** The docs name only the back terraces, the Dawn Stand and the elders' terrace. What Chervil keeps on its front wall, and on the Tenth anywhere, is unsaid.
7. **Tier dimensions.** Thirty tiers, but no tier heights, depths, stairs between tiers or how people move up the wall other than the Spindle lifts.
8. **The middle decks' look.** Beyond Sorrel's landmarks and the general "indoor city of streets and arcades", nothing describes a typical Middle or High deck street, a High deck elders' corridor in Chervil, or how blocks differ by band.
9. **Block contents.** No count of schools, clinics, baths, halls or shops per block or per head. Chervil has "one long table a block" and a choir; the rest is open.
10. **Terraces, the Hollow's edge and the dim decks.** Named as lottery prizes in Chervil but not defined.
11. **The Keel by Ring in Chervil.** Where Chervil's food-plant halls, still, Gate, strain library, cellars, Down Halls (or their equivalent) and tide tanks are: silent. Whether Chervil runs a tide at all is unstated.
12. **Funerals in Chervil.** The docs give Sorrel's Down Halls and the general "dead come home"; Chervil's own rite is not described beyond names read on the Ledger.
13. **Windows for Chervil.** Its children see the River at eight, but whether Chervil keeps its own galleries, how many, and whether a guest can visit, is not said.
14. **Distances to Lovage and Tansy from Chervil.** Only Sorrel (one glide) and Rue (two glides) are given.
15. **The Spindle at night.** Wardens and nets are an open question even for Sorrel; how the Long Flight is organised in shifts, entered and stewarded in Chervil is not described.
16. **Spinball rules.** Only the throwing rule is canon; teams, pitch, scoring and equipment are not.
17. **The Quiet's limits.** Worlds and messages go dark; whether cabs, the Glide, lamps or shops change is not said.
18. **Assembly and courts in Chervil.** The assembly by lot (~1,000) and block courts are given; where the assembly sits and whether Chervil has a court above the block are silent.
19. **Inns and guest flats.** How a Margin flat is furnished, where guest flats cluster, and whether Chervil has inns at all are unstated.
20. **Line-deck life.** Depots, workshops and freight sorting are named; what a line deck looks like to someone walking it is not.
21. **Bicycles and trolleys.** Cycling is permitted and slow trolleys "may" exist; no doc shows a bicycle or says which decks keep trolleys.
22. **Tier names beyond four.** Only the Third, Quarter, Tenth and Twentieth are named of thirty.
