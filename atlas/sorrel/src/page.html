The Sorrel Atlas

One spinning drum, 3 km in radius and 3.75 km long, holding two hundred million people on 376 decks. It has about 70,000 neighbourhoods. Visit one a day and you would finish in 190 years. Every point on this drawing is somewhere: tap it.

    Half-section. The axis is at the top, the rim at the bottom. Drag to move · scroll or pinch to zoom · tap anywhere to open it · arrow keys and +/− work too

    The Climb

    Every landmark in the atlas, in the order you would meet them going in: from the quay outside the hull, up through the Keel and 360 decks of streets, to the Hollow, the Spindle and the axle. Choose one to find it on the drawing.

    About the atlas

    Sorrel is the nation drum of the Kerr swarm world bible's narrative sketches: the Dace family lives in its Ashgrove neighbourhood, and the Crosswise Guide reviews its kitchens. The drum's numbers (radius, decks, gravity, cab speeds, light, prices) come from the world bible's design docs.

    The landmarks were written for this atlas to fit those docs. The neighbourhoods behind every other door are generated from Sorrel's address, so the same door always opens on the same place, but they are sketches, not canon.

    How to read the drawing

    It is a cut through the drum along its axis, at one Hour. The front cap, where power comes in, is on the left; the back cap, where heat leaves for the radiators and the Windows, is on the right. Weight falls from 1 g at the rim to half a g on the Hollow's floor and nearly nothing at the axis.

    Sorrelers give an address as deck, Ring and Hour: nine Rings along the axis, named for the station rings in the Keel, and twelve Hours around it, named for animals nobody in Sorrel has seen.
